# Cross-clade flower-colour atlas — evidence-tier architecture v0.5

## Decision

The programme will stop searching for a single second clade that simultaneously satisfies all of the following:

1. a robust white-like ancestral state;
2. >=3 robust independent historical colour transitions;
3. >=3 matched molecular contrasts spanning those events;
4. current polymorphism;
5. ecological causal evidence.

The current search shows that this complete package is rare. Forcing every clade to pass all five layers would either discard strong evidence or tempt post-hoc relaxation of independence rules.

Instead, the atlas uses **orthogonal evidence tiers** and tests whether the same general architecture recurs across clades.

## Tier A — macroevolutionary transition architecture

Question: how do visible/pigment states move through evolutionary time?

Primary systems:
- *Camellia* — white-like external root context; local colour structure; event identity weak;
- Linoideae — yellow-white deep baseline and dated colour diversification;
- *Hydrangea* sect. Cornidia — white ancestral but strong returns to white; falsifies one-way gain;
- Angraecinae — white -> green repeated low-complexity control;
- *Polygonatum* sect. Verticillata — provisional white-baseline diversification candidate;
- Antirrhineae — transition-rate/polymorphism benchmark;
- *Silene* Physolychnis — repeated red gains, including some from white;
- *Linanthus* — recent phylogenomics indicates colour polymorphism itself is likely ancestral and labile, superseding older preliminary white-ancestor framing.

No published ancestral-state conclusion is copied as the atlas estimator. Terminal trait rows, organ identity, polymorphism and provenance must be rebuilt/admitted under the common protocol.

## Tier B — mechanistic repeatability under common observation

Question: when similar endpoints recur, which molecular modules repeat?

Primary systems:
- *Camellia* — candidate-free A/F/C/P same-system remeasurement; primary matched observation-intervention case;
- *Epimedium* — one common 12-gene candidate panel + HPLC across eight species; core ANS recurrence with DFR/CHS/FLS heterogeneity; historical event identity held unresolved;
- Cape *Erica* — 12 putatively independent colour-shift sets measured with a transferred RT-qPCR anthocyanin panel and UPLC-MS/MS; white/yellow endpoints arise through multiple different regulatory, coding and flux mechanisms;
- Aquilegia — repeated anthocyanin losses associated with convergent late-pathway downregulation;
- Iochrominae — four independent anthocyanin losses with strong pathway-level expression convergence;
- *Ipomoea* — three robust independent red origins repeatedly implicating floral F3'H regulation.

These are not all the same observation regime. Analyses must therefore distinguish:
- candidate-free transcript-state measurement;
- common candidate-panel measurement;
- event-specific functional genetics;
- metabolite/flux evidence.

The atlas may compare hierarchical patterns across regimes but must not pretend they are numerically identical measurements.

## Tier C — regain / latent-capacity tests

Question: can apparently unpigmented states retain enough machinery for later colour reacquisition?

### Petunia

The long-tube lineage provides two independent reacquisitions from a colourless ancestor:
- *P. secreta* restores purple through a 2-bp compensatory deletion that restores the AN2 reading frame;
- *P. exserta* retains nonfunctional AN2 but recruits the paralog DPL and combines this with hydroxylation and acylation reprogramming to produce red.

This is an unusually strong demonstration that `unpigmented != molecular zero` and that regain can be simple in one lineage and multigenic in another.

### Acer / Dalechampia prior-art boundary

Armbruster (2002) already showed that flower-colour diversification can depend on genetic starting context. In *Acer*, red/purple flowers were more likely to evolve in lineages already deploying anthocyanins in vegetative tissues; in *Dalechampia*, floral pigment evolution was linked to vegetative pigment context/pleiotropy.

Therefore the atlas must **not** claim novelty for the general latent-capacity/genetic-context hypothesis. Its contribution would be to connect that idea to standardized molecular-module reuse, event architecture, and persistence across multiple clades.

## Tier D — ecological filtering / realization

Question: which generated states receive enough reproductive advantage to persist?

Strong controls:
- *Euonymus* — red state repeatedly associated with fungus-gnat pollination, short stamens and acetoin; root colour itself remains uncertain;
- *Camellia* — direct annual-climate/coarse-hue models weak; pollinator-service chain better supported but branch cause unresolved;
- *Petunia* — colour, scent and morphology are tightly tied to bee/hawkmoth/hummingbird syndromes with functional genetics;
- Aquilegia — independent anthocyanin losses align with hawkmoth transitions; experimental evidence favors pale flowers under hawkmoth pollination;
- *Silene* — red-flower/hummingbird transitions and within-species polymorphism provide bridge cases;
- *Linanthus parryae* — reciprocal transplant and long-term fitness data show local selection on blue/white morphs, providing a spatial-maintenance control rather than a white-root macro anchor.

## Tier E — reticulation / event-identifiability stress tests

Question: when does a clean transition narrative fail because the lineage history is reticulate or poorly resolved?

- *Epimedium* — nuclear/plastid/topology conflicts and introgression prevent promotion of A− endpoints to a fixed number of historical losses;
- *Nicotiana* — hybridization and polyploidy strongly alter floral spectral evolution;
- *Camellia* — accepted-species event identity is not robust despite aggregate phylogenetic colour structure.

These are not nuisance cases. They test whether the programme's event-identifiability gate is necessary outside Camellia.

## What the search has already falsified

### F1 — `white ancestor -> one-way gain of colour`
Rejected as a general formulation. *Hydrangea* sect. Cornidia has a white ancestral state yet the published transition map is dominated by returns to white.

### F2 — `one derived visible endpoint -> one recurrent molecular programme`
Rejected as a general formulation. Camellia and Epimedium both show broad module reuse with implementation heterogeneity; Cape Erica demonstrates multiple distinct genetic routes to white/yellow endpoints.

### F3 — `the best next clade must contain the whole causal chain`
Rejected as an unnecessary design constraint. The evidence is naturally modular across clades; a tiered atlas can test common rules without weakening within-clade independence criteria.

## Retained cross-clade hypotheses

### H1 — baseline-dependent transition architecture
Ancestral pigment/display state changes the rates and permitted routes among derived states; no universal direction is assumed.

### H2 — retained-capacity / regulatory redeployment
Unpigmented or pale states often retain functional pigment-network capacity, making later regain possible without de-novo invention of the entire pathway.

### H3 — hierarchical repeatability
Across independent transitions, repeatability is expected to be stronger at some molecular modules than at the complete multivariate implementation.

### H4 — observation-regime dependence
The apparent amount of recurrence depends on which molecular coordinates are measured. Common-panel and candidate-free systems should be analyzed separately before cross-regime synthesis.

### H5 — accessibility-realization gap
The set of molecularly feasible states exceeds the set of states that persist as robust lineage-level macroevolutionary patterns.

### H6 — ecological filtering conditional on latent phenotype
Persistence should relate more strongly to flowering-window sensory/reward/pollinator function than to coarse human-visible hue alone, while pleiotropic/genetic-context alternatives remain explicit competitors.

## Current gap

No second non-Camellia clade yet passes the complete combination of:

`white-like ancestor + >=3 robust independent events + >=3 matched molecular event contrasts`.

This is now recorded as an empirical data-availability gap rather than solved by weakening the gate.

The programme therefore proceeds with the tiered atlas while continuing finite searches for a complete event-matched anchor.
