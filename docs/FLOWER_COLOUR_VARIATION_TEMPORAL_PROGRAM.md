# Flower-colour variation through evolutionary time

## Program-level position

`chun` is the **evolutionary-time arm** of a broader research programme on the spatiotemporal organization of flower-colour variation.

The umbrella question is:

> **How is flower-colour variation generated, retained, and organized across evolutionary time and geographic space?**

The current programme has three analytically separate but conceptually linked components:

1. **Camellia Paper 1 — generation:** when similar visible flower-colour states evolve repeatedly, how much of the underlying pigment-network transition is replayed?
2. **Cross-radiation Evolution Letters programme — temporal retention:** how long does flower-colour identity retain lineage history as lineages diverge, and at what phenotypic resolution is that history expressed?
3. **FCP — geographic space:** once within-species colour variation exists, how is it organized geographically — as local coexistence, local continuous structure, or differentiation among populations?

These are not pooled datasets and they do not estimate one common parameter.

The shared decomposition is:

`generation -> temporal retention / loss -> contemporary spatial organization`

## Temporal arm I — mechanistic generation in Camellia

Camellia Paper 1 asks whether repeated visible states replay one invariant molecular programme.

The current answer is bounded:

- repeated visible colour change does not imply replay of one complete A/F/C/P pigment-state package;
- mechanistic recurrence is modular and transition-class dependent;
- accepted wild colour remains locally phylogenetically structured;
- individual accepted-species transition events are not robust enough for branch-specific ecological causation.

Its main contribution to the wider programme is to separate **mechanistic accessibility** from **long-term realization**.

A visible state can be repeatedly accessible without being generated through one invariant molecular route.

## Temporal arm II — evolutionary memory across radiations

The cross-radiation programme asks a different temporal question:

> **As evolutionary divergence accumulates, how much information about flower-colour identity remains, and at what phenotypic depth can that history still be read?**

### Standardized visible-colour breadth

The Flower-clades-51 frame provides the broad comparative backbone.

- 51 predefined source clades entered the common source frame.
- 32 were eligible for direct fine-colour temporal-persistence analysis.
- 28 completed the standardized three-resolution profile.
- 21 had genuine fine-to-coarse opportunity for the hidden-memory test.
- clades failing common-frame or state-variation gates were retained as HOLD rather than rescued post hoc.

This is broad comparative coverage under one ontology and pipeline, but it is not a random or exhaustive census of angiosperms.

### Temporal decay

Across the 32 eligible clades:

- 23/32 had negative excess-retention slopes;
- median slope = -0.1897;
- 23/32 had positive integrated excess-retention area.

The supported biological interpretation is:

> **Exact visible flower-colour identity generally retains more lineage-specific similarity among shallower relatives and loses that excess similarity as relative phylogenetic divergence deepens.**

The axis is relative divergence depth, not calibrated absolute time.

### No universal privileged phenotype scale

The preregistered Iris prediction of a universal intermediate-resolution optimum failed.

Across the 28 standardized completed clades, coarse, fine, tied and no-signal profiles coexist, with zero unique intermediate winners.

Simple pre-outcome tree-geometry predictors did not explain that heterogeneity.

Therefore the temporal programme does not support one universal phenotypic resolution at which flower-colour history is best retained.

### Hidden lineage history

Among the 21 standardized radiations with genuine fine-to-coarse opportunity:

- 18/21 hidden-memory effects are positive;
- median centered effect = +0.0270 AUC;
- one-sided Wilcoxon P = 3.34e-5;
- sign-test P = 7.45e-4.

The structural prediction that more fine-to-coarse collision should mechanically create stronger hidden memory was not supported:

- rho = -0.2571;
- permutation P = 0.26023;
- all 21 leave-one-clade-out correlations are negative.

Thus coarse-graining can **conceal** finer lineage history without the effect being explained simply by how many fine states are collapsed.

### Prospective and cross-representation tests

The independent prospective visible-colour Schistanthe test passed:

- 129 retained tips;
- centered effect = +0.06416 AUC;
- P = 0.0033.

Biochemical generalization is mixed:

- Petunieae retrospective: +0.18794 AUC, P = 0.0001;
- Gesnerioideae prospective: -0.00779 AUC, P = 0.5005, frozen FAIL;
- Ruellia remains outcome-unopened because the authoritative author-used tree is unavailable.

External source-specific tests in Linoideae, Angraecinae and Antirrhineae provide three independent qualitative concordances for finer organization conditional on a coarser state, but their statistics are deliberately not pooled with the standardized AUC analyses.

The correct generalization is therefore **recurrent, not universal**.

## Relation to FCP — the spatial sister arm

The FCP project asks:

> **Once within-species flower-colour variation exists, how is it organized across geographic space?**

Its held-out continuous-colour analysis supports local within-species spatial organization: neighbouring observations are more colour-similar than expected under species-conditioned random labelling.

However, the stronger cross-species test does not confirm one shared global geography of the strongest colour transitions.

This produces a striking temporal–spatial symmetry.

| Research axis | CHUN — evolutionary time | FCP — geographic space |
|---|---|---|
| Coordinate | relative phylogenetic divergence | geographic distance / neighbourhood |
| Local structure | shallower relatives retain excess colour similarity | nearby observations retain excess colour similarity |
| Heterogeneity | memory strength and carrying resolution vary among radiations | organization and transition geography vary among species |
| Universal-template test | no universal privileged phenotype resolution | no confirmed universal global transition boundary |
| Nested/local information | fine lineage history can persist within coarse phenotype classes | within-species colour can be locally structured without one shared global map |
| Current causal gap | why memory decays and hidden history varies | why variation is locally maintained or geographically sorted |

The shared programme-level empirical architecture is:

> **Flower-colour variation is recurrently structured at local or lineage-conditioned scales, but that structure is not governed by one universal global template across either evolutionary time or geographic space.**

This is a conceptual synthesis, not a pooled statistical result.

## What is genuinely general

The strongest current general principle is not a universal mechanism, colour category, scale or map.

It is:

> **Phenotypic organization can be recurrent without being universally templated: evolutionary history and geographic structure are retained locally, while the scale or location at which that structure is expressed varies among lineages and species.**

A second, specifically temporal principle is:

> **Ecological or phenotypic coarse-graining need not erase evolutionary history; finer lineage-specific history can remain hidden inside a broader phenotype class.**

The latter is strongly recurrent in visible flower colour but is not established as universal across biochemical or other phenotype representations.

## How comprehensive is the test?

### Broadly triangulated

The temporal programme has directly tested:

1. a prospective universal-resolution prediction;
2. standardized cross-radiation resolution profiles;
3. temporal decay of exact colour similarity;
4. hidden fine-state history conditional on coarse state;
5. a structural/compression-artifact explanation;
6. equivalence with global signal;
7. equivalence with global fine-resolution persistence;
8. independent prospective visible-colour replication;
9. cross-representation biochemical transport;
10. external nonexchangeable nested-state radiations;
11. explicit negative, FAIL and HOLD outcomes.

This is close to comprehensive with respect to the **main inferential alternatives and failure modes identified by the programme**.

### Not biologically exhaustive

It does not yet provide:

- a probability sample of flowering-plant radiations;
- exhaustive taxonomic coverage;
- exhaustive colour representation across spectra, pigments, regulatory states and developmental modules;
- calibrated absolute-time decay constants comparable among clades;
- a confirmed ecological cause of memory decay;
- a confirmed developmental or molecular cause of hidden-memory realization;
- a direct harmonized test linking macroevolutionary memory to contemporary spatial sorting.

Accordingly, use **recurrent**, **cross-radiation**, **prospectively replicated in visible colour**, or **broadly triangulated**.

Do not use **universal across angiosperms**, **exhaustive**, or **general law across representations**.

## The clean future synthesis

The next genuinely new synthesis is not another retrospective moderator search on the same clades.

It is a harmonized prospective test asking whether one predeclared lineage property predicts both:

1. the rate at which flower-colour memory is lost across evolutionary divergence; and
2. the strength of contemporary geographic turnover or spatial sorting within species.

That would directly connect the temporal and spatial sister papers.

Until those harmonized data exist, CHUN and FCP should remain analytically separate.

## Claim boundaries

This programme framing does not license claims that:

- the same ecological mechanism causes temporal and spatial structure;
- temporal memory predicts FCP spatial sorting in the current datasets;
- the Flower-clades-51 frame is exhaustive of angiosperms;
- hidden memory is universal across phenotype representations;
- global no-signal means absence of all nested history;
- one universal phenotype resolution is biologically correct;
- FCP and CHUN estimate the same quantity.
