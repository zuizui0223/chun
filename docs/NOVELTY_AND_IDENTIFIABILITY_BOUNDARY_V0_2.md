# Novelty and identifiability boundary v0.2

## Supersession

This document supersedes `docs/NOVELTY_AND_IDENTIFIABILITY_BOUNDARY_V0_1.md` for Paper 1 novelty framing. The v0.1 distinction among feasibility, literature ascertainment, macroevolutionary realization and persistence remains valid, but the 2026-08-27 literature audit imposes a stricter prior-art boundary.

## What is established prior art

The following are **not** Paper 1 novelty claims:

1. repeated phenotype can arise from different genes or genetic architectures;
2. repeated flower colour can use different pigment pathways;
3. convergence can occur at a pathway/module level rather than at the exact-gene level;
4. candidate-focused discovery can yield higher estimates of genetic reuse than agnostic designs;
5. ancestral-state and transition-event histories depend on model assumptions, trait coding and phylogenetic uncertainty;
6. Camellia flower-colour diversification has already been linked to genus-scale phylogenomics, structural variation, transposable elements and functional regulatory mechanisms;
7. pollination and temperature can influence floral colour and reproductive performance.

Representative prior-art decisions are frozen in `data/paper1_novelty_prior_art_registry_v0_1.csv`.

## Quantities Paper 1 actually identifies

### 1. Literature-conditioned multivariate recurrence

Published Camellia systems are represented as partially observed A/F/C/P direction signatures. Missing axes are not filled from visible hue. Instead, all admissible completions are enumerated, yielding an identified set for exact-signature recurrence and pairwise concordance under the literature observation regime.

This is not the natural probability of a mechanism and not a population estimate of mutational accessibility.

### 2. Candidate-free same-system recurrence

Five public biological systems are remeasured with one frozen annotation-driven A/F/C/P protocol. The same RefSeq background, annotation-matched paralogs, module-completeness rule and no expected-direction/significance filter are applied to all systems.

This reduces one observation process — within-system candidate selection — while leaving between-system publication/availability bias intact.

### 3. Robust macroevolutionary colour pattern

Accepted-species wild-colour states are tested on two independently inferred nuclear topologies and under strict versus dominant trait coding. The nearest same-colour relative remains closer than expected under count-preserving random placement across the frozen scenarios.

This is an unrooted phylogenetic pattern. It is not a branch transition rate or ecological cause.

### 4. Event-identification gate

Individual accepted-species colour-transition branches are allowed into branch-specific interpretation only if they survive the predeclared wild-colour coding sensitivity. The strict × dominant shared strong-event set is empty.

This does not mean colour evolution did not occur. It means the present public hard-state data do not identify a stable individual event set at the required resolution.

## Strongest empirical novelty

The contribution is the **matched inferential audit** across these quantities.

Two empirical contractions occur:

1. **molecular:** the same public biological systems support stronger complete recurrence under literature-selected observation than under standardized candidate-free remeasurement;
2. **macro:** a robust phylogenetic colour pattern survives topology/coding sensitivity even though individual accepted-species transition events do not survive the event-identity gate.

The resulting defensible statement is:

> **Mechanistic recurrence, macroevolutionary pattern and historical event identity are differently robust to the observation and coding processes used to infer them.**

The novelty is the empirical combination and quantified same-system comparison in Camellia, not invention of any one underlying concept.

## Ecological boundary

Ecological evidence remains biologically important but enters **after** event identity.

Supported uses include:

- reproductive-service and pollen-limitation context;
- seasonal/weather dependence of pollinator service;
- sensory aliasing of coarse visible colour;
- possible filtering, establishment and persistence processes;
- generating explicit future tests once robust transition/event data exist.

Not supported:

- assigning a pollinator shift to an accepted-species colour-transition branch that fails the coding gate;
- assigning a temperature/climate cause to such a branch;
- treating current ecological association as proof of historical selection;
- treating coarse A/W/Y visible states as pollination syndromes.

The biologically useful cross-scale decomposition is therefore:

`molecular feasibility -> observation-conditioned recurrence -> macro realization/pattern -> event identity -> ecological attribution/persistence`.

## Relationship to partial identification

The literature-selected A/F/C/P analysis has a partial-identification structure: unresolved molecular cells imply a set of recurrence values consistent with the observed evidence rather than a single point value. The project uses exact enumeration of admissible completions to expose that set.

A 2026-08-27 novelty search did not identify a directly matching peer-reviewed flower-colour study that combines this incomplete-state bounding with same-system candidate-free remeasurement. This absence is **not** claimed as proof that no such paper exists. The manuscript should describe this as an application-specific design contribution unless a reproducible systematic review later establishes priority.

## Current novelty sentence

Use:

> Using the same public Camellia systems, standardized pathway-wide remeasurement contracts the mechanistic recurrence identifiable from literature-selected observations; independently, robust phylogenetic colour structure persists even though no shared accepted-species transition branch survives alternative wild-colour coding.

Avoid:

> We show for the first time that the same phenotype can evolve by different mechanisms.

or any equivalent broad priority claim.

## Reopening gates

### To claim systematic-review completeness

Require a frozen database protocol with database names, query strings, search dates, hit counts, deduplication and title/abstract/full-text decisions, including Chinese-language Camellia literature.

### To claim branch-specific ecological causation

Require a stable event set across predeclared trait encodings/topologies plus branch-relevant ecological exposure and a causal design or model that does not use the outcome to select predictors.

### To test micro-accessibility as a predictor of macro transitions

Require population-resolved pigment states, accepted-species nuclear histories with reticulation sensitivity, stable transition branches and a common ortholog/paralog map evaluated against the already frozen micro-only score.
