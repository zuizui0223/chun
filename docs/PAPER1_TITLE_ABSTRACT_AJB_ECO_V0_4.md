# Paper 1 title and abstract — AJB ecological integration v0.4

## Primary title

**Flexible molecular routes coexist with locally conserved flower colours in *Camellia***

## Running head

**Molecular flexibility and flower-colour conservatism**

## Structured abstract — 241 words

### Premise of the study

Flower-colour states can arise through multiple pigment-pathway routes, but molecular accessibility need not imply macroevolutionary lability. We asked whether molecular routes to colour recur, whether wild colours remain phylogenetically constrained, and which ecological processes have repeatable effects on reproductive performance in *Camellia*.

### Methods

We combined sequence-aware synthesis of pigment mechanisms with a 339-locus Angiosperms353 nuclear framework, World Flora Online accepted taxonomy, audited wild-colour states, and permutation tests of colour structure. We separately synthesized commensurable pollinator-service effects and structured evidence for pollen limitation, climate/season mediation, sensory function, and floral pigment manipulation.

### Key results

Molecular recurrence did not require one exact gene: FLS showed same-lineage recurrence, whereas independent DFR clusters used different paralog subclasses. Nearest-same-colour conservatism persisted across trait and topology sensitivities; on the UFBoot topology, *P* = 0.00116 (strict) and *P* = 0.000080 (dominant), although broad pairwise clustering was topology-sensitive. Across three independent A, Y, and W species, bird access increased fruit set by 2.29–6.35-fold (geometric mean RR = 3.53). Independent *Camellia oleifera* experiments and pollinator-abundance gradients likewise supported strong, context-dependent pollinator-service effects, while four taxa showed climate/season mediation of pollination conditions. Direct abiotic floral-pigment experiments remained under-replicated. No accepted-species colour-transition branch was robust to both trait scenarios.

### Conclusions

Flexible molecular implementations coexist with local phylogenetic conservatism, while reproductive service is strongly ecologically contingent. Public evidence supports pollinator-service filtering and climate/season mediation at the mechanism level, but cannot yet assign these causes to individual macroevolutionary colour transitions.

## Claim boundary

- ecological service/reliability effects are quantitative but not a formal cross-species inverse-variance random-effects meta-analysis;
- cross-species bird-service evidence spans A, Y, and W, so it does not establish an A/red-specific pollination syndrome;
- climate/season mediation is supported by heterogeneous systems and is not pooled as one commensurable effect;
- direct abiotic floral-pigment evidence remains under-replicated and confounded;
- accepted-species branch-specific causation remains unidentifiable after strict/dominant wild-colour auditing.
