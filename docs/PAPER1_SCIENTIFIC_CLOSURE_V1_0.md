## Final scientific story

The manuscript asks how much of the underlying pigment-network transition is replayed when similar flower-colour states are repeatedly generated through evolutionary time.

The retained answer is hierarchical:

1. standardized candidate-free remeasurement does **not** retain recurrence of one invariant complete A/F/C/P package;
2. molecular repeatability is **modular and transition-class dependent**, with stronger A/C/P agreement in yellow development than whole-package recurrence;
3. realised wild flower-colour states remain **locally phylogenetically structured** across topology and colour-coding sensitivities;
4. individual historical transition branches are **not robustly identifiable** across strict versus dominant wild-colour coding.

Therefore molecular generation, realised phylogenetic pattern, persistence, and historical event identity are treated as distinct evolutionary quantities.

## Ecology boundary

Ecological analyses in the current paper remain contextual/supplementary. The paper does not assign climate, pollinator, or other ecological causes to individual accepted-species colour-transition branches.

The *C. perpetua* seasonal programme, including winter mature-flower measurements, sensory/reward hypotheses, bird/insect access interventions, seasonal heterochrony, and future causal-closure experiments, is **post-Paper-1 work**. It is scientifically motivated by the accessibility-versus-realisation gap but is not a missing result required to support the current AJB manuscript.

*C. perpetua* remains in Paper 1 only where it is already part of the frozen yellow-development molecular dataset. Future seasonal ecological results must not be back-projected into Paper 1 after this closure.
