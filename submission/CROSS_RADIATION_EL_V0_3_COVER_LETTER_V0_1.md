Dear Editors of Evolution Letters,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Flower-color evolutionary memory is transient but lacks a universal phenotypic scale,”** for publication as a Letter in *Evolution Letters*.

The manuscript asks whether evolutionary history in flower colour is consistently captured most strongly at one biological resolution. We first converted an attractive retrospective pattern into a preregistered prediction that intermediate phenotype resolution should maximize same-state phylogenetic structure in an independent *Iris* radiation. That prediction failed. We then applied the same estimator and frozen state-support rules across a standardized 51-clade angiosperm flower-colour dataset. Among the 28 clades completing the three-resolution analysis, no clade showed a uniquely supported intermediate optimum; instead, coarse, fine, tied, and no-signal profiles all occurred.

We then re-expressed the already-observed pairwise structure as a relative-divergence persistence profile. Exact visible flower-colour retention generally declined with increasing relative evolutionary depth across eligible clades, providing a biological time-axis interpretation of the same phylogenetic information without treating that derived analysis as independent replication. A biochemical Petunieae analysis provides a bounded cross-representation example, while the manuscript explicitly avoids claiming a universal biochemical advantage or a first demonstration of flower-colour phylogenetic signal.

We believe the work is suited to *Evolution Letters* because it turns a plausible universal-scale hypothesis into a prospective falsification, then uses standardized cross-radiation analysis to establish a broader negative-and-positive result: flower-colour evolutionary memory is widespread but its strongest phenotypic representation differs among radiations. The paper therefore shifts the comparative question from “which phenotype scale is correct?” to “which ecological or mechanistic properties determine the rate and scale of memory loss?”

The manuscript is approximately 3,770 words excluding references, figure captions, and end matter. It includes five main figures, all with alt text, and all underlying analysis contracts, source manifests, frozen receipts, and code are versioned in the CHUN repository. The source datasets are cited with persistent identifiers, and a submission-specific CHUN archive identifier will be added before final submission.
