# C. perpetua future hypotheses v0.1

## Position relative to Paper 1

These hypotheses are **future-work hypotheses**, not results of the current Paper 1. Paper 1 establishes a gap: molecular flower-colour states are modularly accessible, realised wild colour remains locally phylogenetically structured, direct coarse annual-climate mapping is weak, and current public data do not identify historical transition causes.

*C. perpetua* is useful because public data bring the missing ecological-filter test unusually close to closure at one Guilin system. The aim is therefore not to retrofit a causal mechanism into Paper 1, but to define the next falsifiable study.

## Current empirical anchors

- summer 2022 mature-flower/developmental RNA-seq + metabolomics provide the CHUN `CPERPETUA` A/F/C/P trajectory;
- an independent summer 2023 flower cohort partially reproduces a late-bloom P-like galloylated-catechin decline;
- the nearby/same-program GBG ecology study shows a strong winter shift toward high nectar/sucrose, bird weighting, and reproductive success;
- no eligible winter mature-flower molecular/spectral dataset has been recovered;
- the future 15-plant paired observational runner and separate sensory/pollinator-access interventions are preregistered.

The key scientific question is therefore:

> **When the same long-lived plant flowers in ecologically different seasons, does ecological filtering act only through reward and pollinator availability, or does the flower itself enter a different cryptic biochemical/sensory state?**

---

## HCP1 — Reward-only seasonal filtering

### Idea

Season changes temperature, nectar quantity/composition and the available pollinator guild, but the mature petal molecular/sensory state is effectively stable.

`season -> reward/microclimate -> pollinator weighting -> fitness`

### Predictions

- winter nectar/reward and bird weighting replicate;
- A/F/C/P and primary bee-facing color contrast are within independently frozen equivalence bounds;
- a spectral/sensory variable does not improve pollinator-service prediction beyond season + reward + microclimate;
- direct sensory manipulation does not alter legitimate bee effectiveness after manipulation checks pass.

### What would make it interesting?

This would show that the relevant ecological phenotype is **the whole flower/reproductive niche rather than petal colour itself**. It would explain why coarse visible flower colour can remain phylogenetically conserved even while pollinator function changes strongly across seasons.

### Falsification

A non-equivalent latent-state shift that adds season-adjusted ecological prediction and survives direct sensory manipulation rejects reward-only as a sufficient explanation.

---

## HCP2 — Cryptic sensory reweighting under stable visible colour

### Idea

The flower can remain approximately the same yellow to humans while changing in UV/fluorescence/bee-visible space. Ecological filtering may therefore operate on a cryptic sensory state that coarse colour coding misses.

`season -> cryptic spectral state -> bee response/effectiveness -> pollinator weighting`

### Primary prediction

If winter conditions shift the system away from bees and toward birds, **winter full-bloom flowers should have lower bee-facing chromatic salience than summer**, even if human-visible hue is approximately equivalent.

### Decisive tests

1. human-visible colour equivalence but lower winter Apis color-hexagon contrast;
2. season-adjusted G4: bee contrast improves held-out service prediction beyond season + nectar + sucrose + temperature;
3. G4X: an active manipulation that reduces bee-facing contrast, while passing odor/temperature/reward controls, causally reduces legitimate bee response or single-visit pollen deposition.

### Why this is especially important

This directly tests the emerging CHUN distinction:

`visible hue != ecological floral state`.

A positive result would also explain why annual climate fails to predict coarse A/W colour: selection may act on dimensions invisible to the coarse trait matrix.

### Falsification

Bee-facing contrast is seasonally equivalent, or a validated optical manipulation changes the cue but not bee response/effectiveness.

---

## HCP3 — Bird reliability / reproductive-insurance hypothesis

### Idea

Winter does not necessarily select a 'bird flower' in the classical syndrome sense. Instead, birds may become disproportionately valuable when cold-season insect service becomes unreliable.

`winter bee unreliability -> increased marginal value of bird access -> reproductive insurance`

### Predictions

- open flowers outperform bird-excluded flowers in winter seed set;
- the open-minus-bird-exclusion contrast is larger in winter than summer;
- in summer, bird-excluded flowers still outperform full animal exclusion because insects remain useful;
- hand cross-pollination provides a ceiling/pollen-limitation context rather than defining the mechanism.

### Decisive intervention

Use the frozen 30-bud allocation per plant per season and block-randomized treatment assignment:
- open 8;
- bird exclusion 8;
- full animal exclusion 7;
- hand cross 7.

Inference permutes flower/bud treatment labels only within plant × season blocks.

### Why this is biologically interesting

The prediction is not 'red/yellow flowers evolved for birds.' It is stronger and more general:

> **pollinator identity can act as a context-dependent insurance system, so the ecological value of a floral state depends on when the plant flowers.**

This is a plausible mechanism by which ecological filtering could determine persistence without requiring deterministic pollination syndromes.

### Falsification

Valid bird exclusion does not reduce winter seed set, or bird contribution is not greater in winter.

## HCP4 — Seasonal heterochrony of floral molecular state

### Idea

This is the most mechanistically novel hypothesis. Winter flowers may not invent a new biochemical state. Instead, full-bloom winter flowers may occupy a different position on the **same developmental trajectory** already observed across summer S1–S5.

`season -> developmental timing/state shift -> different full-bloom biochemical/sensory phenotype`

### Competing predictions

**Heterochrony:** winter full-bloom samples project primarily along the frozen summer S1–S5 molecular trajectory.

**Season-specific reprogramming:** winter full-bloom samples lie substantially orthogonal to that summer trajectory and therefore represent a new seasonal state rather than a timing displacement.

### Test

- freeze the summer developmental manifold using the existing Zhu 2024 A/F/C/P and, if technically comparable, transcriptome/metabolome space;
- collect winter full-bloom samples without using them to refit the summer manifold;
- decompose winter displacement into along-trajectory projection and orthogonal residual distance;
- batch/assay controls must be resolved before biological interpretation.

### Why this could be a strong paper

It connects **developmental evolution/plasticity and pollination ecology**. If supported, seasonal ecological filtering would operate partly by reusing an existing developmental programme at a shifted endpoint rather than creating a new pathway state.

### Falsification

Winter samples fall outside the summer developmental manifold after technical controls, favoring season-specific reprogramming.

---

## HCP5 — Modular ecological reconfiguration

### Idea

Paper 1 finds modularity at the molecular-evolutionary level. The ecological phenotype may be modular too: reward, petal chemistry/sensory state and pollinator service need not move as one classical 'pollination syndrome.'

`season -> {reward module, sensory module, biochemical module} -> service`

### Predictions

- seasonal changes are not captured by one dominant floral syndrome axis alone;
- reward and sensory modules can show different magnitudes or even different directions;
- sensory state can add ecological information after reward is already known, or reward can remain important when sensory state is weak;
- module-specific intervention effects are therefore expected rather than all traits changing coordinately.

### Test

Freeze module scores before viewing pollinator outcomes, quantify cross-module covariance, and compare out-of-sample models with reward and sensory modules entered separately. The current G4 already provides one key conditional test.

### Why it matters

This would extend the main conceptual result across scales:

> **modularity may characterize not only how flower colour is generated, but also how floral phenotypes are ecologically filtered.**

### Falsification

One dominant latent syndrome explains essentially all seasonal floral change and additional modules add no independent predictive or experimental information.

---

## HCP6 — Developmental P-like canalization with ecological plasticity elsewhere

### Idea

The late-bloom P-like decline is the one *C. perpetua* molecular pattern with partial independent summer replication. It may be a developmentally canalized module while other axes remain seasonally plastic.

### Prediction

Matched winter developmental sampling retains the late-stage P-like/flavan-3-ol decline even if A/F/C, spectra or rewards change seasonally.

### Why it matters

This would provide a concrete example of **stable and plastic modules coexisting in the same flower**, linking the Paper 1 modular-recurrence result to within-species ecological plasticity.

### Falsification

Winter removes or reverses the late-bloom P-like decline beyond frozen repeatability bounds.

## Priority

### Highest ecological-causal value

**HCP3 bird reliability insurance + HCP2 cryptic sensory reweighting.**

Together they close both sides of the ecological filter:

`sensory state -> pollinator response` and `pollinator access -> fitness`.

### Highest conceptual novelty

**HCP4 seasonal heterochrony.**

This can reveal whether seasonal floral states are generated by shifting an existing developmental programme or by orthogonal biochemical reprogramming.

### Strongest bridge back to Paper 1

**HCP5 modular ecological reconfiguration.**

It tests whether the modularity discovered in molecular recurrence also appears in the ecological filtering layer.

### Essential competing null

**HCP1 reward-only filtering.**

A negative sensory result is scientifically useful if equivalence is established rather than inferred from nonsignificance.

## Claim boundary

Even full support for HCP2–HCP5 would establish an **extant seasonal ecological-filter mechanism within one long-lived Camellia system**. It would not identify the cause of a specific historical accepted-species flower-colour transition. That macrohistorical question remains separate until robust transition events and comparable population-level trait states become identifiable.
