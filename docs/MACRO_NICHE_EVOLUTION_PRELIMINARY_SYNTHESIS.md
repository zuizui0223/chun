# Macro niche evolution: preliminary synthesis generated from chun results

## 1. Keep source conclusions separate from chun discoveries

### Published conclusions used as inputs

- Fan et al. 2026 reconstruct white as the most likely ancestral **visible** flower colour of *Camellia* and show that yellow taxa are geographically concentrated near the putative southwestern China–northern Vietnam ancestral region, while red and white states are geographically broader.
- Wang et al. 2022 show across 7,673 Chinese woody species that anthocyanin-associated red/blue-purple flower classes are relatively enriched in colder, drier, more seasonal and higher-UV parts of China; after phylogenetic/regional correction, red frequency is negatively associated with warmth index and positively associated with UV-B.
- Koski & Ashman 2016 show across 177 Potentilleae species that quantitative UV-absorbing floral pigmentation is greater at higher altitude and lower temperature, with positive UV-B association; an OU correlation structure fit better than BM for this trait-environment relationship.
- Shrestha et al. 2014 show that floral colour diversity is greater in the high-elevation subalpine Himalayan assemblage and that several lineages leading to high-elevation taxa show greater absolute/rate of colour evolution.

These are literature-level results. None by itself demonstrates that anthocyanin enabled *Camellia* to invade cold environments.

## 2. chun preliminary macro pattern

When the independent macro studies are recoded around the same causal question, a recurrent pattern appears:

> Floral pigmentation/colour disparity tends to increase or shift in colder, higher-elevation and/or higher-UV environments, whereas the basal yellow-rich *Camellia* radiation is concentrated in the warm-humid southern ancestral region and red/white states have expanded more broadly.

This cross-study recurrence is a **chun synthesis result**, not a claim copied from one paper.

It is still heterogeneous: visible anthocyanin colour, UV-absorbing pigmentation and colour disparity are not the same trait. Therefore the result is a signal that justifies a targeted *Camellia* analysis, not a pooled pigment effect size yet.

## 3. Problem generated by the preliminary pattern

The micro-analysis already found repeated reuse of regulatory/flux nodes rather than repeated destruction/reconstruction of pigment pathways. The macro synthesis now suggests that floral pigmentation is repeatedly associated with environmental transitions.

The resulting problem is:

> **Does molecular accessibility of pigment states merely generate visible variation, or does it change which climatic niches a lineage can enter and persist in?**

This is different from asking whether colour and climate are correlated at present.

The causal ordering to test is:

`accessible pigment-state change -> climatic niche shift / range expansion`

versus alternatives:

`climatic niche shift -> secondary pigment change`

or

`shared geography/phylogeny -> both pigment and niche without causal coupling`.

## 4. Primary new hypothesis: pigment-enabled niche expansion

### HN1 — Anthocyanin-enabled cold/UV niche expansion

Lineages that can up-regulate floral anthocyanin deployment have a higher probability of shifting into colder, higher-UV or more seasonal climatic niches than lineages remaining in low-pigment states.

This hypothesis is generated from the combination of two **chun preliminary findings**:

1. anthocyanin deployment is repeatedly accessible through regulatory/flux changes at micro scales;
2. macro comparative datasets independently show stronger floral pigmentation or colour disparity in cold/high-altitude/high-UV settings.

#### Predictions

- branches with W -> A (white/low-pigment to anthocyanin-rich) transitions should be followed by a larger decrease in thermal niche optimum than matched branches without colour transitions;
- climatic niche rate should increase around pigment-state transitions if colour change opens previously inaccessible environmental space;
- red/pink lineages should occupy colder lower-tail temperatures or greater climatic breadth after phylogeny/geography are controlled;
- the effect should be stronger for mechanistically verified anthocyanin deployment than for human-visible red labels alone;
- if cold adaptation is causal, pigment transitions should temporally precede or coincide with cold-niche shifts more often than follow them.

#### Falsifiers

- cold-niche shifts consistently precede pigment shifts;
- colour state adds no predictive power after geography and phylogeny;
- mechanistic anthocyanin scores do not associate with cold/UV niche axes even when visible red does;
- yellow/white lineages repeatedly enter equally cold niches without corresponding protective pigment deployment.

## 5. Competing hypotheses

### HN0 — Historical sorting only

Colour and climate covary because major clades originated in different regions. Once ancestral range and phylogeny are controlled, colour does not predict niche evolution.

### HN2 — Stress-induced follower

Cold/UV environments select for increased floral pigmentation only after lineages enter those environments. Pigmentation is a response to niche change, not an enabling innovation.

### HN3 — Pollinator-mediated climate coupling

Climate changes pollinator communities/seasonal reliability, and colour evolves primarily through sensory selection. Apparent colour-climate association is mediated by pollinators rather than direct abiotic protection.

### HN4 — Dual-function co-option

The same flavonoid network is selected simultaneously for abiotic stress protection and pollinator signalling. This predicts stronger coupling between climate and pigment chemistry than between climate and human-visible hue.

### HN5 — Accessibility-filter interaction

Molecular accessibility biases which pigment phenotypes are generated; climate and pollinators then filter those variants. Macroevolutionary transition rates are therefore an interaction between mechanistic distance and ecological opportunity.

## 6. Existing niche-evolution models and the methodological gap

### Brownian motion (BM)

BM models niche evolution as unconstrained diffusion. It provides a neutral baseline but does not represent adaptive optima or state-dependent accessibility.

### Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (OU)

OU adds attraction toward an optimum. Multiple-optimum OU can represent different climatic adaptive zones, but OU support alone must not be interpreted as proof of stabilizing selection or adaptation.

### Early Burst (EB)

EB allows high early rates followed by slowdown. It is useful for testing adaptive-radiation tempo, but a whole-clade EB does not identify whether pigment transitions open environmental space; nested/multirate models are more appropriate.

### Bounded Brownian Motion (BBM)

The likely "Brownian something" relevant here is **Bounded Brownian Motion**: BM evolving between reflective bounds. BBM can distinguish a hard environmental constraint from OU-like attraction to an optimum. This is important because *Camellia* climatic niches may be limited by available East Asian climate rather than stabilizing selection.

### White noise / lambda / kappa and rate-shift models

WN describes very rapid loss of phylogenetic signal; Pagel transformations and multirate BM/OU can model different tempo or phylogenetic dependence. These are useful sensitivity models but still do not encode molecular accessibility as the driver of a niche transition.

### The gap relevant to chun

Most comparative niche models model **the niche value or its rate**. They generally do not ask whether a discrete molecularly accessible trait transition changes the probability/rate/direction of subsequent niche movement.

The target model comparison for chun should therefore include:

1. BM / EB / BBM / single-optimum OU baselines for climatic niche axes;
2. multi-regime OU or multirate BM where regimes are defined by pigment state;
3. branch-event models testing whether climatic shift magnitude/rate changes after W->A, A->W, W->Y, etc.;
4. directional lead-lag tests comparing `pigment first` versus `niche first` histories;
5. hidden-state alternatives because unmeasured traits (pollinator, leaf physiology, growth form) may generate apparent colour-dependent rates.

## 7. Immediate Camellia macro meta-analysis

### M8 — species climatic niche matrix

For every *Camellia* taxon that can be joined to an admitted nuclear tree:

- clean native GBIF occurrences;
- extract CHELSA/WorldClim climate;
- calculate species means, medians and tails for temperature, precipitation, seasonality, VPD/UV proxies and elevation;
- retain climatic breadth and not only centroid values.

### M9 — phylogenetic signal and model comparison

For each niche axis compare:

- BM;
- OU;
- EB;
- BBM where computationally feasible;
- multirate BM;
- colour-regime OU.

Do not label OU as adaptation without predictive/temporal evidence.

### M10 — event-centred niche-shift meta-analysis

Using stochastic maps across nuclear tree uncertainty, estimate for every colour-transition branch:

- pre/post thermal niche shift;
- change in niche evolutionary rate;
- shift in lower cold limit;
- shift in climatic breadth.

Then pool event-level effects hierarchically rather than treating species as independent replicates.

### M11 — trait-first versus niche-first

Use stochastic histories / ancestral niche reconstruction to compare the timing of pigment and environmental shifts. The key statistic is not present-day correlation but the fraction of transition histories in which pigment change precedes cold-niche movement.

### M12 — micro-to-macro-to-niche enrichment

Combine the micro accessibility score with macro transition and niche-shift magnitude:

`micro accessibility of pigment module -> macro reuse probability -> climatic niche shift magnitude`.

This is the strongest cross-scale test of the programme.

## 8. Claim boundary

Supported now as a preliminary synthesis:

> Independent macro comparative datasets repeatedly associate floral pigmentation/colour diversification with high elevation, low temperature and/or high UV, while *Camellia* micro-mechanistic evidence shows that anthocyanin deployment is repeatedly reachable through regulatory/flux changes.

Not yet supported:

- anthocyanin caused cold-climate colonisation in *Camellia*;
- red flowers are universally cold-adapted;
- OU support would prove adaptive evolution;
- flower pigment rather than correlated leaf/whole-plant physiology is the causal stress-tolerance trait.

The next decisive result is therefore a branch-level lead-lag test linking pigment-state transitions to climatic niche shifts on multiple nuclear tree sets.
