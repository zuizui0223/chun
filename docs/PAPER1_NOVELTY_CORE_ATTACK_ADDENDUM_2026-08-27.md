# Paper 1 novelty core attack addendum — 2026-08-27

## Purpose

After the high-recall flower-colour and Camellia-specific audit, this addendum attacks the **remaining conceptual core** outside flower-colour biology. The question is whether the terms now carrying the paper — `identified set`, `identifiability`, and `observation/measurement process` — are themselves new ideas.

## Verdict

**No. None of those concepts is new in isolation.**

The defensible Paper 1 contribution is therefore entirely empirical/application-specific:

> in the same public *Camellia* biological systems, a standardized A/F/C/P observation protocol contracts the recurrence identified from literature-selected measurements; separately, a topology/coding-robust visible-colour pattern remains identifiable while no shared accepted-species transition branch survives the predeclared event gate.

The paper should not imply that it invented partial identification, biological measurement-process thinking, or phylogenetic identifiability.

## 1. Partial identification is established statistical theory

Manski (2003, 2005) formalized partial identification as inference on a set of parameter values compatible with observed data and maintained assumptions when missing data or weak assumptions do not point-identify the target. In the missing-data case, an identification region is obtained by considering all admissible values/distributions for what is unobserved rather than silently imputing one value.

This is structurally close to the Paper 1 literature A/F/C/P analysis: unresolved cluster×axis directions generate a set of admissible exact-signature recurrence and pairwise-concordance values.

### Consequence

Do **not** claim:

- a new theory of identified sets;
- a new statistical principle that missing states should be bounded rather than imputed;
- `partial identification` as a general methodological invention of this paper.

What remains specific is the biological construction: exact enumeration of missing pigment-state directions in a repeated-evolution literature matrix, followed by contraction of that set after same-system candidate-free remeasurement.

## 2. Identifiability is established in phylogenetic comparative methods

Identifiability has a long technical history in phylogenetics. Chang (1996) addressed identifiability of Markov evolutionary-tree models. Ho and Ané (2014) showed intrinsic non-identifiability/ridge problems for ancestral states and selection optima in Ornstein–Uhlenbeck trait models. Gill et al. (2017) explicitly designed a relaxed directional random-walk model to preserve identifiability of branch trends.

### Consequence

Do **not** claim:

- that event/process identifiability is a new phylogenetic concept;
- that recognizing unstable ancestral histories is a new methodological insight;
- that the paper introduces identifiability into comparative biology.

What remains specific is the empirical gate: under one predeclared *Camellia* design, nearest-same-colour structure survives two nuclear topologies and two wild-colour treatments while the shared robust transition-event set is empty.

## 3. Measurement-process dependence is established biological theory

Mitteroecker (2026) argues explicitly that biological measurements are outcomes of biological, technical and experimental measurement processes and need not be treated as noisy access to one process-independent `true value`.

Conte et al. (2012) already gives a closer evolutionary example: estimates of genetic reuse differ by discovery method, including higher estimates in candidate-gene studies than in genetic-cross studies.

### Consequence

Do **not** claim:

- `measurement process matters` as a new philosophical principle;
- `observation regime shapes inference` as a generic first demonstration.

What remains specific is the matched empirical manipulation of observation regime in *Camellia*: the biological system set is held fixed while the molecular observation rule is standardized, and the identified recurrence changes quantitatively.

## 4. Strongest surviving novelty after the conceptual attack

The most defensible contribution is not any node below by itself:

`mechanistic heterogeneity`

`candidate-gene ascertainment`

`partial identification`

`phylogenetic identifiability`

`measurement-process dependence`

`micro-to-macro integration`

All have prior art.

The surviving contribution is the **joint empirical result under a frozen cross-scale design**:

1. the published mechanism matrix is incomplete and nonuniform;
2. exact bounds expose what recurrence is and is not identified before imputation;
3. the same public biological systems are remeasured with one outcome-independent A/F/C/P protocol;
4. the identified set contracts and whole-package recurrence is lost while transition-class-specific modular reuse remains;
5. accepted wild flower colours retain a topology/coding-robust local phylogenetic pattern;
6. individual accepted-species transition events fail the separate coding-robustness gate;
7. ecological attribution is stopped rather than allowed to fill that event uncertainty.

No directly matching peer-reviewed study was located in the 2026-08-27 searches that combines all seven operations in one repeated flower-colour analysis.

This is an **absence in a high-recall novelty audit**, not proof of universal priority. Avoid `first` unless a formal systematic search later establishes it.

## 5. Ecological significance after the attack

The ecology is still substantive, but its value is not a new pollination or climate mechanism.

The biological interpretation is:

`molecular accessibility -> realization -> ecological filtering/persistence`

cannot be collapsed into one arrow from mechanism to historical adaptation.

Multiple molecular implementations are feasible and modules recur, yet extant wild colour is locally phylogenetically structured. This leaves establishment and persistence as real biological filters. Current pollination/climate data motivate candidate filters but cannot be attached to individual historical branches because event identity fails first.

That is an ecologically meaningful negative boundary: it tells us **which next population-level measurements can turn a broad macro pattern into a causal ecological test**.

## 6. Manuscript terminology rule

Preferred wording:

- `identified set` or `exact bounds over unresolved A/F/C/P cells`, with no priority claim;
- `event-identification gate` as a project-specific operational gate;
- `observation-regime standardization` as an empirical design;
- `matched cross-scale inferential audit` as the contribution.

Avoid wording that makes the paper sound like a new general statistical/philosophical theory of identification or measurement.
