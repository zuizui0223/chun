# Cross-radiation resolution-dependent flower-colour predictability — prior-art audit v0.1

## Decision

This is a **targeted representative prior-art audit**, not a systematic review and not evidence for a priority claim.

The broad ingredients of the current synthesis are individually established in prior work. The defensible novelty candidate is therefore not any one ingredient, but their empirical alignment across independent flower-colour radiations.

Current status:

`NOVELTY_CANDIDATE = EMPIRICAL_CROSS_RADIATION_ALIGNMENT_OF_RESOLUTION_DEPENDENCE`

## What is already known

### 1. Transition type changes which genetic targets are predictable

Rausher (2008), Wessinger & Rausher (2012), and Sobel & Streisfeld (2013) already established that flower-colour transitions are not exchangeable. Gain/loss direction, pathway position, regulatory versus functional changes, developmental context, and population frequency can alter expected genetic routes.

Therefore this programme cannot claim novelty for saying that repeatability is transition dependent.

### 2. Similar visible flower colours can arise by different biochemical or genetic routes

Ng & Smith (2016) showed at Solanaceae scale that repeated red flowers can be made with anthocyanins, carotenoids, or both, with historical/phylogenetic structure in mechanism use. Ipomoea and Mimulus work likewise established recurrent visible outcomes with both recurrent and divergent lower-level mechanisms.

Therefore the statement `same visible colour != same molecular mechanism` is prior art.

### 3. Pathway structure can constrain accessible colour phenotypes

Stepwise Solanaceae anthocyanin evolution and the broader flower-colour evo-devo literature already connect pathway topology to accessible evolutionary routes.

Therefore biochemical-pathway constraint is not a new conceptual contribution.

### 4. Different pigmentation dimensions can map to different pathway regions within a radiation

Larter et al. (2018) showed in Iochrominae that repeated pigment losses converge on late pathway genes while hue shifts involve branching genes. Wheeler et al. (2023) connected multivariate Petunieae pigment axes with coordinated pathway expression.

Therefore phenotype-axis-specific molecular mapping within one clade is not itself novel.

### 5. Trait representation and recoding can alter phylogenetic inference

Tarasov (2019) formalized hierarchical/hidden problems in discrete phenotypic character construction. Vera-Ruiz et al. (2022) showed formally that state recoding/lumping can fail to preserve the original Markov process and can distort phylogenetic quantities.

Therefore generic claims that character coding, aggregation, or resolution matter are prior art.

### 6. Multi-scale flower-colour synthesis is established

Modern reviews already connect molecular, developmental, microevolutionary and macroevolutionary flower-colour processes. Simply combining multiple scales is not a novelty claim.

## What remains distinct in the current programme

The present repository now tests **one empirical candidate across several independent evidence layers** rather than merely citing the conceptual ingredients:

### Macro state representation

Across Linoideae, Angraecinae and Antirrhineae:

- fine-state phylogenetic organization = **3/3**;
- strong universal transition direction = **0/3** comparable radiations;
- enriched preselected biological coarse boundary = **0/3**.

Thus the repeated property is fine-state organization, not one shared direction or one shared coarse partition.

### Molecular phenotype-axis localization

Across Iochrominae, Cape Erica and Petunieae:

- hue/hydroxylation -> branching machinery = **3/3** systems;
- Petunieae supplies the first preregistered prospective component in this repository and passes the hue gate with AICc margin **11.60282936**;
- fixed late/output localization for pigment amount/depletion = **1/3** systems;
- Cape Erica is heterogeneous and the preregistered Petunieae raw-amount axis fails its primary distinct-output prediction.

Thus a biochemically specific phenotype axis appears more cross-system stable than a broader amount/depletion axis, while the full two-axis rule remains unvalidated.

### Mechanistic recurrence level

The seven-system benchmark spans exact-gene/regulatory recurrence, pathway-module recurrence, regulator-class recurrence, recurrent-core recurrence, and heterogeneous causal nodes. None of the seven systems requires an invariant complete multivariate programme as its strongest supported recurrence level.

These denominators are not exchangeable and are not pooled numerically.

## Current novelty candidate

> **We test whether resolution-dependent evolutionary predictability aligns across independent flower-colour radiations at macroevolutionary state-representation, phenotype-axis-to-molecular-subspace, and mechanistic-recurrence levels.**

The potentially publishable contribution is the **empirical cross-radiation alignment**:

1. fine-state organization is more reproducible than one privileged coarse state representation;
2. some biochemically resolved phenotype axes are more reproducibly localized to molecular subspaces than broader visible/pigment dimensions;
3. molecular recurrence is distributed hierarchically rather than concentrated at one universal complete programme level.

This is a candidate synthesis, not yet a universal law.

## Why no priority wording is allowed

The present audit is representative and targeted. It was designed to attack the exact novelty claim, but it is not PRISMA-complete, citation-network-complete, or a proof that no earlier study integrated comparable layers.

Forbidden language includes:

- “first demonstration that flower-colour evolution is resolution dependent”;
- “first demonstration that state coding matters”;
- “first evidence that the same colour can arise by different mechanisms”;
- “first pathway-level predictability test”;
- “a universal law of flower-colour predictability.”

## Strongest competitors / conceptual precursors

- Rausher 2008 — transition asymmetry and repeated pathway tendencies.
- Wessinger & Rausher 2012 — transition-type dependence of mutational targets.
- Sobel & Streisfeld 2013 — flower colour as a predictability/evo-devo model.
- Ng & Smith 2016 — repeated visible convergence through alternate pigment pathways.
- Larter et al. 2018 — pathway-level predictability and distinct molecular regions for loss versus hue transitions in Iochrominae.
- Tarasov 2019 — structured/hierarchical character representation and hidden developmental processes.
- Vera-Ruiz et al. 2022 — formal phylogenetic consequences of state lumping/recoding.
- Wheeler et al. 2023 — multivariate floral pigment axes linked to pathway expression in Petunieae.
- Kellenberger & Glover 2023 — flower-colour evolution across biological timescales.

## Next falsification gate

The programme should now broaden evidence **by phenotype dimension**, not by looking for one clade that copies Camellia completely.

### M1 — matched-axis radiation tier

Keep Iochrominae, Cape Erica and Petunieae as the directly comparable phenotype-axis -> molecular-subspace set. Add a system only when both hue/branching and amount/depletion information can be separated under the same radiation-level observation frame.

### M2 — hue/branching external functional tier

Use systems such as Ipomoea as independent functional stress tests for repeated branching-node involvement. Do not silently count them as M1 if the observation regime is event-specific rather than radiation-wide matched measurement.

### M3 — pigment amount/depletion tier

Use Aquilegia and other repeated losses to test whether output/late-module localization is genuinely less stable than hue/branching localization. Keep event-level and cross-species-expression units separate.

### Prospective rule

Every newly admitted M1 test must freeze phenotype definitions, molecular subspaces, transformations, phylogenetic treatment and decision thresholds before decisive molecular values are inspected.

## Journal consequence

This cross-radiation programme is scientifically broader than the Camellia AJB Paper 1 and should be developed as a **separate manuscript**. A general-evolution target becomes plausible if the cross-radiation result survives expanded admission and prior-art stress testing; the present audit alone does not authorize a journal escalation claim.

Camellia Paper 1 remains separate and scientifically closed.
