# Cross-scale result: accessibility is not macro realization

## Current result

The project now has independent evidence at three scales that cannot be explained by one simple `red = adaptation` narrative.

### 1. Molecular generation layer

Dependence-aware mechanistic synthesis shows repeated regulatory/pathway-flux changes (8/8 independence clusters) and recurrent red-direction anthocyanin increase (6/6 informative clusters).

### 2. Macro historical realization layer

The full Fan 2026 formal-section table contains 98 deterministic species across 17 traditional sections after removing source rows where `Vietnam` is a country label rather than a formal section.

Count-controlled 100,000-label-shuffle results:

- A: 25 species, 4 sections, expected 9.568; breadth P=0.00002; entropy P<0.00001;
- Y: 17 species, 4 sections, expected 8.045; breadth P=0.00130; entropy P<0.00001;
- W: 56 species, 13 sections, expected 13.480; breadth P=0.496.

Thus the exact-50 accessibility–persistence pattern reproduces in the larger source-derived section table. The A concentration is not an artifact of the climate-subset sampling frame.

### 3. Ecological mapping layer

Coarse visible A/W differences do not identify greater thermal-niche divergence within the two shared traditional-section proxies. The pollination-function diagnostic likewise shows that coarse visible hue is not a deterministic reproductive state.

## The new cross-scale problem

The molecular evidence says large pigment-state changes are repeatedly achievable. The macro evidence says A and Y are highly non-uniform across historical backgrounds. The ecological screens say human-visible hue alone does not explain that non-uniformity through either one thermal niche axis or one fixed pollinator syndrome.

Therefore the main problem is now:

> **What converts mechanistic accessibility into long-term macroevolutionary realization?**

The answer cannot be assumed to be selection/persistence alone. Macro concentration can arise because gains are lineage-gated, because gains are broadly generated but rarely persist, because one early origin radiated, or because reticulation redistributed the state.

## Required decomposition

`micro accessibility -> macro gain hazard -> persistence/loss hazard -> extant state distribution`

The next nuclear-tree analyses should estimate the generation and persistence components separately.

This changes the interpretation of `H_LINEAGE_PERMISSIVITY`:

- **generation permissivity:** lineage background changes the probability that an accessible molecular switch becomes a heritable macro transition;
- **persistence permissivity:** lineage/ecological background changes the probability that a generated transition survives and spreads.

## Why this matters for reactivation

A candidate active -> low -> active history should not be interpreted only as “the old pathway was available again.” The decisive questions are:

1. was reactivation generated more readily in a particular genomic background?
2. was the reactivated state selected/persisted only after a climate/pollinator/phenology change?
3. is the apparent regain actually one retained ancestral lineage or introgressed state?

Thus reactivation selection-pressure attribution belongs primarily to the persistence/order layer after the historical regain itself is established.

## Claim boundary

Traditional sections are only a coarse history proxy and may not be monophyletic. These analyses demonstrate cross-scale non-uniformity and generate the decomposition problem; they do not estimate actual gain/loss rates or prove adaptive filtering.
