# Cross-clade H1–H6 decision matrix — v0.1

## Purpose

The cross-clade programme now contains enough material that the six retained hypotheses should not be presented as equally mature. This matrix asks which hypotheses are directly supported across independent systems, which are only partially supported, and which have already been reduced by counterexamples or prior art.

The matrix uses the 18-clade v0.5 atlas plus the later prospectively admitted Petunieae phenotype-axis test. Missing cells are not treated as biological negatives.

## Decision summary

### H4 — representation / observation resolution dependence

**Decision: primary flagship claim.**

Direct support spans **seven systems across both macro and molecular levels**.

Macro state representation:
- Linoideae;
- Angraecinae;
- Antirrhineae.

In all three, fine-state phylogenetic organization is supported, while a shared privileged coarse partition or universal direction is not.

Molecular / observation resolution:
- Camellia — literature observation versus frozen candidate-free A/F/C/P remeasurement;
- Iochrominae — hue versus intensity map to different molecular subspaces;
- Cape Erica — hue branch versus pigment depletion localize differently;
- Petunieae — prospectively frozen hue PASS / raw amount FAIL-MIXED.

Petunieae is the only prospective paired phenotype-axis component.

The cross-scale candidate is therefore:

> **Evolutionary predictability in flower colour depends on the resolution at which states and mechanisms are represented. Fine phylogenetic states and selected biochemical or regulatory dimensions recur more consistently than privileged coarse boundaries, universal transition directions or complete molecular implementations.**

### H3 — hierarchical repeatability

**Decision: secondary mechanistic spine.**

Seven systems provide direct or partial support:
- Camellia;
- Epimedium;
- Petunia;
- Cape Erica;
- Aquilegia;
- Iochrominae;
- Ipomoea.

They do not share one numeric denominator. Their common result is categorical: repeatability can be strongest at exact regulatory, pathway-module, regulator-class or core-module levels while lower-level or complete implementations remain heterogeneous.

This is a strong cross-clade result, but generic hierarchical convergence is prior art. The contribution is its empirical alignment with H4, not invention of hierarchy itself.

### H6 — ecological filtering conditional on latent phenotype

**Decision: supported ecological context, not flagship causal claim.**

Direct support: Petunia, Aquilegia, Euonymus, Linanthus.
Partial support: Camellia, Silene.

The common lesson is that selection/filtering is often tied to pollinator function, correlated floral traits or local fitness rather than coarse human-visible hue alone. However, branch-specific causes are not resolved across the atlas.

### H2 — retained capacity / regulatory redeployment

**Decision: supported but novelty-preempted.**

Petunia provides direct regain evidence. Acer and Dalechampia are explicit prior-art controls showing genetic-context dependence. Epimedium is compatible with retained network capacity but lacks a resolved historical regain event.

The atlas may use H2 as a mechanistic bridge, but not as a novelty headline.

### H1 — baseline-dependent transition architecture

**Decision: stronger formulation falsified; core rate/route hypothesis unresolved.**

Hydrangea and Linanthus reject simple universal white-root/one-way-gain narratives. Linoideae, Angraecinae, Antirrhineae and Silene are compatible with baseline- or state-dependent transition architecture, but none independently isolates a cross-baseline causal rate effect.

Therefore the defensible result is negative/conditional:

> there is no universal one-way flower-colour transition direction, and ancestral state representation can matter, but a general baseline-dependent transition-rate law is not yet established.

### H5 — accessibility–realization gap

**Decision: partial only.**

Camellia separates molecular generation, robust pattern and event identity. Epimedium separates endpoint mechanism from historical event independence. Nicotiana stresses event identity under reticulation.

These are compatible with an accessibility–realization gap but do not estimate one common feasible-state versus realized-state quantity. H5 should not be used as the flagship until such an estimator exists.

## Resulting paper architecture

### Claim 1 — flagship

**Representation-dependent predictability.**

At the macro scale, fine-state organization replicates while coarse boundaries/directions do not. At the molecular scale, resolved phenotype dimensions map reproducibly to particular molecular resolutions, including one prospective Petunieae component.

### Claim 2 — mechanistic explanation

**Hierarchical repeatability.**

The level at which evolution repeats differs among transitions and clades. Complete molecular replay is not required for reproducible organization at a higher mechanistic level.

### Claim 3 — boundary rather than positive law

**Ecology and history constrain realization, but branch-specific causes remain incompletely identified.**

This keeps H6/H5 visible without turning incomplete causal evidence into the paper headline.

## What not to claim

- one universal colour direction;
- one universal pigment gene;
- one complete recurrent pathway programme;
- a pooled recurrence percentage across unlike units;
- a universal baseline-dependent transition-rate law;
- an atlas-wide branch-specific pollinator or climate cause;
- full prospective replication of H4.

## Current evidence maturity ranking

1. **H4 — flagship-ready**;
2. **H3 — strong secondary spine**;
3. **H6 — supported context, causal generalization open**;
4. **H2 — supported but prior-art limited**;
5. **H1 — stronger form falsified, core hypothesis unresolved**;
6. **H5 — partial only**.

Camellia Paper 1 remains closed and unchanged.
