# Temporal flower-colour variation: novelty boundary and next systems v0.1

## Programme claim

The programme is about **flower-colour variation across orthogonal dimensions**, not about *Camellia* as an endpoint.

- `fcp` asks how variation is maintained or spatially sorted **across geographic space**.
- `chun` asks how visible states are repeatedly generated and realised **through evolutionary time**.

The useful visual metaphor is not a population-level polymorphism stretched literally through time. It is a sequence of lineage-level state realizations:

`time -> • white-like -> • red/pink -> • white/yellow -> • red/pink ...`

where each point is an observed lineage/state realization and repeated state classes form natural evolutionary replicates.

Use **flower-colour variation**, **evolutionary-time recurrence**, or **temporal recurrence of flower-colour states** for this axis. Reserve **flower-colour polymorphism** for documented within-population coexistence, because the strict population-genetic term should not be redefined.

## What is not novel

The following ideas already have clear prior art and must not be claimed as conceptual novelties:

1. studying flower-colour transitions on phylogenies or across macroevolutionary time;
2. comparing gains/losses and tempo/mode of pigmentation transitions (Smith & Goldberg 2015, DOI 10.3732/ajb.1500163);
3. asking whether repeated flower-colour changes reuse the same genes/pathways (e.g. Rausher 2008; Wessinger & Rausher 2012; Larter et al. 2018, DOI 10.1093/molbev/msy117);
4. relating extant polymorphism to longer-term transition patterns: Antirrhineae already showed that the most common polymorphism class parallels the most frequent macroevolutionary transition class and explicitly argued that short-term variance can reflect longer-term evolution (Ellis & Field 2016, DOI 10.1093/aob/mcw043);
5. treating flower colour across multiple evolutionary timescales (Kellenberger & Glover 2023, DOI 10.1016/j.cub.2023.01.055).

Therefore the novelty cannot be "we put flower colour on a time axis".

## What `chun` adds

The retained novelty is an **empirical same-system temporal-repeatability audit**:

1. repeated visible state classes are represented as natural evolutionary replicates;
2. heterogeneous published mechanisms are placed in one explicit multivariate A/F/C/P state space without filling missing axes from visible colour;
3. the **same public biological systems** are then remeasured under one frozen, outcome-independent observation rule;
4. the amount of molecular-state recurrence that survives standardized observation is quantified rather than inferred from candidate-study narratives;
5. molecular generation/reuse is then separated from long-term realised phylogenetic structure and from the stricter question of individual historical-event identity.

The distinctive statement is therefore:

> **The temporal recurrence of a visible flower-colour state does not have one fixed degree of mechanistic repeatability: repeatability depends on transition class and molecular level, and long-term realised colour structure is more constrained than molecular accessibility alone predicts.**

This is stronger and safer than claiming a new temporal concept.

## Relation between the two axes

The programme can be written as:

`generation through evolutionary time -> establishment/persistence -> spatial organisation of extant variation`

or as a two-axis decomposition:

| Axis | Question | Unit | Existing project |
|---|---|---|---|
| time | how often and by what molecular routes does a colour state reappear? | lineage / transition class | `chun` |
| space | once variation exists, is it locally maintained or geographically sorted? | population / species | `fcp` |

The next programme step should not merely repeat `chun` in another genus. It should connect the axes by asking whether **present-day polymorphic states are disproportionately located near, or mechanistically resemble, the transition routes repeatedly used over macroevolutionary time**.

## Next-system screen

### Rank 1 — Antirrhineae (`Antirrhinum` + relatives)

**Why it is the best conceptual next target**

- 369 taxa were surveyed for anthocyanin/yellow pigmentation and 169 placed on a phylogeny in Ellis & Field 2016.
- 23–24 taxa were described as colour-polymorphic.
- Long-term transitions between anthocyanin and yellow are inferred to pass mainly through a white intermediate; double-pigmented states are rare.
- Crucially, white/anthocyanin is both the most frequent polymorphism class and the most frequent long-term transition class.
- `Antirrhinum` has unusually deep functional-genetic knowledge of colour loci, hybrid zones and selection, plus newer phylogenomic/RAD resources.

**New question beyond the 2016 paper**

> Are extant polymorphic taxa merely frequency snapshots, or do their molecular pigment states occupy the same transition corridors repeatedly used through deep time?

This directly links `fcp` and `chun`:

`current within-species polymorphism -> molecular state -> macro transition corridor`.

**Main risk**

The macro pattern is already known. Novelty requires adding a modern molecular-state layer across multiple polymorphic/monomorphic contrasts, not re-running ancestral-state reconstruction.

**Gate before committing**

Audit whether at least 3 independent colour-transition/polymorphism systems have public raw floral RNA-seq or comparable expression/metabolite data that can be re-expressed in a common anthocyanin/aurone/flavonol state space.

### Rank 2 — Mimulus / Erythranthe

**Why it is powerful**

- Classic flower-colour/pollinator genetics connects phenotype, genes and fitness.
- A 2025 Nature Communications study found two independently arisen yellow morphs within red hummingbird-pollinated species and measured pigments, transcriptomic/genomic variation and bumblebee response in parallel.
- This gives unusually strong **incipient transition** material: the system contains present-day morphs that may represent early points on an evolutionary trajectory.
- Modern phylogenomics has also shown that earlier reconstructions of repeated hummingbird-syndrome origins were partly wrong because of introgression/gene-tree discordance, making it an excellent test bed for `chun`'s event-identifiability philosophy.

**Best question**

> Do repeated within-species colour shifts follow the same molecular trajectory as fixed interspecific colour transitions, or does the route change as a variant moves from polymorphism toward lineage fixation?

**Main risk**

The focal section is small and already intensively studied; the value is micro-to-macro linkage, not broad taxonomic replication.

### Rank 3 — Iochrominae

**Why it is a strong benchmark**

- Around 40 species with broad colour diversity.
- Repeated anthocyanin shifts, at least one regain after loss, and pathway-wide expression/pigment work already exist.
- Larter et al. 2018 directly tested pathway-level regulatory convergence across 28 species.

**Use**

Excellent external validation of the `chun` framework: does a common multivariate-state reanalysis reproduce their strong pathway-level convergence, and at what hierarchy does recurrence break down?

**Main risk**

Because pathway-level convergence is already the central published result, a new paper would have limited conceptual novelty unless it adds present-day polymorphism, ecological filtering or a genuinely independent observation-regime test.

### Rank 4 — Penstemon

**Why it is biologically spectacular**

- Red/hummingbird-associated flowers have evolved repeatedly; prior work reports repeated but distinct loss-of-function mutations in F3'5'h across many red lineages.
- Recent phylogenomics resolves repeated hummingbird-syndrome origins and asks whether repeated adaptation comes from de novo mutation, standing variation or introgression.

**Why it is not Rank 1 now**

This space is currently very active and crowded. A 2026 preprint/paper-level analysis reports four independent hummingbird-syndrome origins with rapid multivariate convergence. A simple repeatability paper would collide directly with ongoing work.

**Use**

Best as a positive-control system for highly predictable gene-level repeatability, or later for testing whether repeatability differs between flower colour and other floral modules.

## Recommended next action

Do **not** start another full genus project immediately.

Run a finite admission screen for Antirrhineae first:

1. list the 23–24 reported polymorphic taxa and their colour combinations;
2. map them onto the current Antirrhinum/Antirrhineae phylogenetic resources;
3. search SRA/ENA/GEO for floral RNA-seq or metabolomic contrasts in at least three independent colour systems;
4. determine whether a common state space can include anthocyanin + aurone/yellow + flavonol branches without outcome-dependent candidate selection;
5. proceed only if at least three independent systems are auditable.

If that admission gate fails, move to **Mimulus/Erythranthe** as the strongest micro-to-macro bridge rather than forcing a weak Antirrhineae molecular dataset.

## Bottom line

The programme-level position is coherent, but should be phrased carefully:

> **`fcp` studies the spatial organisation and maintenance of extant flower-colour variation; `chun` studies the repeated generation and realisation of flower-colour states through evolutionary time. The novelty of `chun` is not the time axis itself, but a matched same-system test of how much molecular-state repeatability survives standardized observation, followed by an explicit separation of generation, persistence and historical event identity.**
