# Fan 2026 species-scale Camellia thermal result v0.1

## Question

Does the macroevolutionary distribution of *Camellia* flower-colour states support a simple model in which anthocyanin-like red/pink pigmentation enabled movement into colder climatic niches?

This document explicitly separates **published input facts** from **results generated by `chun`**.

## 1. Published inputs — not chun discoveries

Fan et al. 2026 (`10.1111/pbi.70442`) provide an open-access Data S1 with accession-level fields including genus, section, taxon name, petal colour, country and geographic area. Their paper also reconstructs white as the most likely ancestral visible flower colour on their 237-accession nuclear SNP phylogeny and describes yellow taxa as geographically concentrated around southwestern China/northern Vietnam.

Those are source-level conclusions/observations.

The source Data S1 was recovered through the PMC Open Access package and independently normalized by `chun`; no flower-colour or geography value below is inferred from GBIF imagery.

## 2. chun taxon normalization result

After removing sample suffixes, expanding `C.` to *Camellia*, collapsing infraspecific/sample rows only when their source colour states agree, and excluding ambiguous/polymorphic species, the deterministic species table contains:

- **106 species**;
- white (`W`) = **56**;
- anthocyanin-like red/pink/purple-red (`A`) = **28**;
- yellow (`Y`) = **22**.

*C. pitardii* is excluded from deterministic species coding because red-family and alba/white source rows coexist.

## 3. chun macro-geography result

Using the Data S1 source-area labels, define the southern putative ancestral belt as South China + Southwest China + Vietnam.

Species-level confinement is:

- `Y`: **22/22** southern-belt only;
- `A`: **21/28** southern-belt only, 7 with source areas outside;
- `W`: **43/56** southern-belt only, 13 with source areas outside.

Tests frozen in `data/fan2026_colour_geography_tests_v0_1.csv`:

- Y vs non-Y southern confinement: one-sided Fisher **P = 0.00552049**;
- A vs W southern confinement: odds ratio ≈ **0.907**, two-sided Fisher **P = 1.0**.

### Result

> **The geographically distinctive state is yellow. Red/pink and white have indistinguishable south-versus-outside proportions in the source sampling.**

This is a `chun` quantitative result. It does not support the idea that acquisition of a visible anthocyanin-like state uniquely enabled geographic expansion beyond the southern ancestral region.

## 4. chun GBIF × CHELSA thermal analysis

The 106-species deterministic colour table was joined to public GBIF occurrences and CHELSA v2.1 BIO1/BIO6.

Admission rules:

- GBIF taxon match must resolve at species rank;
- source-provenance country filter from Data S1;
- occurrence must be present with coordinates and no GBIF geospatial issue;
- explicit cultivated/non-native and high-coordinate-uncertainty records removed;
- 0.1° spatial thinning;
- maximum 80 thinned points/species;
- at least five climate-bearing points/species.

After these gates, **51 species** were admitted:

- `A` = **14**;
- `W` = **35**;
- `Y` = **2**.

The low Y admission rate is itself important: rare/small-range southern yellow species are poorly represented in GBIF, so direct thermal inference for Y remains weak.

## 5. Main result: A and W thermal niches are effectively indistinguishable

### Mean annual temperature — BIO1 species median

- A mean = **18.807 °C**
- W mean = **18.667 °C**
- A − W = **+0.140 °C**
- fixed-seed 100,000-label permutation, two-sided **P = 0.83056**

### Minimum temperature of coldest month — BIO6 species median

- A mean = **4.225 °C**
- W mean = **4.780 °C**
- A − W = **−0.555 °C**
- two-sided **P = 0.52740**

### Cold-tail — species BIO6 5th percentile

- A mean = **−2.864 °C**
- W mean = **−2.276 °C**
- A − W = **−0.588 °C**
- two-sided **P = 0.71787**

### Thermal breadth — BIO1 IQR

- A mean = **2.807 °C**
- W mean = **3.032 °C**
- A − W = **−0.225 °C**
- two-sided **P = 0.55572**

### Conclusion

> **Within the admitted species sample, anthocyanin-like red/pink (`A`) species are not detectably colder, broader-niched, or more cold-tolerant than white (`W`) species.**

This directly rejects the strong macro proposition that visible transition from white to red/pink generally opened a colder thermal niche in *Camellia*.

## 6. Coarse taxonomic-block sensitivity

Traditional sections are not reliable phylogenetic substitutes in *Camellia*, but they provide a conservative coarse block for the few sections containing both A and W states.

Two shared sections contribute A–W contrasts. For BIO1 median, BIO6 median and BIO6 cold tail, the direction is split one-versus-one across sections; exact sign **P = 1.0** for all three temperature-position metrics.

Thus the A≈W result is not reversed by this coarse within-section sensitivity.

This is **not** a nuclear phylogenetic correction; BM/OU/state-dependent inference remains gated on a machine-readable nuclear tree.

## 7. Yellow result: warm central niches, but only two admitted species

The admitted yellow species are *C. euphlebia* and *C. nitidissima*, both Sect. *Chrysantha* and South China.

For BIO6 median:

- A vs Y: difference = **−4.875 °C**, exact two-sided **P = 0.025**;
- W vs Y: difference = **−4.320 °C**, exact two-sided **P = 0.0390**.

For BIO1 median:

- A vs Y: **P = 0.075**;
- W vs Y: **P = 0.0826**.

Cold-tail differences are not significant (A vs Y P=0.60; W vs Y P=0.703).

### Interpretation

These results are consistent with the southern warm-centred distribution of yellow taxa, but they do **not** identify a yellow physiological optimum because:

1. only two Y species pass the occurrence gate;
2. both belong to the same traditional section;
3. both are South-China taxa;
4. range restriction and sampling scarcity strongly confound thermal estimation.

Combined with the geography analysis, the robust current statement is therefore **historical/geographic restriction of yellow**, not a demonstrated yellow thermal-adaptation regime.

## 8. Integration with the close-pair counterexample

A separate taxon-audited analysis of red *C. japonica* and red *C. rusticana* found that *C. rusticana* occupies substantially colder climates:

- BIO1 median: −3.60 °C relative to *C. japonica*;
- BIO6 median: −4.55 °C;
- BIO6 q05: −6.05 °C.

Published trait work nevertheless reports *C. rusticana* petals as slightly lighter rather than redder.

Together with the 51-species A≈W result, this makes a monotonic `more floral redness -> colder niche` model untenable as a general explanation.

## 9. What the result says about the micro-to-macro programme

The micro meta-analysis shows highly recurrent pigment regulation/flux accessibility:

- regulatory/flux evidence: 8/8 independent clusters;
- red/pink direction has more anthocyanin: 6/6 informative clusters.

The macro result is different:

- A and W thermal niches are indistinguishable;
- yellow is strongly geographically constrained;
- a colder red species does not need to become visibly redder.

Therefore the micro result does **not** propagate into a universal macro climatic advantage.

### New problem generated by chun

> **Why are some highly accessible pigment states repeatedly generated, yet only conditionally associated with environmental niche shifts and long-term geographic persistence?**

This is now stronger than the original question “is anthocyanin a cold adaptation?”

## 10. Current primary hypothesis

### Accessibility × ecological filtering

Pigment-network architecture determines which phenotypes are easy to generate. Historical geography, climate/light regime, pollinator sensory environment and other whole-plant traits determine which accessible phenotypes persist and spread.

`molecular accessibility -> recurrent phenotype generation -> ecological filtering -> persistence -> macro pattern`

### Consequence for anthocyanin

Anthocyanin remains a plausible **conditional** abiotic/pollinator trait in particular lineages, but the current macro data reject it as a universal cold-niche enabling innovation in *Camellia*.

## 11. BM / OU / EB / BBM status

The current 51-species climate sample is large enough for comparative models **only if** a defensible machine-readable nuclear tree with branch lengths can be joined.

Current tree evidence:

- Fan 2026 provides the broad colour phylogeny as figures but not public Newick/raw SNPs;
- Wu 2022 `PRJNA665925` provides a fully public 405-low-copy-gene sequence backbone, with ~45 stable Fan-colour species overlapping after normalization, but a machine-readable species-tree file has not yet been recovered;
- an OA-package audit of Cheng 2022 found only an XLSX sampling/data table in the nested supplementary ZIP, not a Newick/treefile.

Therefore BM/OU/EB/BBM fitting is currently blocked by **tree-file provenance**, not by the trait/climate sample size.

Forcing a digitized figure or traditional section topology into OU/BM would manufacture precision and is not admitted.

## 12. Claim boundary

### Supported by chun preliminary analysis

> **Visible anthocyanin-like red/pink and white flower states do not differ detectably in species-level thermal niche across the current 51-species GBIF×CHELSA sample. Yellow is strongly geographically restricted to the southern ancestral belt, while A and W show similar geographic expansion. The micro-level repeatability of pigment regulation therefore does not translate into a universal macro cold-adaptation signal.**

### Not yet supported

- exact colour-state-dependent BM/OU parameters;
- pigment-first versus niche-first branch ordering;
- a causal climatic function of floral anthocyanin in any particular macro transition;
- a yellow-specific thermal optimum independent of clade/geography;
- a claim that climate rather than pollinators/history explains persistence of a colour state.

The remaining decisive macro task is nuclear-tree admission/reconstruction, followed by event-centred and state-dependent modelling rather than another present-day colour–temperature correlation.
