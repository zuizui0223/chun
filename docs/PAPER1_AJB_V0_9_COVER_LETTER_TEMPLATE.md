Please consider our Research Article, **“Repeated generation of flower-colour states does not replay one pigment-state programme in *Camellia*,”** for publication in the *American Journal of Botany*.

### What questions are addressed?

Flower-colour variation is repeatedly generated through evolutionary time, but repeated visible change does not by itself show how much of the underlying molecular transition has been replayed. We ask whether independent *Camellia* colour transitions repeat one complete pigment-network programme or reuse only selected modules, and whether the realised macroevolutionary colour pattern remains identifiable when individual historical transition events are not.

To answer this, we represent molecular change on the same four prespecified transcript-state axes—anthocyanin, flavonol, carotenoid, and proanthocyanidin diversion (A/F/C/P)—across independent anthocyanin-gain and yellow-development systems. We first bound recurrence in the partially observed published literature and then remeasured five auditable public RNA-seq systems with one outcome-independent pathway-wide protocol. Separately, we tested accepted-species wild-colour structure across nuclear-topology and trait-coding sensitivities and imposed a robustness gate before assigning historical branch-level causes.

### What is the major contribution?

The study shows that repeated visible flower-colour generation does **not** replay one invariant complete A/F/C/P programme. For anthocyanin gain, standardized remeasurement fixes exact whole-signature recurrence at one third and limits pairwise A/F/C/P concordance to 0.333–0.5. Yellow development shows stronger modular repeatability: the two trajectories share A-down, C-up, and P-down directions while differing at F. Thus the result is neither complete mechanistic convergence nor absence of recurrence; repeatability is **modular and transition-class dependent**.

The macroevolutionary result establishes a complementary boundary. Wild flower colours remain locally phylogenetically structured across independent nuclear topologies and alternative wild-colour codings, but no accepted-species transition branch survives the strict-versus-dominant coding robustness gate. The study therefore separates **generation**, **establishment/persistence**, and **historical event identity** rather than assuming that a recurrent phenotype, a recurrent molecular programme, and an identifiable causal branch are the same quantity.

### Why is this of broad interest?

The work addresses a general problem in evolutionary biology: how repeatable is phenotypic evolution, and at what biological level should “repeatability” be measured? Flower colour provides a tractable system in which similar visible states can mask distinct combinations of pathway modules. *Camellia* is especially informative because repeated colour states, diverse molecular implementations, dense phylogenomic resources, and contrasting pollination contexts coexist within one genus. The framework should therefore interest readers working on convergent evolution, evolutionary predictability, plant–pollinator interactions, molecular evolution, comparative phylogenetics, and the connection between microevolutionary mechanism and macroevolutionary pattern.

The manuscript does **not** claim that alternative flower-colour mechanisms, pathway convergence, measurement-process dependence, or ancestral-state uncertainty are themselves new concepts. Its contribution is the matched empirical test of molecular replay in a common multivariate state space and the explicit separation of that result from macroevolutionary pattern and event identity.
