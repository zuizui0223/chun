# Paper 1 title and abstract — AJB draft v0.1

## Primary title

**Flexible molecular routes, locally conserved flower colours: a cross-scale analysis of *Camellia***

## Alternative titles

1. **Molecular accessibility and local phylogenetic conservatism of flower colour in *Camellia***
2. **Why accessible flower colours are not evolutionarily labile: a cross-scale analysis of *Camellia***
3. **Flexible pigment pathways, constrained evolutionary outcomes in *Camellia* flower colour**

The primary title is preferred because it states the two demonstrated results without implying a causal mechanism that current public data cannot identify.

## Structured abstract

### Premise

Flower colour is shaped by pigment pathways that can be altered through multiple regulatory and enzymatic routes, but molecular accessibility need not translate into macroevolutionary lability. We asked whether recurrent *Camellia* colour variation is implemented by the same molecular nodes and whether wild visible flower colours remain phylogenetically constrained after accounting for taxonomic inflation, within-species colour variation, and nuclear-topology uncertainty.

### Methods

We combined a sequence-aware synthesis of public flower-colour mechanism studies with an independent nuclear phylogenetic framework. FLS, DFR, ANS/LDOX, and ANR evidence was resolved to paralog or family level where public sequences permitted. A 339-locus Angiosperms353 backbone was normalized to the World Flora Online Plant List 2026-06 accepted-species taxonomy. Species-level colour labels were then audited against wild/floristic descriptions to construct a strict 24-species seed and a dominant-colour 30-species sensitivity seed. We tested colour phylogenetic structure with count-preserving permutations on accepted-species topologies derived independently from FastTree and IQ-TREE/UFBoot gene-tree ensembles. Climate and pollination literature were used only as screening evidence for alternative ecological explanations.

### Key Results

Molecular recurrence was not equivalent to repeated use of one exact gene: FLS included a strongly resolved same-lineage recurrence, whereas independent DFR evidence clusters used different paralog subclasses. The 93 legacy *Camellia* nuclear tips collapsed to 55 accepted species, and wild-colour auditing demoted 11 of 35 provisional hard colour states. The two nuclear pipelines were highly concordant on 53 shared accepted species (46/50 nontrivial splits; normalized Robinson–Foulds distance = 0.08). Across both trait scenarios and both topology pipelines, labelled species had a nearer same-colour relative than expected by chance; on the UFBoot topology, nearest-same-colour permutation tests gave *P* = 0.00116 for the strict seed and *P* = 0.000080 for the dominant-colour sensitivity. In contrast, broad same-colour mean pairwise-distance clustering was topology-sensitive. No specific accepted-species colour-transition branch was robust to both strict and dominant wild-colour assumptions.

### Conclusions

*Camellia* flower colour shows a cross-scale mismatch: pigment states can be reached through flexible molecular implementations, yet wild visible colour retains local phylogenetic conservatism. Current public species-level data identify this macroevolutionary pattern but not robust transition events to which climate, pollination, or molecular causes can be assigned. Resolving why accessible floral states persist will require population-resolved colour and spectral phenotypes, effective pollination measurements, reproductive fitness, flowering-window environments, and paralog-specific expression.

- Does **not** claim a definitive white ancestor.
- Does **not** claim universal cold adaptation or pollinator causation.
- Does **not** promote the legacy three W→A branches.
