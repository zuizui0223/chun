# Paper 1 evolutionary-event boundary amendment — v0.1

## Status

This amendment tightens the inferential target of Paper 1 without changing any scientific estimate, contrast, figure input, or robustness gate.

## Core correction

The standardized RNA-seq contrasts are **not direct observations of independent macroevolutionary branch events**.

They quantify how prespecified pigment-network transcript modules change when a visible colour state is generated within each admitted public biological system. The admitted contrasts include same-cultivar petal-sector comparisons and ordered developmental trajectories. These are appropriate for measuring molecular state generation, but they must not be described as direct measurements of the historical branch transitions reconstructed in the macroevolutionary analysis.

The paper therefore separates two inferential layers:

1. **Molecular generation layer** — how repeatable is the A/F/C/P transcript-state change associated with generating a colour state under one standardized observation protocol?
2. **Macroevolutionary realization layer** — how are realised wild-colour states distributed and retained across the accepted-species phylogeny, and are individual historical transition events robustly identifiable?

The connection between these layers is a cross-scale comparison, not a claim that each RNA-seq contrast is itself an independently observed evolutionary event.

## Revised biological headline

Preferred formulation:

> Flower-colour repeatability in *Camellia* is hierarchical. Standardized molecular contrasts show transition-class-dependent modular reuse rather than one invariant complete A/F/C/P programme, while realised wild colours remain locally phylogenetically structured even though individual historical transition events are not robustly identified.

Avoid using `evolutionary replay` as shorthand for the RNA-seq contrasts unless the sentence explicitly states that replay is being evaluated at the level of a standardized molecular state representation rather than directly observed branch events.

## Candidate-free terminology

On first use, define `candidate-free` as:

> **annotation-driven, outcome-independent quantification within prespecified pigment modules**

This makes the scope explicit. The analysis is not genome-wide hypothesis-free: A/F/C/P are prespecified biological modules. It is candidate-free in the narrower operational sense that genes/paralogs are admitted from annotation rules rather than selected because their observed effects fit an expected colour mechanism, and retention is not determined by significance or expected direction.

After this definition, `candidate-free` may be retained as shorthand.

## Repeatability hierarchy

The biological result should be organized by level rather than as a binary replay/non-replay claim:

- complete A/F/C/P package — not invariantly repeated;
- module-level directions — partially repeated;
- transition class — repeatability differs between anthocyanin gain and yellow development;
- realised visible colour — locally phylogenetically structured;
- exact historical event identity — fails the strict-versus-dominant robustness gate.

This hierarchy is more faithful to the authoritative results than a generic `replay versus no replay` framing.

## Manuscript-facing replacement language

### Abstract / premise

Use:

> Similar flower-colour states can be generated through multiple molecular routes. We asked how repeatable the underlying pigment-network state change is when public systems are remeasured under one standardized observation protocol, and separately whether realised wild-colour states and individual historical transition events show the same robustness at macroevolutionary scale.

### Methods

Use:

> The standardized molecular arm used annotation-driven, outcome-independent quantification within four prespecified pigment modules (A/F/C/P). These contrasts measure colour-state-generating transcript changes within admitted systems; they are not treated as direct observations of macroevolutionary branch events.

### Discussion

Use:

> The molecular and macro analyses address different quantities. RNA-seq contrasts identify repeatability of colour-state-generating transcript changes under a common measurement rule, whereas the phylogenetic analysis asks whether realised wild-colour structure and individual historical branch events are robustly identifiable. Their juxtaposition is therefore cross-scale, not event-for-event matching.

## Claim ceiling added by this amendment

Do not write or imply:

- that a same-cultivar petal-sector contrast is an independent evolutionary origin;
- that a developmental colour trajectory is itself a macroevolutionary transition;
- that candidate-free means genome-wide hypothesis-free;
- that low whole-package recurrence means molecular repeatability is absent;
- that molecular accessibility directly predicts branch-level evolutionary lability.

## Science status

No result is reopened. The authoritative numerical conclusions remain Paper 1 science v0.2.2.
