# Second complete-anchor screen — v0.1

## Purpose

This screen asks whether any currently recovered non-Camellia clade can independently reproduce the full empirical architecture desired for a second anchor:

`white-like ancestral baseline + >=3 robust historical colour events + >=3 matched molecular contrasts spanning those events`.

Current polymorphism and ecological evidence are recorded but are not required for the minimum complete-anchor definition.

## Verdict

**No candidate currently passes.**

This is a finite negative result, not a reason to relax the criteria.

## Closest candidates and why they fail

### Epimedium

Strong molecular common-panel evidence exists, but event identity among the four A− endpoint taxa is not robust across nuclear, plastid and species-delimitation evidence. It is retained as mechanistic validation, not event-matched recurrence.

### Antirrhineae

The macroevolutionary side is excellent: hundreds of taxa, repeated transitions and a direct link between present polymorphism and long-term transition rates. The molecular gate fails because auditable direct colour RNA-seq is concentrated in one *Antirrhinum majus* Rosea/Eluta regulatory background.

### Petunia long-tube clade

This is the strongest regain/latent-capacity system. Two independent regains from a colourless ancestor are mechanistically resolved, but there are only two events and the white/colorless state is derived within a genus whose deeper ancestral state is pigmented.

### North American Silene / Physolychnis

Repeated red gains are well supported, including gains from white ancestors, but the available molecular flower-colour datasets are not matched across at least three of those specific red-gain events. *Silene littorea* supplies a strong present-day white/pink polymorphism RNA-seq/biochemistry bridge, but not the missing event-matched set.

### Linoideae

The dated macro history from a yellow-white ancestral reconstruction is strong, but the terminal trait matrix must first be rebuilt and the molecular colour layer is currently too uneven for three event-matched contrasts.

### Hydrangea sect. Cornidia

White ancestry and repeated transitions are strong. The published stochastic-map QC is especially useful because returns to white exceed gains from white, but a wild, event-matched molecular colour layer is not available at the required depth.

### Angraecinae

Repeated white-green transitions provide a useful low-complexity macro control, but the pigment/mechanistic layer is too sparse.

### Polygonatum sect. Verticillata

Promising for colour diversity and current colour phenotypes, but event identity and the flower-colour molecular mechanism are not yet sufficiently resolved.

### Jasminum

A white ancestor with derived yellow flowers is reported in an Indian phylogeny, but the system does not currently supply the repeated event/molecular replication required for the programme.

### Nicotiana

Some sectional ancestral nodes are reconstructed as white and spectral transitions are abundant, but hybridization/polyploidy and equivocal deeper nodes prevent a clean second-anchor event design.

## Wrong-baseline controls retained

### Linanthus

Recent complete sampling supports flower-colour polymorphism itself as likely ancestral and labile. This supersedes older preliminary white-root framing. It is valuable for current-polymorphism evolution, not as a white-baseline anchor.

### Cape Erica

The common molecular panel is exceptionally useful, but the plesiomorphic state is pink rather than white-like. It remains a mechanistic heterogeneity control.

## Consequence for programme design

The search justifies the evidence-tier architecture:

- use white-like clades for macro transition architecture;
- use strong event-matched coloured-ancestor clades as molecular recurrence controls;
- use Petunia for regain/latent-capacity tests;
- use polymorphic systems for the present-to-past bridge;
- use ecological systems for persistence/filtering.

The absence of a second all-in-one system is itself informative about data availability. It also prevents one convenient clade from being overinterpreted as a complete replication of Camellia.

## Reopen rule

Do not rerun this broad screen unless at least one of the following occurs:

1. a new multi-species floral transcriptome/metabolome study appears in a white-like ancestral clade;
2. raw colour-molecular data become public for enough independent Antirrhineae/Silene events to meet the gate;
3. a robust new phylogeny changes event identity in Epimedium or another held candidate;
4. a newly described clade clearly satisfies the white-like baseline and >=3 event criteria.

Otherwise continue the tiered atlas and original terminal-trait reconstruction rather than repeating discovery searches.
