# Ecological-driver meta-analysis v1 — quantitative result

## Question

After molecular accessibility and local macroevolutionary colour conservatism were established, which ecological processes have quantitative support in *Camellia*?

This analysis deliberately separates outcome families. It does **not** combine pollinator exclusion, pollen supplementation, seasonal nectar, sensory choice, and pigment manipulation into one omnibus effect.

## A. Pollinator-service contribution to fruit set

Three independent bird-access/exclusion systems can be expressed on the same fruit-set log response-ratio scale:

| taxon | visible state | comparison | RR (full/open access ÷ bird excluded) |
|---|---|---|---:|
| *C. japonica* | A | 36.2% vs 5.7% | 6.35 |
| *C. petelotii* | Y | bird-excluded fruit set reduced 67.06% | 3.04 |
| *C. oleifera* | W | bird-excluded fruit set reduced 56.28% | 2.29 |

All three effects point in the same direction. The equal-weight mean lnRR is 1.262, corresponding to a geometric-mean RR of **3.53**. Leaving out one study at a time keeps the geometric-mean RR between **2.64 and 4.39**.

This is a quantitative magnitude synthesis, not yet a conventional inverse-variance random-effects meta-analysis, because defensible sampling variances are not available for all three primary effects. It is nevertheless important that the three systems span coarse A, Y and W states. The current data therefore do not support the idea that large bird-pollination benefits are specific to red/A flowers.

## B. Pollen limitation and pollinator reliability

*C. pubipetala* provides an exact count-based pollen-supplementation contrast:

- open fruit set = 4/60 = 6.7%;
- supplemental fruit set = 7/30 = 23.3%;
- RR = **3.50**;
- lnRR = 1.253;
- reconstructed SE(lnRR) = 0.586;
- approximate 95% RR interval = **1.11–11.03**.

By contrast, *C. petelotii* showed no detectable open-versus-supplemental pollen difference in the source study (fruit χ² = 0.01, P = 0.91; seed χ² = 0.07, P = 0.79), despite a large bird-exclusion effect. Thus pollinator-group contribution and pollen limitation are related but not interchangeable quantities.

The regional *C. oleifera* study of Xie et al. (2013) adds a second form of quantitative reliability evidence. Across eight forests, pollen limitation declined with the visit density of the legitimate solitary bee *Andrena camellia*:

`PL = 0.48 - 0.007 × VD`, F = 20.05, df = 6, P = 0.004.

Natural-pollinated stigmas received a median of 9 pollen grains in the primary region but 0 in the plateau region. This is direct evidence that pollinator availability can generate large spatial differences in realized reproductive service.

## C. Climate / flowering-window mediation of pollination

The ecological evidence is more coherent for climate acting through phenology and pollinator service than for a universal direct climate-to-visible-colour pathway.

### *C. hainanica* northward transplantation

At the cooler transplanted site:

- flowering was delayed by **45 days**;
- mean temperature during anthesis was 7.89°C versus 24.63°C at the native site;
- peak pollinator visitation declined by **92%** (RR ≈ 0.08);
- pollen deposition declined by **57%** (RR ≈ 0.43);
- natural fruit set collapsed to 0%, while artificial cross-pollination at the transplanted site achieved 11% fruit set.

This is a quasi-experiment rather than a flower-colour experiment, but it directly supports the causal chain `environment / flowering window -> pollination service -> reproduction`.

### *C. perpetua* seasonal contrast

Winter versus summer:

- nectar volume = 453.66 versus 129.14 μL, RR ≈ **3.51**;
- sucrose:hexose ratio = 49.69 versus 6.99, RR ≈ **7.11**;
- the primary study reports stronger bird visitation and reproductive contribution in winter.

### *C. petelotii*

Honeybee visitation decreases in cloudy/rainy weather while bird access contributes strongly to reproduction.

Together these systems elevate the hypothesis that flowering-window climate affects floral-state fitness indirectly by changing pollinator reliability, visitor composition and reward economics.

## D. Direct abiotic effects on floral pigment deployment

The current *Camellia* floral-manipulation seed is much thinner.

In *C. japonica* 'Nuccio's Pearl', 7°C dark storage gave petal anthocyanin values:

- control: 0.240 μmol g⁻¹ FW;
- 6 weeks: 0.195 (RR = 0.813; lnRR = -0.208);
- 8 weeks: 0.174 (RR = 0.725; lnRR = -0.322).

However, temperature and darkness are confounded. This demonstrates environmental plasticity of petal pigment deployment, not a clean cold-adaptation effect. A formal direct-abiotic floral meta-analysis requires additional petal-specific experiments with separable temperature/light/water treatments.

## E. Visible hue aliases sensory/pollinator function

A paired same-visible-red experiment provides a large sensory collision:

- *C. rusticana*: 25.8 ± 4.3 bumblebee visits per 10 min;
- *C. japonica*: 1.1 ± 1.1;
- raw mean ratio ≈ **23.45** (lnRR ≈ 3.16).

The pair also differs in UV reflectance and floral fluorescence. Thus visible red is demonstrably not a unique pollinator-functional state.

## Current ecological conclusion

The quantitative evidence currently has an asymmetry:

1. **Reproductive-service / reliability effects are large and repeat across independent *Camellia* systems.** Bird access increases fruit set by roughly 2.3–6.4× in three independent species spanning A, Y and W visible states; pollinator scarcity predicts pollen limitation within *C. oleifera*; and a pollen-supplementation experiment in *C. pubipetala* produces a 3.5× fruit-set increase.
2. **Climate/season can strongly restructure pollination conditions**, through flowering phenology, visitor activity, reward and effective pollen delivery.
3. **Direct abiotic control of petal pigment is currently under-replicated and experimentally confounded** within *Camellia*.
4. **Visible hue is too coarse to act as a deterministic ecological-state variable.**

The current best-supported ultimate-driver model is therefore not `red -> cold adaptation` or `red -> bird syndrome`. It is a conditional filtering model:

`molecular accessibility -> latent pigment/sensory/reward state -> flowering-window environment + pollinator-service filtering -> reproductive success -> evolutionary persistence`.

This model is supported at the mechanism/service level but not yet demonstrated as the cause of accepted-species macro flower-colour transitions.
