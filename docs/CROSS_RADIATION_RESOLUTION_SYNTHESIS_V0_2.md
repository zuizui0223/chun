# Cross-radiation resolution-dependent flower-colour predictability — v0.2 after prospective Iris falsification

## Main decision

The programme no longer supports a universal `coarse < intermediate > fine` ordering across radiations.

The stronger evidence-backed statement is:

> **The biological resolution at which flower-colour evolution is most predictable is itself radiation dependent.**

This is not inferred from retrospective heterogeneity alone. A preregistered fourth-radiation test in *Iris* prospectively falsified the frozen intermediate-resolution ordering.

## Prospective falsification

The frozen Iris analysis used one common eligible-tip frame for coarse, intermediate and fine state representations, removed fine states with fewer than five eligible tips before all three analyses, and used 9,999 joint taxon permutations with seed 20260913. The trait-blind six-locus tree was frozen before outcome exposure.

Primary frame: 169 eligible tips.

- AUC coarse = 0.4617285166
- AUC intermediate = 0.4543945669
- AUC fine = 0.4870504419
- intermediate − coarse = -0.0073339497
- intermediate − fine = -0.0326558750
- intermediate signal p = 0.9718
- fine > intermediate fail-side p = 0.0486

Terminal preregistered decision: **FAIL**.

The intermediate representation therefore was not merely non-superior: its AUC was below 0.5, and the fine representation exceeded it on the preregistered fail-side contrast.

No sensitivity analysis may upgrade this result.

## What survives the falsification

The Iris result rejects a universal resolution optimum, but it does not erase the earlier cross-radiation structure.

### 1. Representation dependence remains real

Across previously assembled radiations, coarse recodings, fine states, biochemical state classes and mechanistic modules do not carry equal information. The earlier synthesis already showed that conclusions change with biological representation.

The prospective Iris FAIL strengthens this point by showing that **the winning representation is not fixed across radiations**.

### 2. Fine-state organization remains recurrent but not universally dominant

Linoideae, Angraecinae and Antirrhineae supported fine-state phylogenetic organization while failing to support one shared privileged coarse boundary or universal direction.

Iris is consistent with rejecting the intermediate optimum and is directionally most favorable to the fine representation among the three tested levels, but all three Iris AUCs are below 0.5. Therefore Iris is not evidence that fine resolution is universally predictive either.

### 3. Molecular phenotype-axis localization remains axis specific

The molecular comparison is separate from the Iris macro representation test.

- hue / hydroxylation localization: supported in Iochrominae, Cape Erica and the preregistered Petunieae hue component;
- pigment amount / depletion localization: substantially less stable across those systems.

This asymmetry remains useful, but it cannot be collapsed into one universal intermediate-resolution rule after the Iris falsification.

### 4. Mechanistic recurrence remains hierarchical

Across the admitted mechanistic systems, repeatability occurs at different levels: exact regulatory reuse, pathway-module reuse, regulator-class recurrence, recurrent cores with heterogeneous implementation, or heterogeneous causal nodes.

The strongest defensible synthesis is therefore not that evolution repeats at one privileged scale, but that **repeatability has a system-specific resolution profile**.

## Revised integrated candidate

Old candidate to retire as a general rule:

> Flower-colour predictability peaks at an intermediate biological resolution across radiations.

Revised candidate:

> **Independent flower-colour radiations differ in their resolution profiles of evolutionary predictability: the representation that preserves phylogenetic, molecular or mechanistic signal is system dependent, while very coarse universal boundaries and complete programme identity are especially unstable across systems.**

The key novelty candidate is now the empirical mapping of **between-radiation variation in the resolution profile**, not the generic claim that character coding matters.

## What the prospective test contributes

Before Iris, the main weakness was that the cross-radiation resolution pattern could be read as a retrospective synthesis assembled after seeing heterogeneous outcomes.

After Iris, the paper can explicitly separate:

1. **retrospective discovery** — multiple radiations suggested that biological resolution matters;
2. **frozen prediction** — these observations were compressed into a specific intermediate-resolution ordering;
3. **prospective refutation** — the held-out Iris radiation rejected that ordering;
4. **revised generalization** — resolution dependence is real, but the optimum is radiation contingent rather than universal.

This is stronger evidence for the boundary of the generalization than quietly adding another retrospective radiation.

## New main question

The next falsifiable question is no longer:

> Does intermediate resolution win again?

It is:

> **What observable properties of a radiation predict which biological resolution will retain evolutionary signal?**

Candidate moderators must be defined before opening further held-out outcomes. Plausible classes include:

- state-frequency imbalance / entropy;
- number of biologically meaningful states;
- pathway architecture and whether visible states collapse multiple biochemical routes;
- phylogenetic depth and branch-length dispersion;
- transition density / lability;
- reticulation or hybridization burden;
- whether the phenotype axis corresponds closely to one biochemical branch point versus distributed pathway output.

These are hypotheses for a new moderator model, not post-hoc explanations of Iris.

## Manuscript architecture after Iris

Working title:

> **The resolution of evolutionary predictability varies among flower-colour radiations**

Alternative:

> **Flower-colour radiations differ in the biological scale at which evolution is predictable**

Main figures:

1. **Discovery atlas** — retrospective evidence across macro, molecular and mechanistic layers.
2. **Resolution profiles** — coarse/intermediate/fine or equivalent nested representations by radiation, without forcing a common winner.
3. **Prospective Iris falsification** — frozen timeline, three AUCs, joint-permutation contrasts, terminal FAIL.
4. **Molecular-axis heterogeneity** — hue/branching versus amount/depletion localization.
5. **Boundary model** — proposed moderator framework and explicit predictions for future held-out radiations.

## Claim boundaries

- Do not claim a universal intermediate optimum.
- Do not reinterpret Iris as a fine-resolution success; all three AUCs are below 0.5.
- Do not use sensitivity analyses to rescue the frozen FAIL.
- Do not pool heterogeneous biological evidence units into one recurrence proportion.
- Do not claim that the same mechanistic scale is optimal across radiations.
- Do claim that a prespecified universal ordering was prospectively falsified.
- Do claim that the falsification narrows the defensible generalization from a common optimum to radiation-specific resolution profiles.

## Immediate next step

Before adding another held-out radiation, freeze a **moderator rule** that predicts the expected resolution profile from outcome-independent properties of the system. A new prospective system should then test the moderator prediction, not merely repeat the three-level AUC exercise and choose the winner afterward.
