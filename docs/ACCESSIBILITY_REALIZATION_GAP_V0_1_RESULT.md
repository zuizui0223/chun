# Accessibility–realization gap v0.1 — supplementary section-proxy sensitivity

## Disposition

This analysis is retained as a supplementary historical-background sensitivity. It is **no longer the primary macro realization result** because traditional *Camellia* sections are taxonomic bins rather than a nuclear phylogeny.

The primary macro layer is now `docs/NUCLEAR_COLOUR_REALIZATION_ROBUSTNESS_V0_1.md`, which uses accepted-species public nuclear topologies and wild-colour audits.

## Question

Is short-timescale mechanistic accessibility sufficient to explain broad realization of coarse flower-colour outcome classes across traditional section backgrounds?

The analysis combines two admitted facts:

1. short-timescale mechanistic transitions are documented for outcome classes A, W and Y;
2. the Fan 2026 full traditional-section table provides a count-controlled null for how broadly each visible state is distributed across 17 section proxies.

Micro study counts are not interpreted as natural generation rates. Traditional sections are not interpreted as branch transition rates or independent evolutionary events.

## Result

Define a section-breadth realization gap as:

`1 - observed section breadth / expected section breadth under the count-controlled label-shuffle null`.

| state | observed / expected section breadth | realization gap | breadth lower-tail P |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 4 / 9.568 | **0.582** | **0.000020** |
| Y | 4 / 8.045 | **0.503** | **0.00130** |
| W | 13 / 13.480 | **0.036** | 0.496 |

Entropy gives the same qualitative result:

- A entropy gap = **0.752**;
- Y entropy gap = **0.583**;
- W entropy gap = **0.084**.

## Interpretation

A and Y occur across substantially fewer traditional section backgrounds than expected after controlling for their species counts, whereas W does not show excess section concentration.

This is consistent with the stronger nuclear-tree conclusion that all feasible visible states do not share one historical realization pattern. Importantly, the state details are not identical across the two approaches: strict wild nuclear evidence gives especially robust Y clustering, A is trait-coding sensitive, and W is not individually clustered. Therefore the section analysis must not be used to upgrade A to a strict nuclear result.

## What the result does not identify

The section gap does not distinguish among:

- lineage-specific generation permissivity;
- differential persistence or loss;
- one early origin followed by radiation;
- introgression / reticulation;
- ecological filtering.

It also cannot replace branch-transition reconstruction or a mechanistic-state phylogeny.

## Current role

Use this analysis only as evidence that the historical-background mismatch is not an artifact of one nuclear-tree implementation. The main paper should lead with topology-robust accepted-species local phylogenetic conservatism and retain this section-level count-controlled result in supplementary sensitivity material.
