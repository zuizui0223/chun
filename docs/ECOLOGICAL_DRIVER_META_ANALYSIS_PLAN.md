# Ecological-driver meta-analysis before Paper 1 submission

## Decision

The AJB submission bundle is a provisional freeze, not the final scientific boundary. Branch-specific ecological causation is not identifiable from the current accepted-species hard-state matrix, but this does **not** imply that study-level ecological effects cannot be meta-analysed.

Before final submission, quantify the ecological evidence that generated the ultimate-driver hypotheses.

## Core question

> Which ecological processes have measurable effects on floral-state persistence or reproductive performance in *Camellia*, and which hypotheses remain unsupported after quantitative synthesis?

## Meta-analysis axes

### A. Pollinator-service contribution
Primary effect: reproductive success with full pollinator access versus selective exclusion of a functional group.

Preferred effect size: log response ratio (lnRR) for fruit set; seed set is a correlated secondary outcome.

Current quantitative seed:
- *C. japonica*: open fruit set 36.2% vs bird-excluded 5.7% (Kunitake et al. 2004).
- *C. petelotii*: bird exclusion reduces fruit set 67.06% and seed set 64.12% (Sun et al. 2017).
- *C. oleifera*: bird exclusion reduces fruit set 56.28% and seed set 27.61% (Zhang et al. 2024).

Question: is bird contribution consistently positive across visible states rather than specific to red/A flowers?

### B. Pollen limitation / reproductive reliability
Primary effect: supplemental cross-pollination versus open pollination.

Preferred effect size: lnRR for fruit set, plus published pollen-limitation index where reported.

Current quantitative seed:
- *C. pubipetala*: fruit set 6.7% open vs 23.3% supplemented; seed set 38.9% vs 64.7%; fruit PL index 0.743 (Chai et al. 2019).
- *C. petelotii*: open vs pollen supplement not detectably different (Sun et al. 2017).
- *C. oleifera*: multi-site pollinator-shortage/pollen-limitation experiment in Xie et al. 2013; extract habitat-specific values from primary figures/tables before pooling.

Question: does reproductive limitation vary with pollinator availability and ecological context strongly enough to support a reliability-filter hypothesis?

### C. Season/weather-mediated pollinator turnover
Do not pool raw visitation and fruit-set outcomes into one effect. Extract standardized contrasts within response family.

Current quantitative seed:
- *C. petelotii*: bee visitation falls in cloudy/rainy weather while bird access strongly contributes to reproduction.
- *C. perpetua*: winter vs summer contrast in bird/bee weighting, reproductive success, nectar volume (453.66 vs 129.14 uL), and sucrose/hexose ratio (49.69 vs 6.99).
- *C. japonica*: disturbance alters bird movement/pollen-flow context.
- *C. sinensis*: pollinator contribution is seasonal in cultivated seed-garden evidence.

Question: is flowering-window ecological context a stronger and more mechanistically coherent predictor than annual climate means?

### D. Abiotic manipulation of pigment state
Keep experimental treatment effects separate from macro climatic association.

Current quantitative seed:
- *C. japonica* 'Nuccio's Pearl': 7 C dark/chilling treatment for 0/4/6/8 weeks gives petal anthocyanin 0.240/0.256/0.195/0.174 umol g-1 FW (Berruti et al. 2015). The treatment is confounded by darkness, so it is evidence that the flowering environment can alter pigment deployment, **not** a clean cold-adaptation test.

Search for additional *Camellia* petal-specific light/temperature/water manipulations. Leaf-only *C. sinensis* anthocyanin experiments are external mechanistic support and must not be pooled with floral responses.

## Model hierarchy

1. Fit each outcome family separately; do not standardize incompatible outcomes into one omnibus effect.
2. Use one primary effect per study/species/outcome to avoid pseudoreplication; correlated secondary outcomes are sensitivity analyses.
3. Prefer lnRR where raw means/proportions are available.
4. Estimate sampling variance only where denominators/SE/SD or model contrast statistics permit; otherwise flag magnitude-only effects and keep them out of inverse-variance pooling.
5. With k < 5, report pooled estimates as exploratory and emphasize prediction intervals / leave-one-out sensitivity rather than asymptotic P values.
6. Moderator tests (visible state, pollinator class, flowering season) require at least two independent studies per level and are otherwise descriptive only.

## Scientific decision rules

- **Supports reproductive-service filtering:** comparable exclusion/supplement studies show repeatable large reproductive effects across independent systems.
- **Supports climate-mediated reproductive turnover:** season/weather contrasts repeatedly change effective pollinator weighting or reproductive success, beyond annual-mean climate associations.
- **Supports direct abiotic pigment control:** multiple flower-specific manipulations show consistent pigment response to the same abiotic axis without a major treatment confound.
- **Supports visible-hue syndrome:** visible A/W/Y predicts ecological function after study design and phylogeny are accounted for. Current evidence does not establish this.
- **Public-data boundary:** if an ecological axis lacks >=3 comparable independent effect sizes after systematic extraction, retain it as structured evidence synthesis rather than force a pooled meta-analysis.

## Consequence for Paper 1

Do not finalize the Discussion or abstract until this ecological-driver synthesis is complete. The existing molecular-accessibility and accepted-species macro-pattern results remain frozen; only the ecological-driver layer is reopened.
