# Macro Paper public-data boundary and empirical handoff

## Decision

**A macroevolutionary paper is now defensible, but only with a narrower claim than the earlier branch-causation programme.**

The publishable result is not a new Camellia phylogeny, not a definitive white-flowered ancestor, and not a demonstrated climate- or pollinator-driven W→A transition. The defensible cross-scale result is:

> Camellia pigment states appear molecularly accessible through multiple implementation routes, yet accepted-species wild flower colours remain locally phylogenetically conserved. Current public hard-state trait data are insufficient to identify specific colour-transition branches robustly enough for branch-level ecological or molecular causal attribution.

This pattern-without-identifiable-events boundary is itself the endpoint of the public-data programme and defines the next empirical study.

## Research logic that led here

### Stage 1 — hypotheses from prior literature

Initial candidate explanations were:

1. direct abiotic filtering, especially cold/light/stress selection on anthocyanin;
2. pollinator-mediated floral adaptation;
3. molecular/pathway constraint on which colour states are accessible.

Primary pollination literature subsequently motivated more explicit ecological alternatives: pollination-service reliability, pollinator conflict, latent sensory/reward phenotype, flowering-window effects and ecological hysteresis.

### Stage 2 — micro reanalysis changed the molecular question

Sequence-aware reanalysis did not support a simple universal `same gene reused` model.

- FLS contains a supported same-paralog-lineage recurrence (CnFLS2-like / CSA008358), while a later white-directed tea locus is a distinct FLS paralog.
- DFR recurrence is anchored to different paralog subclasses: CjDFR is CsDFRa-like whereas the tea white/pink source locus CSA003949 is CsDFRb2.
- ANS/LDOX and ANR retain copy-specific directional heterogeneity and are safer at family/module resolution than exact-node resolution.

The resulting micro hypothesis is **hierarchical reuse / paralog substitution**: similar pigment-network states may be repeatedly accessible even when the exact molecular implementation differs.

Claim boundary: these micro data show accessibility and implementation diversity; they do not show repeated exact molecular deployment on independently reconstructed macroevolutionary colour-transition branches.

### Stage 3 — simple ecological explanations were screened

The current climate screens do not support a universal visible-colour-to-cold-niche chain. Within historical-section proxies, A/W-different pairs were not climatically farther apart than same-colour pairs.

The pollination-function literature seed also rejects a deterministic `visible hue -> pollinator guild` interpretation: the same coarse visible state can contain different effective pollinator functions, and pollination context can change seasonally or environmentally without a visible hue change.

This raised the alternative that A/W/Y is an observation layer, whereas selection may act on a latent combination of pigment chemistry, UV/visible spectrum, fluorescence, floral geometry, reward, phenology and pollinator-service quality.

### Stage 4 — independent nuclear topology produced a macro pattern

An independent 339-locus Angiosperms353 nuclear analysis was built without flower-colour information. WFO Plant List 2026-06 normalization then collapsed the legacy sample to accepted species, and a species-level wild/floristic colour audit replaced provisional Fan-derived hard labels.

The audit materially changed the data:

- 55 accepted Camellia species in the FastTree/ASTRAL sensitivity backbone;
- 36 accepted species with any Fan colour evidence;
- 35 provisional single hard states before wild-colour audit;
- strict wild seed: **24** species (A=1, W=19, Y=4);
- dominant-colour sensitivity: **30** species (A=4, W=22, Y=4);
- 11 provisional hard labels demoted in the strict analysis because of wild polymorphism, dominant-only colour, ontogenetic tint/fading or insufficient exact-species colour evidence.

### Stage 5 — event-level claims failed the accepted-species trait test

On the accepted-species tree:

- strict wild-colour scenario: **0** strong robust branch transitions;
- dominant sensitivity: one W→A branch;
- strict × dominant cross-scenario gate: **0** accepted robust transitions.

Therefore no specific accepted-species colour-transition branch can currently be promoted into branch-climate, branch-pollination or branch-molecular causal enrichment.

This supersedes the three historical W→A branches obtained on the taxonomically inflated legacy-tip tree.

### Stage 6 — the pattern survives, but only locally

FastTree/WFO55 topology with the audited seeds showed colour-wide phylogenetic conservatism under both strict and dominant trait scenarios.

The stronger IQ-TREE LG+G4 + 1000 UFBoot gene-tree ensemble produced a highly concordant accepted-species topology on the 53-species overlap:

- 46/50 nontrivial splits shared;
- reciprocal split recall = 0.92;
- split Jaccard = 0.8519;
- normalized RF = 0.08.

Directly repeating the wild-colour permutation tests on this UFBoot topology narrowed the robust result:

**Strict UFBoot (23 species; A=1, W=19, Y=3)**
- global same-state MPD P=0.1724 — not significant;
- nearest-same-state P=0.00116 — significant;
- Y MPD P=0.00771 and MNTD P=0.00721;
- W not individually significant; A not testable as a singleton.

**Dominant UFBoot (29 species; A=4, W=22, Y=3)**
- global same-state MPD P=0.1327 — not significant;
- nearest-same-state P=0.0000800 — significant;
- A MPD P=0.00110 and MNTD P=0.0119;
- Y MPD P=0.00501 and MNTD P=0.00530;
- W not individually significant.

Thus the topology- and trait-scenario-robust macro claim is **local same-colour phylogenetic conservatism**, not broad global compression of all same-colour species and not A-specific permissivity.

## What Paper 1 can claim

### Core claim 1 — molecular accessibility is implementation-flexible

Published Camellia molecular evidence, re-resolved at paralog/sequence level, contains both same-lineage recurrence and different-paralog implementation within recurrent pigment modules. This motivates a distinction between exact-gene reuse and module-level accessibility.

### Core claim 2 — molecular accessibility does not imply macroevolutionary lability

After accepted taxonomy, wild-colour auditing and two independent nuclear gene-tree pipelines, a species with a known wild colour tends to have a closer same-colour relative than expected from count-preserving random placements.

### Core claim 3 — visible colour is an incomplete ecological state

Current primary pollination evidence contains same-hue taxa with different effective pollinator functions and context-dependent changes in pollination service without hue change. Existing climate screens also fail to support a simple universal visible A/W -> thermal-niche divergence model.

### Core claim 4 — public hard-state data identify a macro pattern but not its causal events

No accepted-species transition branch survives both strict wild-colour and dominant-colour assumptions. Therefore public data currently identify **pattern-level historical constraint**, but not transition-specific ecological causation or exact micro-to-macro molecular reuse.

## What Paper 1 must NOT claim

Do not claim:

- a novel Camellia phylogeny;
- a novel definitive white ancestor (Fan et al. 2026 already reports a likely white ancestor; our accepted-species analysis retains W/Y uncertainty);
- universal cold adaptation of anthocyanin/red flowers;
- pollinator-driven colour evolution at genus level;
- a robust set of accepted-species W→A branches;
- exact-node molecular recurrence on reconstructed macro branches;
- that A/red has a unique lineage-permissivity effect;
- that all same-colour species are globally clustered under all nuclear topologies.

## Relation to recent literature

Fan et al. (2026, Plant Biotechnology Journal, DOI 10.1111/pbi.70442) uses 237 accessions and 11 genomes to infer a likely white ancestor and show TE-mediated structural variation / regulatory rewiring associated with Camellia flower-colour diversification. Our paper should not compete on ancestral colour or structural-variant mechanism.

Zan et al. (2023, Molecular Phylogenetics and Evolution) and Yan et al. (2024, Molecular Phylogenetics and Evolution) already establish extensive nuclear gene-tree heterogeneity, reticulation/ILS and rapid radiation in Camellia. Our nuclear analyses are therefore a trait-independent robustness framework, not a claim of a superior new genus phylogeny.

The distinct contribution is the **cross-scale mismatch and identifiability result**: sequence-level molecular implementation is flexible, accepted-species wild colour still shows local phylogenetic conservatism, and current public hard-state traits are too coarse/sparse to assign that macro pattern to specific ecological transition events.

## Recommended Paper 1 framing

### Primary question

**Why does molecular accessibility not translate into macroevolutionary flower-colour lability in Camellia?**

### Working title candidates

1. **Molecularly accessible yet phylogenetically constrained: flower-colour evolution across Camellia**
2. **Flexible molecular routes, constrained floral states: a cross-scale analysis of Camellia flower-colour evolution**
3. **When molecular accessibility does not predict macroevolutionary lability: flower colour in Camellia**

### Paper architecture

1. **Literature-derived alternatives** — direct climate adaptation, pollinator filtering, molecular constraint.
2. **Sequence-aware micro synthesis** — same-lineage recurrence versus paralog substitution.
3. **Taxonomy- and provenance-aware macro data construction** — WFO accepted taxonomy and wild-colour audit.
4. **Independent nuclear topology sensitivity** — FastTree versus IQ-TREE/UFBoot gene-tree ensembles.
5. **Macro pattern** — topology-robust nearest-same-colour phylogenetic conservatism.
6. **Ecological screening** — no simple climate or visible-hue/pollinator-syndrome explanation from current public data.
7. **Identifiability test** — zero strict × dominant robust accepted-species transition branches.
8. **Public-data boundary** — causal event attribution requires new observations.

## Publication assessment

**Verdict: yes, potentially publishable as a macroevolutionary / integrative plant-evolution paper, provided the manuscript is framed around the cross-scale mismatch and the public-data identifiability boundary rather than adaptation causation.**

The strongest conceptual result is not a large effect size but a logically sharp contrast:

> multiple molecular routes make pigment-state generation accessible, while accepted wild colour remains locally history-dependent; the available public phenotype data resolve the pattern but not the causal events.

A plant-evolution / macroevolution journal is a better fit than a molecular-genomics journal. A higher-tier general evolutionary venue would require demonstrating that the accessibility-versus-persistence framework generalizes beyond Camellia or adding stronger empirical ecological evidence; that should not be forced into Paper 1.

## PUBLIC-DATA BOUNDARY — stop rule reached

The public-data programme should stop branch-specific causal expansion here because:

1. accepted-species hard colour states are sparse and often genuinely polymorphic;
2. strict and dominant trait scenarios yield zero shared robust colour-transition branches;
3. pollinator-function evidence is too sparse and design-heterogeneous for genus-level branch causation;
4. coarse visible hue aliases multiple spectral/reward/pollination states;
5. climate screens do not rescue a simple universal abiotic explanation;
6. additional model complexity cannot manufacture the missing wild population-level phenotypes or reproductive-function measurements.

Further branch-climate or branch-pollinator modelling with the same data would create precision without identifiability and should not be added to Paper 1.

## Empirical Study 1 — question generated by Paper 1

### Primary empirical question

**What ecological and molecular factors determine persistence of an accessible floral pigment state within naturally variable Camellia lineages?**

### Hypotheses to distinguish

1. **Pollination-service reliability** — colour/sensory states track effective pollen transfer and reproductive success across weather/season, not raw visitor abundance.
2. **Pollinator conflict** — visual/chemical traits reduce costly or ineffective visitors while retaining effective pollinators.
3. **Latent sensory-state filtering** — UV/visible spectra, fluorescence, reward and morphology predict reproductive function better than human A/W/Y labels.
4. **Ecological hysteresis / molecular memory** — pigment pathways remain accessible and are redeployed through regulatory/paralog shifts when ecological context changes.
5. **Direct abiotic conditional effect** — temperature/light/stress contributes within particular lineages but is not a universal genus-level driver.

### Empirical measurements required

The field/experimental study should measure, in the same populations and individuals where possible:

- population-level wild flower-colour frequencies rather than one species label;
- standardized UV–visible reflectance and fluorescence;
- pigment chemistry / anthocyanin-flavonol composition;
- nectar volume and composition;
- floral morphology and orientation;
- visitor identity and visitation rate;
- **single-visit stigma pollen deposition / effective pollen transfer**;
- pollen removal where feasible;
- bird/insect or guild-exclusion treatments;
- fruit set and seed set;
- flowering-window temperature, radiation, rain and wind;
- developmental and paralog-specific expression of FLS/DFR/ANS/ANR targets.

### Priority biological systems

The most informative systems are not necessarily the cleanest fixed-colour species. The public-data failure itself prioritizes naturally variable taxa and same-hue ecological contrasts.

**Priority A — natural A/W polymorphism / hard-state failure**
- `C. japonica`;
- `C. saluenensis`;
- `C. brevistyla`;
- `C. cuspidata`.

These directly resolve the trait uncertainty that prevented accepted-species transition identification.

**Priority B — pigmented-dominant with rare white**
- `C. reticulata`;
- `C. polyodonta`;
- `C. subintegra`.

These test whether rare white morphs differ in pollination service, environment and paralog deployment within a shared lineage background.

**Priority C — same visible state, different reproductive function**
- `C. japonica` versus `C. rusticana` for the A/red state;
- yellow-flowered `C. petelotii`, `C. pubipetala`, `C. perpetua` for season/weather and bird/insect contribution.

These test whether latent sensory/reward phenotype outperforms visible hue as the ecological selection target.

## Paper-to-empirical handoff

Paper 1 should end with a concrete statement, not a generic call for more work:

> Public phylogenomic, molecular and ecological evidence is sufficient to show that flower-colour generation and persistence operate at different scales, but it is insufficient to identify the ecological causes of accepted-species colour transitions because species-level colour labels collapse natural polymorphism and reproductive function. The next test therefore requires population-resolved colour/spectral states, effective pollination and reproductive success measured together with paralog-specific pigment-network deployment.

That empirical dataset is the direct test of the hypothesis generated by Paper 1, not an unrelated follow-up.
