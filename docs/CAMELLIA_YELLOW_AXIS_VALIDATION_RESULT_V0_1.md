# Camellia yellow-axis validation — result v0.1

## Outcome

The pre-frozen validation result is **`YELLOW_AXIS_MIXED`**.

The exploratory hypothesis came from the completed exhaustive partition controls in Linoideae and Antirrhineae. Camellia was then tested prospectively using two accepted-species topology treatments and strict/dominant wild-colour codings.

## Four frozen settings

| Setting | Yellow rank | Yellow capture | Best partition |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| FastTree × strict | 1 | 1.027 | YELLOW |
| FastTree × dominant | 3 | 0.394 | ANTHOCYANIN |
| UFBoot × strict | 1 | 1.056 | YELLOW |
| UFBoot × dominant | 3 | 0.337 | ANTHOCYANIN |

Yellow/non-yellow is therefore best in **2/4** settings, while its capture is positive in **4/4**.

The pre-frozen PASS criterion required yellow to rank first in at least 3/4 settings plus positive capture in all four. It is not met.

## Interpretation

The result is informative rather than null in a vague sense:

- under the strict wild-colour coding, yellow/non-yellow is the strongest coarse partition on both topology treatments;
- once dominant-colour/polymorphic species are admitted, anthocyanin/non-anthocyanin becomes strongest on both topology treatments;
- the switch is coding-dependent, not topology-dependent.

Thus an apparent cross-system yellow axis discovered in Linoideae and Antirrhineae does **not** validate as a stable Camellia coarse boundary under the predeclared rule.

This supports the merged v0.3 state-granularity interpretation: the phylogenetically informative coarse partition is representation- and coding-dependent rather than universal.

## What is not claimed

This analysis does not infer a yellow pigment pathway, selective cause, ancestral yellow state, pollinator effect, or transition direction. The strict/dominant contrast is a sensitivity result and does not identify which biological coding is uniquely correct.

Paper 1 remains unchanged.
