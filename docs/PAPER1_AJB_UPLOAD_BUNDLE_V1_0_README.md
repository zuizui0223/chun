## Novelty headline

The paper no longer relies on the generic claim that similar flower colours can evolve through different mechanisms. Its strongest defensible contribution is the matched empirical intervention:

> Hold the biological systems fixed, change the molecular observation rule, and measure how much mechanistic replay remains identifiable.

The same public *Camellia* systems are first represented by the heterogeneous, partially observed A/F/C/P states available in the literature and then remeasured under one frozen, outcome-independent pathway-wide protocol. For anthocyanin gain, literature-compatible exact recurrence spans 0.333–1.0 but standardized remeasurement fixes it at 0.333; pairwise concordance contracts from 0.333–1.0 to 0.333–0.5.

The second cross-scale contribution is an explicit macro robustness split: topology/coding-robust local colour structure is retained while no individual accepted-species transition branch survives strict-versus-dominant coding. Pattern robustness and event identity are therefore tested separately rather than collapsed into one historical story.

## Claim ceiling

Do not claim priority for:

- same phenotype arising from different mechanisms;
- pathway-level or modular flower-colour convergence;
- candidate/discovery-method effects on apparent repeatability;
- partial identification as a general statistical idea;
- ancestral-state uncertainty or coding sensitivity;
- the first micro-to-macro *Camellia* flower-colour study.

Do not infer branch-specific pollinator, climate, or other ecological causes while transition-event identity is not robust. White remains a visible evolutionary context, not a molecular zero or a definitive root-state claim.
