# Cross-radiation resolution tier expansion — v0.1

## Purpose

This step attacks the binary interpretation that hue-related transitions are repeatable whereas pigment depletion/loss transitions are not. That wording is too strong.

The question is instead:

> **When flower-colour evolution repeats, does the most stable mechanistic resolution differ among phenotype dimensions?**

The analysis keeps heterogeneous observation regimes separate and uses a tiered stress-test design rather than pooling unlike denominators.

## M1 — primary paired within-radiation comparison

Three radiations contain both a hue/hydroxylation axis and a pigment amount/depletion axis under the same source-level comparative frame:

- Iochrominae;
- Cape *Erica*;
- Petunieae.

### Hue / hydroxylation

All three localize repeatability to a branching-specific or finer molecular level:

- Iochrominae: hue maps to a branching-enzyme subspace;
- Cape *Erica*: both yellow taxa implicate F3'H, via different regulatory/coding implementations;
- Petunieae: the prospectively frozen hue response selects `BRANCHING_HUE` with AICc margin **11.60282936** and beats the null.

Result: **3/3 branch-specific-or-finer**.

### Pigment amount / depletion

The paired amount/depletion side is less specifically localized:

- Iochrominae: intensity maps to a late-pathway coexpression module;
- Cape *Erica*: six white taxa span four focal node classes across late structural and regulatory nodes;
- Petunieae: the frozen raw amount response fails the primary gate (best margin **0.44436806**, best model does not beat the null). The source-method log sensitivity selects `LATE_OUTPUT`, but it is post-gate and cannot upgrade the primary result.

Result at the branch-specific-or-finer level: **0/3**. One system supports a coarser late-pathway module, while two are heterogeneous or primary-fail.

This M1 paired contrast is the main evidence because the phenotype axes are compared within the same three radiations.

## M2 — external hue/branching stress test

*Ipomoea* supplies an independent radiation with robust macro-event identity. Three independent blue/purple-to-red origins repeatedly implicate reduced floral F3'H regulation.

This is an **exact-gene regulatory** positive control and qualitatively extends the M1 hue/branching pattern outside the three paired systems.

## M3 — external depletion/loss stress tests

Two independent systems test whether depletion/loss truly lacks repeatability.

### Aquilegia

Repeated anthocyanin-loss lineages show convergent stronger downstream pathway downregulation. The repeatability is therefore real, but the clean shared level is **late-pathway expression**, not one exact causal regulator in every event.

### Epimedium

A-minus endpoints share **ANS low in 4/4**, while DFR is low in 3/4 and CHS-copy/FLS implementations vary. The broad signature is recurrent only in a subset, and copy-aware complete implementations are still more heterogeneous. Historical event identity remains unresolved, so this is an endpoint/common-panel stress test rather than an event-rate estimate.

Together these systems reject the binary wording `hue stable / depletion unstable`. Depletion/loss can be highly repeatable, but its stable recurrence is supported at a **coarser module/core level** in these external controls.

## M4 — complementary regain test

Two independent *Petunia* colour regains share restoration/recruitment of R2R3-MYB function, but use different regulatory solutions and have **0/2 complete implementation recurrence**.

This is not a hue-versus-depletion test. It supports the broader hierarchy: recurrence can be strong at one functional level while complete molecular replay remains absent.

## Revised result

The retained candidate is:

> **Both hue and pigment-loss transitions can repeat, but their most stable repeatability occurs at different mechanistic resolutions. Hue/hydroxylation can recur at a branching-enzyme or exact-regulatory level, whereas pigment depletion/loss more often recurs at late-pathway or core-module level with heterogeneous lower-level implementation.**

This is stronger and safer than calling one phenotype dimension repeatable and the other non-repeatable.

## Relationship to the macro result

The macro cross-radiation result is structurally parallel:

- fine-state phylogenetic organization: 3/3 external radiations;
- universal transition direction: 0/3;
- privileged preselected coarse boundary: 0/3.

Across macro and molecular scales, the recurring signal therefore appears **resolution dependent**: some resolved dimensions are repeatable, while coarse aggregate states, universal directions, and whole implementations are less stable.

## Claim boundary

Do not claim:

- a universal F3'H law;
- that depletion/loss cannot recur at exact-gene level;
- a pooled cross-tier effect size;
- independence for repeated rows from the same radiation;
- event-for-event matching between the macro and molecular datasets;
- full prospective replication.

Only Petunieae supplies a prospectively frozen paired phenotype-axis test. M2/M3 are external qualitative stress tests under different observation regimes.

Camellia Paper 1 remains unchanged and closed.
