# Accessibility–realization gap v0.1

## Question

Is short-timescale mechanistic accessibility sufficient to explain broad macroevolutionary realization of coarse flower-colour outcome classes?

This is deliberately weaker than a branch-transition test. It combines only two already admitted facts:

1. short-timescale mechanistic transitions are documented for outcome classes A, W and Y;
2. the Fan 2026 full traditional-section table provides a count-controlled null for how broadly each visible state is distributed across 17 historical section proxies.

Micro study counts are not interpreted as natural generation rates. Traditional sections are not interpreted as branch transition rates.

## Result

Define a section-breadth realization gap as:

`1 - observed section breadth / expected section breadth under the count-controlled label-shuffle null`.

The current values are:

| state | observed / expected section breadth | realization gap | breadth lower-tail P |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 4 / 9.568 | **0.582** | **0.000020** |
| Y | 4 / 8.045 | **0.503** | **0.00130** |
| W | 13 / 13.480 | **0.036** | 0.496 |

Entropy gives the same qualitative result:

- A entropy gap = **0.752**;
- Y entropy gap = **0.583**;
- W entropy gap = **0.084**.

## Interpretation

Short-timescale mechanistic accessibility is documented for all three coarse outcome classes, but their macro realization differs sharply. A and Y occur across substantially fewer historical section backgrounds than expected after controlling for their species counts, whereas W does not show excess section concentration.

Therefore the simple model

`mechanistically accessible -> broadly realized`

is not sufficient for the observed *Camellia* macro pattern.

This is a genuine cross-scale mismatch result. It narrows the next question to the missing process between generation and realization:

`micro accessibility -> macro gain hazard -> persistence/loss hazard -> extant distribution`.

## What the result does not identify

The realization gap does not distinguish among:

- lineage-specific generation permissivity;
- differential persistence or loss;
- one early origin followed by radiation;
- introgression / reticulation;
- ecological filtering.

Those alternatives require the admitted nuclear-tree layer and, for ecology, temporal/branch alignment.

## Novelty consequence

The current paper should not be framed as another demonstration that anthocyanin regulation is labile. The stronger public-data result is that **molecular accessibility and macroevolutionary realization are empirically separable**. Accessible colour states need not be evenly realized across historical backgrounds.

The next analysis must identify whether that separation arises during generation or persistence rather than assuming selection after the fact.
