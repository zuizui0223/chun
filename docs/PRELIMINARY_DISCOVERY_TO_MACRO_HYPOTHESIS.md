# From preliminary meta-analysis to macroevolutionary hypotheses

## Rule of inference

This project distinguishes three layers.

1. **Published conclusions** are source-level results already demonstrated in individual studies.
2. **Preliminary discoveries from `chun`** are patterns that emerge only after integrating those studies under one dependence-aware analysis.
3. **`chun` hypotheses** are new explanations generated from those preliminary discoveries and then tested at broader phylogenetic and ecological scales.

A previous paper's discussion or future-work paragraph is not, by itself, a `chun` hypothesis.

---

## I. Published conclusions: inputs to the synthesis

The following are examples of results contributed by individual studies and therefore must not be claimed as discoveries of this repository.

- Redder or pinker Camellia petals can contain more anthocyanin than paler flowers.
- Particular studies implicate `DFR`, `ANS/LDOX`, `FLS`, `ANR/LAR`, MYB/bHLH/WD40/WRKY regulators, and TE/SV-associated regulatory changes in specific colour contrasts.
- Developmental or sectorial colour changes can occur within one genetic background in some Camellia cultivars.
- Fan et al. 2026 reconstruct white as the most likely ancestral visible flower colour in their 237-accession nuclear analysis.
- Individual pollination studies demonstrate strong bird or insect contributions in particular Camellia species.

These are literature-derived facts. Their role in `chun` is to provide comparable observations for synthesis.

## II. Preliminary discoveries generated by `chun`

### Discovery 1 — recurrent directionality across independent systems

After grouping 12 mechanistic study records into 8 independence clusters, the current preliminary synthesis finds:

- more-red/pink states have higher anthocyanin in 6/6 informative independence clusters;
- downstream anthocyanin deployment is stronger in the more-red state in 4/4 informative clusters;
- at least one competing branch is stronger in the less-red/white/yellow state in 5/5 informative clusters;
- regulatory/pathway-flux evidence is present in 8/8 independence clusters;
- no positive case in the current synthesis requires wholesale structural-gene loss for the focal colour contrast.

The important result is not that any one gene was already known from a paper. It is the **cross-study recurrence of the same mechanistic direction** after dependence is controlled.

### Discovery 2 — the same network is labile across biological scales

The integrated evidence contains large colour changes at several scales:

- developmental transitions within one flower or genotype;
- spatial petal-sector differences within one genotype;
- bud sports within a lineage;
- cultivar contrasts within species;
- species-level differences;
- genus-level recurrent visible-colour transitions.

This cross-scale recurrence is not supplied by a single source paper. It is a pattern that appears only after the evidence is placed on one common pigment-network framework.

### Discovery 3 — visible colour is better represented as network allocation than as an ordered hue axis

Across the synthesis, less-red states do not converge on one universal biochemical programme. They can involve:

- stronger `FLS`/flavonol allocation;
- stronger `ANR/LAR` or proanthocyanidin/procyanidin diversion;
- carotenoid deployment;
- reduced anthocyanin-side flux;
- combinations of these.

Therefore the preliminary synthesis supports a multidimensional pigment-state model rather than a simple white–yellow–red ordered trajectory.

### Discovery 4 — mechanism accessibility and macroevolution can now be asked in the same coordinate system

Because the same pathway modules can be measured in developmental, within-genotype and species-level contrasts, `chun` can ask a question that the individual studies were not designed to answer:

> Are the pigment-network states that are easiest to reach at short biological scales also the states most often reused over macroevolutionary time?

This is the main bridge from molecular mechanism to macroevolution.

## III. Problems generated by the preliminary analysis

The preliminary results create several new problems.

### Problem A — accessibility bias

If the same small set of pathway nodes repeatedly changes across independent systems and biological scales, long-term evolutionary transitions may not sample pigment phenotype space uniformly.

The unexplained pattern is therefore not simply repeated colour change. It is **repeated use of similar mechanistic routes**.

### Problem B — state-space asymmetry

If some pigment states require only small regulatory/flux changes while others require coordinated changes across multiple branches, transition probabilities should be asymmetric.

The problem becomes whether macroevolutionary transition rates reflect biochemical/network distance.

### Problem C — white-state role

The synthesis repeatedly finds alternative pathway allocation in pale/white states rather than direct evidence of pathway deletion. This raises the empirical question of whether some white states retain high latent accessibility to coloured states.

This is a question generated by the synthesis; it is not assumed from the published white-MRCA result.

### Problem D — generation versus ecological filtering

The molecular synthesis addresses which phenotypes are easy to generate. It does not explain which variants persist in wild populations.

That creates a separate macroecological problem: after accessibility generates candidate floral states, do pollinators, season, climate/light environment and geography filter them non-randomly?

## IV. Hypotheses generated from `chun` preliminary results

### H1 — Accessibility-biased macroevolution

**Hypothesis:** pigment-network states that are reachable through small regulatory or flux changes at developmental/microevolutionary scales are disproportionately reused during species-level and clade-level flower-colour evolution.

**Generated from:** Discoveries 1 and 2.

**Predictions:**

- micro-labile genes/modules are enriched among macro-transition candidates;
- state-transition rates correlate with mechanistic accessibility;
- recurrent colour evolution preferentially reuses a limited subset of network nodes;
- rare transitions require more coordinated changes, an intermediate state, or introgression.

### H2 — Latent white accessibility

**Hypothesis:** a subset of white/low-pigment states retains sufficient pigment-network competence to serve as highly accessible starting states for later anthocyanin-rich or yellow deployment.

**Generated from:** Discovery 3 plus the absence of positive structural-loss cases in the current preliminary synthesis.

**Predictions:**

- extant white taxa frequently retain intact orthologs and measurable pathway activity;
- white-to-coloured branches are enriched for regulatory/flux changes rather than pathway reconstruction;
- candidate A→W→A histories are more plausible where the W branch retains pathway competence.

This does not assume that every white lineage is latent or that white is a universal intermediate.

### H3 — Recurrent flux partitioning

**Hypothesis:** repeated flower-colour evolution is driven more often by redistribution among anthocyanin, flavonol, carotenoid and proanthocyanidin/procyanidin branches than by repeated construction or destruction of whole pathways.

**Generated from:** Discoveries 1 and 3.

### H4 — Micro-to-macro node reuse

**Hypothesis:** network nodes that repeatedly switch within development, sectors or bud sports have elevated probability of appearing at macroevolutionary colour transitions.

**Generated from:** Discovery 2.

This is a stricter, gene/module-level version of H1 and can be tested as an enrichment problem.

### H5 — Ecological filtering of accessible variants

**Hypothesis:** molecular accessibility determines which floral phenotypes are readily generated, while pollinator sensory environment, nectar economics, flowering season, climate/light environment and geography determine which of those accessible states persist and spread.

**Generated from:** the mismatch between strong molecular recurrence and the observation that human-visible hue alone does not predict pollinator guild consistently.

Conceptually:

`network accessibility -> generated phenotype space -> ecological filtering -> observed macroevolutionary distribution`

### H6 — Adaptive signal diversification

**Hypothesis:** where ecological filtering repeatedly favours different floral signal states, changes in spectral/UV display and associated floral traits alter effective pollen-transfer networks and can contribute to reproductive isolation and lineage diversification.

**Generated from:** H5 plus species-level pollination contrasts. It is a downstream hypothesis, not a current conclusion.

## V. Analysis programme: molecular micro scale to macro scale

### M1 — Micro-accessibility score

Reanalyse public petal RNA-seq and quantify, for each pathway gene/module, how often and how strongly it changes in:

- development;
- petal sectors;
- bud sports;
- within-species cultivar contrasts.

Output: a node/module-level **micro-accessibility score**.

### M2 — Common pathway effect sizes

Estimate standardized effects for anthocyanin, flavonol, carotenoid and ANR/LAR diversion modules across public datasets. This replaces directional votes with comparable quantitative effects.

### M3 — Macro transition reconstruction

Across multiple independent nuclear tree sets, reconstruct visible-colour and mechanistic pigment-state transitions under ER/SYM/ARD and stochastic-mapping models.

Outputs include distributions of transition counts and branch-level probabilities rather than a single painted-tree count.

### M4 — Micro-to-macro enrichment test

For each gene/module, compare the micro-accessibility score with its occurrence among macro-transition candidate mechanisms.

Core test:

> Are highly micro-accessible nodes overrepresented at macroevolutionary transitions relative to other pigment-network genes?

### M5 — Mechanistic transition-distance model

Define distances among pigment states from the number/magnitude of module changes required in the public molecular data. Test whether inferred macroevolutionary transition rates decrease as mechanistic distance increases.

### M6 — White-state competence test

For white taxa with public genome/transcriptome data, quantify structural-gene integrity and pathway activity. Compare retained-competence white states with branches inferred to later gain/recruit coloured states.

### M7 — Ecological filtering meta-regression

Only after transition history is estimated, test whether state persistence or transition probability covaries with:

- reflectance/UV signal;
- effective bird/insect contribution;
- nectar volume and sugar composition;
- flowering season;
- climate/light environment;
- geography;
- phylogenetic background.

### M8 — Diversification consequence

As the final extension, test whether branches with reconstructed signal/pollinator-state shifts show changes in diversification or reproductive-isolation proxies. This is downstream and should not be used to explain the molecular recurrence before H1–H5 are tested.

---

## VI. Claim hierarchy

### Published conclusions

These must always be attributed to the source studies.

### `chun` preliminary discoveries

The repository can currently claim that its dependence-aware synthesis reveals recurrent regulatory/flux directionality and cross-scale reuse of the same pigment-network architecture.

### `chun` hypotheses

Accessibility-biased macroevolution, latent-white accessibility, micro-to-macro node reuse, and ecological filtering are hypotheses **generated from those preliminary repository results**.

They must remain labelled as hypotheses until the corresponding macroevolutionary tests are completed.

---

## Central research question

> **Do short-scale molecular accessibility constraints in a conserved pigment network predict the long-term evolutionary routes by which Camellia repeatedly diversifies floral colour, and are those accessible variants subsequently filtered by ecology into the phylogenetic and geographic patterns observed today?**

This is the main micro-to-macro extension of the `chun` programme.
