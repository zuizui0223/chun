# Ecological-driver meta-analysis v2 — triangulated result

## Question

Which ecological processes have repeatable quantitative effects on reproductive performance in *Camellia*, after keeping molecular accessibility and accepted-species macro-pattern results fixed?

The v2 synthesis does not force heterogeneous ecological outcomes into a single omnibus effect size. It separates genus-level cross-species evidence from repeated tests within *C. oleifera* and from climate/season mediation.

## 1. Cross-species pollinator-service magnitude remains large

Three independent bird-access/exclusion systems span all three coarse visible states:

- *C. japonica* (A): RR = 6.35;
- *C. petelotii* (Y): RR = 3.04;
- *C. oleifera* (W): RR = 2.29.

All point in the same direction. Equal-weight mean lnRR = 1.262, geometric-mean RR = **3.53**. Leave-one-out geometric means remain **2.64–4.39**.

This remains a magnitude synthesis rather than an inverse-variance random-effects meta-analysis because defensible sampling variances are not available for all three effects. The important biological result is that large reproductive benefits of bird access occur in A, Y and W systems, so the evidence does not support a red-specific bird syndrome.

## 2. Independent experiments within *C. oleifera* replicate strong pollinator-service effects

Two independent experiments using different functional groups and designs give similar fruit-set benefits:

- Zhang et al. 2024 bird access vs bird exclusion: RR = **2.29**;
- Liu et al. 2025 *Apis cerana* cage introduction vs no-bee cage: RR = **2.56**.

Their equal-weight geometric mean is **2.42**. A secondary open-field contrast in Liu et al. gives RR = **4.31**, but this is not counted as a third independent study.

Thus the cross-species bird result is complemented by within-species evidence that reproductive service is not tied to one pollinator guild: both birds and bees can substantially raise *C. oleifera* reproductive output under different ecological contexts.

## 3. Pollinator reliability is reproduced across gradients and years

Independent *C. oleifera* studies converge on the same mechanism.

- Xie et al. 2013: pollen limitation decreases with legitimate *Andrena camellia* visit density, `PL = 0.48 - 0.007 × VD`, F = 20.05, df = 6, P = 0.004.
- Li et al. 2021: wild-bee abundance positively predicts fruit set in both 2018 (estimate 0.1968 ± 0.0137) and 2019 (0.1948 ± 0.01315).
- In the same two years, fruit set declines with distance from a large bee nesting aggregation (-0.00543 ± 0.00059; -0.006171 ± 0.000676).

Across these five registered gradient effects, **5/5 have the direction predicted by pollinator-service limitation**. Coefficients are on incompatible scales, so they are not pooled numerically.

## 4. Pollen limitation is real but context dependent

For *C. pubipetala*, supplemental pollination increases fruit set from 4/60 to 7/30 flowers:

- RR = **3.50**;
- lnRR = 1.253;
- reconstructed SE(lnRR) = 0.586;
- approximate 95% RR interval = **1.11–11.03**.

In contrast, *C. petelotii* shows no detectable open-versus-supplemental difference despite a large bird-exclusion effect. Xie et al. further show that pollen limitation varies continuously with pollinator availability across *C. oleifera* forests.

A *C. oleifera* cross-pollination vs open contrast (RR ≈ 2.02) is retained only as sensitivity evidence because the breeding-system treatment also changes emasculation/bagging and paternal compatibility.

The ecological implication is that pollinator contribution and pollen limitation are related but not interchangeable; whether service limits reproduction depends on ecological context and mating system.

## 5. Climate and season repeatedly act through pollination conditions

Five admitted studies representing four taxa contain explicit climate/season effects on pollinator service, reward or reproductive conditions.

- *C. hainanica*: a cooler northward site delayed anthesis by 45 d, reduced peak pollinator visitation by 92%, reduced pollen deposition by 57%, and produced 0 natural fruit set while hand cross-pollination still produced 11%.
- *C. perpetua*: winter vs summer nectar volume RR ≈ 3.51 and sucrose:hexose ratio RR ≈ 7.11, with stronger winter bird/reproductive weighting.
- *C. petelotii*: bee visitation declines in cloudy/rainy weather while bird access strongly contributes to reproduction.
- *C. oleifera*: historical flowering-weather observations distinguish natural-pollination conditions (approximately >=8 C with pollinator activity) from artificial-pollination fruit set possible from approximately 5 C under sunny frost-free conditions; the three-year honey-bee experiment also reports weather-dependent pollination benefit.

These effects are deliberately not pooled because response variables differ. Their triangulation supports `flowering-window environment -> pollinator/reward conditions -> reproductive service` more directly than a universal `annual climate -> visible colour` model.

## 6. Direct abiotic floral-pigment evidence remains insufficient

Only one admitted flower-specific manipulation currently tests pigment deployment directly: *C. japonica* 'Nuccio's Pearl' under 7 C dark storage. Anthocyanin declines at 6 and 8 weeks, but cold and darkness are confounded. Leaf-only tea anthocyanin experiments are excluded from this floral response family.

Therefore a direct abiotic floral-pigment meta-analysis is **not identifiable from current Camellia experiments**.

## 7. Visible hue remains an inadequate ecological state

The same-red *C. rusticana* vs *C. japonica* paired experiment gives a bumblebee visit ratio of approximately **23.46**, alongside UV/fluorescence differences. Together with large bird-service effects in A, Y and W flowers, this rejects a deterministic mapping from coarse visible hue to pollination function.

## Revised ecological conclusion

The strongest ecological evidence is not a simple red-bird or red-cold syndrome. It is a **conditional reproductive-service filter**:

`molecular accessibility -> latent pigment/spectral/reward phenotype -> flowering-window environment + pollinator availability/effectiveness -> reproductive success -> persistence`.

Support is strongest for the middle of this chain: pollinator availability and effective access repeatedly produce large reproductive consequences, and climate/season repeatedly changes the conditions under which that service is delivered. Direct abiotic control of floral pigment is much less replicated.

This still does not prove that these ecological effects caused the accepted-species colour transitions, because robust transition branches are not identifiable after taxonomy and wild-polymorphism audit. It does, however, provide quantitative evidence for the ecological filtering mechanism that Paper 1 previously treated only as a hypothesis.

## Paper 1 consequence

The Discussion should no longer say simply that ecological causes are untestable. It should distinguish:

1. **mechanism/service level:** quantitative support for pollinator-service and reliability filtering, including climate/season mediation;
2. **macroevolutionary event level:** causal alignment to individual accepted-species colour transitions remains unidentifiable;
3. **direct abiotic pigment level:** current Camellia floral experiments remain too sparse/confounded for a comparable synthesis.

Formal inverse-variance pooling is still not justified for the main cross-species fruit-set service family until sampling variances for Kunitake 2004 and Sun 2017 can be reconstructed defensibly.
