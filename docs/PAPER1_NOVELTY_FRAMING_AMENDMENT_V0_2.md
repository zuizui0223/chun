# Paper 1 novelty framing amendment — v0.2

## Status

This document translates `docs/PAPER1_NOVELTY_LITERATURE_AUDIT_2026-08-27.md` into manuscript-facing edits without changing any scientific result. It supersedes broad novelty language that can no longer survive the prior-art audit.

## Core rule

Paper 1 does **not** claim novelty for any of the following ideas in isolation:

- repeated flower colour can be produced by different genes or pigment pathways;
- pathway-level or modular convergence exists;
- candidate-focused discovery can overestimate genetic repeatability;
- ancestral-state or transition-event inference is uncertain or coding-sensitive;
- Camellia flower colour has already been linked across genomics, phylogeny and functional mechanism;
- pollinators or climate are plausible flower-colour selective agents.

The defensible contribution is the empirical combination:

> **same-system observation-regime standardization + explicit incomplete-state bounds + transition-class-specific multivariate recurrence + topology/coding-robust macro pattern + an event-identity stop rule.**

## Introduction replacement A — broad repeated-evolution framing

### Replace the generic novelty implication

Do not let the opening imply that `same phenotype != same mechanism` is the unresolved conceptual gap.

### Manuscript-facing text

Repeated phenotypic evolution does not require exact molecular repetition. Flower-colour studies have already shown both alternative genetic architectures for similar visible states and convergence at broader biochemical or pathway levels, while work on repeated evolution more generally has shown that estimated genetic reuse can depend on how candidate mechanisms are discovered (Conte et al., 2012; Ng and Smith, 2016; Larter et al., 2018; Wheeler et al., 2023). The unresolved problem addressed here is therefore narrower: when different studies have observed different subsets of a multivariate pigment system, how much mechanistic recurrence is actually identified by the published evidence, and does that conclusion persist when the **same biological systems** are remeasured under one outcome-independent observation protocol?

## Introduction replacement B — Camellia-specific gap

### Replace/extend the paragraph beginning with the genus-scale genomic context

### Manuscript-facing text

Camellia is not an unexplored molecular or macroevolutionary system. Recent work has synthesized the flavonoid, DFR/FLS and MBW regulatory literature in detail, and genus-scale phylogenomics has already linked flower-colour diversification to structural variation and transposable-element-mediated regulatory change; yellow-Camellia genomic work has further connected flavonol evolution to WRKY23–FLS regulation (Xiao et al., 2025; Fan et al., 2026a, b). The opportunity is therefore not to provide the first molecular explanation of Camellia colour diversity or the first micro-to-macro reconstruction. Instead, we ask whether the strength of *recurrent mechanism* inferred from the existing literature survives a standardized remeasurement of the same public systems, and how far any resulting molecular inference can be projected onto a macroevolutionary history once taxonomy, wild-colour coding and nuclear-topology sensitivity are made explicit.

## Introduction final-question paragraph

### Recommended final framing

Here we treated observation and historical identification as explicit inferential layers rather than assuming that published molecular mechanisms and reconstructed transition branches were direct samples of biological recurrence. We first represented the literature as partially observed A/F/C/P pigment-state signatures and bounded recurrence over unresolved axes. We then remeasured the same public RNA-seq systems with one frozen candidate-free protocol. Separately, we tested whether accepted wild flower colours showed phylogenetic structure robust to independent nuclear topologies and alternative trait codings, and whether any individual transition branches survived those same coding sensitivities. This design asks not whether mechanistic heterogeneity or ancestral-state uncertainty exist in principle, but **where the available Camellia evidence ceases to identify the stronger cross-scale claim**.

## Discussion replacement A — molecular result

### Manuscript-facing text

The molecular result should not be read as the first demonstration that similar flower colours can arise through different mechanisms. That principle is established across floral systems. The new empirical point is that the *estimated degree of multivariate recurrence changes when the observation regime is standardized on the same Camellia systems*. Literature-selected measurements left complete A/F/C/P recurrence admissible over broad identified sets; candidate-free remeasurement contracted those sets and fixed lower whole-package recurrence while retaining substantial modular reuse. The contrast therefore quantifies an observation-process contribution to the mechanistic recurrence inferred from an existing literature corpus rather than merely documenting another case of genetic heterogeneity.

## Discussion replacement B — macro pattern versus event identity

### Manuscript-facing text

Uncertainty in ancestral-state reconstruction and sensitivity to character coding are longstanding phylogenetic problems, so the absence of a robust event set is not itself a new methodological discovery. The empirical contribution here is the separation obtained under the same predeclared robustness design: nearest-same-colour phylogenetic structure survives two independently inferred nuclear topologies and both wild-colour encodings, whereas no accepted-species transition branch is shared by strict and dominant coding. Thus the data support a macroevolutionary *pattern* without supplying a comparably robust set of individual historical *events*. This distinction determines which downstream questions are identifiable rather than serving only as a generic caution about ancestral reconstruction.

## Discussion replacement C — ecological meaning

### Manuscript-facing text

This event-identification boundary also changes the ecological interpretation. Pollinator service, sensory differentiation, seasonal reliability and temperature effects are all established components of flower-colour and Camellia biology, and Camellia pollination research extends at least to the specialist-bee work on C. oleifera in the 1970s. These data motivate ecological filtering and persistence as plausible processes, but they do not restore the identity of individual accepted-species colour-transition branches. We therefore retain ecology at the level supported by the evidence: it can help explain why accessible colour states differ in establishment, reproductive service or persistence, but the present dataset does not identify a particular pollinator or climate cause for a particular historical branch.

## Strongest synthesis paragraph

### Manuscript-facing text

Taken together, the two empirical mismatches occur at different inferential boundaries. At the molecular scale, standardizing what is observed changes the recurrence that is identifiable from the same biological systems. At the macroevolutionary scale, a robust phylogenetic colour pattern survives even when individual historical events do not. The resulting contribution is therefore not a new claim that evolution is mechanistically heterogeneous. It is a matched cross-scale demonstration that **mechanistic recurrence, macroevolutionary pattern and historical event identity can have different robustness to the observation and coding processes used to infer them**.

## Novelty sentence suitable for Abstract/cover letter

> Using the same public Camellia systems, we show that standardizing the molecular observation regime contracts the mechanistic recurrence identifiable from the literature, while an independently robust macroevolutionary colour pattern persists without a robust shared set of individual transition branches.

## Claims forbidden after this audit

The final manuscript and cover letter must not use the following as novelty claims:

- `same phenotype does not mean same mechanism` without further qualification;
- `first pathway-wide analysis of flower-colour convergence`;
- `first demonstration of candidate-gene bias in repeated evolution`;
- `first demonstration that reconstructed events depend on coding`;
- `first micro-to-macro Camellia flower-colour study`;
- `pollinator-driven` or `climate-driven` accepted-species colour transitions.
