# C. perpetua winter mature-flower public-data audit v0.1

## Question

Can the quasi-matched Guilin *Camellia perpetua* system already be closed with an open/public winter mature-flower molecular or spectral dataset?

PR #136 established the useful geometry of the problem: the admitted summer molecular site and the GBG seasonal ecology site are about 104 m apart, and the ecology experiment includes a winter reproductive regime. The remaining mechanistic gap is the floral state in winter.

## Frozen screen

The registry `data/cperpetua_winter_public_data_screen_v0_1.csv` separates eligible mature-flower state measurements from phenology/bud/leaf context.

### Admitted summer mature-flower molecular anchors

1. Zhu et al. 2024 (`10.1007/s44281-024-00052-5`): June 2022 S1–S5 RNA-seq + widely targeted metabolomics from the Guangxi Institute of Botany golden-camellia nursery. This is the current CHUN candidate-free `CPERPETUA` source.
2. Deng et al. 2025 (`10.5511/plantbiotechnology.25.0317a`): June 23, 2023 flower full-length transcriptome plus catechin-related expression/chemistry from the same institutional nursery context. This is an independent summer floral molecular resource, not a winter contrast.

### Public metadata

PRJCA015979 / CRA010489 exposes 15 S1–S5 samples and stage × replicate aliases. It does not expose a winter condition or a plant-ID crosswalk to the seasonal ecology material.

### Seasonal molecular context that does not answer the petal-state question

- March/September transcriptomics exists for terminal and basal flower buds and resolves flowering-gene/GA/TFL1 biology, not mature-petal pigment/spectral state.
- Annual-cycle hormone/histology data exists for leaves and flower-bud differentiation, not mature petals.

These are biologically useful but are not admitted as substitutes for winter mature-flower A/F/C/P, pigment chemistry or spectra.

### Seasonal ecology anchor

Jiang et al. 2025 (`10.1016/j.flora.2025.152727`) directly compares summer and winter nectar, pollinator visitation and reproductive output in GBG and PRV. It provides the ecological side of the bridge but no mature-petal transcriptome/metabolome/spectra.

## Open-indexed saturation result

English and Chinese searches targeted combinations of:

- *Camellia perpetua* / 四季花金花茶 / 四季金花茶;
- winter / January / 冬季 / 冬夏;
- mature flower / petal / 花瓣 / 花;
- transcriptome / RNA-seq / metabolome / gene expression / flavonoid / pigment / spectra.

The screen repeatedly recovered the two summer flower molecular resources, March/September flower-bud transcriptomics, annual leaf/bud physiology, and the summer/winter pollination-ecology paper. It did **not** recover an eligible winter mature-flower molecular or spectral dataset.

This is an open/public indexed stopping result, not a proof that inaccessible, unpublished or non-indexed data do not exist.

## CHUN result

> **The best public Camellia ecological bridge is now localized to one missing measurement: winter mature-flower latent state in the quasi-matched GBG C. perpetua system.**

The existing summer developmental signature must not be treated as a season-invariant species state. In particular, the frozen June candidate-free trajectory `A down / F down / C up / P down` cannot be projected onto winter flowers.

## Decisive next measurement

The minimal high-value design is the same GBG population, preferably tagged plants, with winter and summer full-bloom flowers measured for:

1. CHUN A/F/C/P transcript state (targeted panel or RNA-seq using the same annotation rule);
2. flavonoid/carotenoid/pigment chemistry;
3. UV-visible reflectance and fluorescence;
4. nectar traits and effective bird/bee visitation in the same flowering window;
5. fruit/seed output attached to the same plant/population unit.

This would test the extant ecological filter directly:

`season -> latent floral state + reward -> pollinator effectiveness -> reproductive fitness`.

It would still be distinct from proving that the same process caused a historical accepted-species flower-colour transition.

## Claim boundary

Supported: no eligible winter mature-flower molecular/spectral anchor was found in the frozen open-indexed screen.

Not supported: that winter flowers differ molecularly from summer flowers; that pollinators cause a seasonal pigment-state shift; or that pollinator filtering caused a historical macroevolutionary colour transition.
