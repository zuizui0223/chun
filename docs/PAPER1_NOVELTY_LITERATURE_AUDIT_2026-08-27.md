# Paper 1 novelty literature audit — 2026-08-27

## Purpose

Test whether the current Paper 1 novelty claims survive a literature-first attack. This audit is deliberately stricter than the manuscript: it asks which ideas are already established, which are only partly pre-empted, and what empirical combination remains defensible as the contribution of `chun`.

## Search scope

Search date: 2026-08-27.

Search families were run separately for:

1. repeated / parallel / convergent evolution and candidate-gene ascertainment;
2. flower-colour genetic and pathway-level convergence;
3. multivariate / pathway-wide floral pigment expression and pigment-space studies;
4. ancestral-state / stochastic-character-mapping uncertainty, topology sensitivity and alternative character coding;
5. flower-colour pollinator and abiotic selection reviews;
6. Camellia-specific flower-colour molecular, genomic, phylogenetic and pollination literature, including 2025–2026 work.

Representative query families included combinations of:

- `mechanistic recurrence flower color evolution`, `parallel evolution flower color different genes`, `pathway-level convergence flower color`, `candidate gene bias convergent evolution`, `candidate genes genome-wide parallel evolution`;
- `multivariate pigment pathway flower color evolution`, `anthocyanin flavonol carotenoid flower color evolution`, `pathway-wide flower color gene expression`;
- `phylogenetic signal ancestral state reconstruction uncertainty`, `alternative trait coding ancestral state reconstruction`, `robust across topologies ancestral state reconstruction`;
- `Camellia flower color molecular review`, `Camellia flower colour evolution phylogenomics`, `Camellia pollination flower color`, `Camellia 2026 flavonol genome`.

The search used publisher pages, PubMed/PMC, journal/index records and current review articles as citation-chaining anchors. It is a novelty audit, not a PRISMA systematic review: it does not claim a closed universe of all papers or a reproducible database hit count. The prior-art decisions that matter for manuscript claims are frozen in `data/paper1_novelty_prior_art_registry_v0_1.csv`.

## Verdict by claim

### Claim N1 — observation regime changes apparent recurrence

**Broad novelty: rejected.**

Conte et al. (2012) already showed that estimated genetic reuse depends on discovery method: candidate-gene estimates were higher than genetic-cross estimates, with ascertainment/publication bias explicitly discussed. Poore et al. (2023) likewise contrasted candidate expectations with agnostic genome-wide analysis and found weak repeated genetic divergence despite repeated phenotype.

Therefore Paper 1 must not claim that it discovered the general principle that candidate-focused observation inflates apparent repeatability.

**Narrow novelty retained:** the same public Camellia biological systems are remeasured under one frozen annotation-driven A/F/C/P protocol, unresolved cells remain unresolved, and literature-selected versus candidate-free recurrence is compared on the same system set. The empirical result is a measurable contraction of the literature-permitted identified set, not merely a warning about bias.

### Claim N2 — repeated visible flower colour does not require one complete mechanism

**Broad novelty: strongly rejected.**

This is well established.

- Cooley et al. (2009): repeated red floral pigmentation in Mimulus via different genetic architectures.
- Streisfeld & Rausher (2009): repeated red Ipomoea transitions with pathway-level commonality but alternative possible mutations.
- Ng & Smith (2016): at least 30 independent red origins in Solanaceae via anthocyanins, carotenoids or both.
- Larter et al. (2018): pathway-level multivariate convergence across 28 Iochrominae species.
- Wheeler et al. (2023): pathway-wide expression and pigment-space analysis in Petunieae, including anthocyanin–flavonol trade-offs.
- Rosenblum et al. (2014) and Laruson et al. (2020): general hierarchical convergence and genotype-to-phenotype redundancy.

Paper 1 therefore cannot sell `same phenotype != same mechanism`, `pathway-level convergence`, `modularity`, or `many-to-one genotype-to-phenotype mapping` as first principles discovered here.

**Narrow novelty retained:** Camellia literature-selected mechanisms are represented as partially observed four-axis A/F/C/P signatures, exact recurrence is bounded over unresolved cells, and the same systems are then remeasured under a candidate-free rule. The resulting transition-class-specific result — whole-package recurrence collapses while modular reuse remains, differently for anthocyanin gain and yellow development — is a specific empirical contribution.

### Claim N3 — robust phylogenetic pattern need not identify robust historical events

**Broad novelty: rejected as a methodological principle.**

Stochastic character mapping has long treated character histories as uncertain (Bollback 2006). Revell (2025) reviews strong ancestral-state sensitivity to model misspecification. Kraisitudomsook et al. (2024) provides an empirical example in which alternative coding schemes and broader datasets change ancestral-state reconstructions and transition counts.

Therefore Paper 1 cannot claim discovery of the general fact that ancestral events are uncertain or coding-sensitive.

**Narrow novelty retained:** accepted-species Camellia wild-colour data are subjected to a predeclared two-topology × two-colour-coding robustness design. Local same-colour phylogenetic structure survives all four scenarios, while the set of accepted-species transition branches shared by strict and dominant coding is empty. This explicit separation between a robust unrooted pattern and a failed event-identity gate is the empirical result.

### Claim N4 — ecological value

**Generic pollinator or climate explanation: not novel and not identified here.**

Trunschke et al. (2021) found surprisingly limited direct evidence for pollinator-mediated selection on continuous flower colour despite a large literature, and only a small subset of studies used hand-pollination designs. Lacey (2025) reviews temperature-mediated colour evolution and explicitly notes unresolved direct/indirect selection and phylogenetic-pattern questions. The 2026 AJB flower-colour special issue frames colour as a joint product of pollination, abiotic function, correlated traits and pigment physiology.

Within Camellia, Mori et al. (2023) directly shows that the same coarse red phenotype can encode very different UV/fluorescence signals and pollinator responses. Existing Camellia pollination studies already establish strong context dependence in bird and insect service.

**Ecological value retained:** Paper 1 identifies where ecology can and cannot be attached to the macro history. Ecological evidence supports filtering, persistence and sensory/reproductive context, but current accepted-species event identity is too unstable for branch-specific causal attribution. This is valuable as an inferential boundary, not as a new pollination syndrome or climate adaptation result.

### Claim N5 — micro-to-macro Camellia integration

**`First micro-to-macro Camellia flower-colour study`: rejected.**

Fan et al. (2026, Plant Biotechnology Journal) is a direct competitor: 237 accessions, 11 genomes, genus-scale phylogeny and visible-colour reconstruction, structural variation / TE analyses, and functional MYB60 evidence. Fan et al. (2026, Industrial Crops and Products) adds yellow-Camellia population genomics and WRKY23-to-FLS functional evidence. Xiao et al. (2025) is a comprehensive Camellia flower-colour molecular review with roughly 180 references.

**Strongest remaining novelty:** the project does not optimize one integrated causal story. It explicitly separates:

`mechanistic feasibility -> observation regime -> identified mechanistic recurrence -> macro pattern -> event identity -> ecological attribution`.

Within one genus it then demonstrates two different failures of naive cross-scale reading:

1. literature-selected molecular evidence permits much stronger whole-package recurrence than a standardized remeasurement of the same systems;
2. a robust macroevolutionary colour pattern persists even though individual accepted-species transition events do not survive a predeclared trait-coding robustness gate.

No directly matching prior study was found in this audit that combines **same-system observation-regime standardization + explicit incomplete-state bounds + transition-class modular recurrence + topology/coding-robust macro pattern + an event-identity stop rule** in a flower-colour system.

This is an empirical-combination novelty claim, not a claim that any one underlying concept is unprecedented.

## Consequence for manuscript positioning

### Claims to remove or avoid

Do not write or imply:

- first demonstration that repeated flower colour can have different mechanisms;
- first pathway-level or modular analysis of flower-colour convergence;
- first demonstration that candidate-gene approaches can inflate repeatability;
- first demonstration that ancestral-state/transition inference depends on coding or model choice;
- first micro-to-macro analysis of Camellia flower colour;
- evidence that pollinators or climate caused particular accepted-species transitions.

### Defensible headline

A defensible high-level framing is:

> Repeated flower-colour evolution is often analysed as if the observed molecular literature and reconstructed macroevolutionary events were direct samples of biological recurrence. In Camellia, standardizing the molecular observation regime contracts the recurrence that is identifiable from the same public systems, while topology/coding-robust colour structure persists without a robust shared set of individual transition branches.

The novelty is the **matched inferential audit across scales** and its empirical consequences, not the generic existence of mechanistic heterogeneity.

## Strongest ecological interpretation

The micro and macro results jointly imply that accessibility, realization and persistence are distinct:

`molecular accessibility != macroevolutionary lability`.

Multiple molecular implementations are feasible and some modules recur, yet wild colour remains locally phylogenetically structured. Ecology is therefore relevant as a potential filter on establishment and persistence, but the present data do not identify branch-specific selective causes. That separation is biologically meaningful because it prevents a common shortcut from molecular accessibility or extant ecological association to a unique historical adaptive narrative.
