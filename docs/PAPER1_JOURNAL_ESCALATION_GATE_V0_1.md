## Official-criteria and prior-art attack

The full source-linked audit is in `docs/PAPER1_JOURNAL_ESCALATION_WEB_AND_PRIOR_ART_AUDIT_2026-08-28.md`.

### What recent prior art removes from the novelty claim

1. Discovery method and candidate ascertainment already change estimated genetic reuse (Conte et al. 2012; Poore et al. 2023).
2. Stricter definitions of expression repeatability already reduce measured similarity in a large multi-study reanalysis (Li and Zhang 2026).
3. Same phenotype/different route, pathway-level convergence, and modular regulatory recurrence are established in flower-colour systems (Ng and Smith 2016; Larter et al. 2018; Wheeler et al. 2023; Wenzell et al. 2025).
4. Phenotypic and molecular convergence can be decoupled at genomic scale (Berger et al. 2026).
5. Uncertain historical character paths, partial identification, and pattern-versus-node sensitivity are established inferential problems (Bollback 2006; Manski 2005; Ho and Ané 2014; Cockx et al. 2026).
6. Genus-scale *Camellia* colour phylogenomics and functional mechanisms already exist (Fan et al. 2026).

Therefore hierarchical convergence, many-to-one mapping, observation dependence, and ancestral-state uncertainty are context, not Paper 1 novelty.

### What survives

The bounded search did not locate a direct match for this exact empirical combination:

1. hold the admitted public biological systems fixed;
2. enumerate recurrence compatible with unresolved literature-selected A/F/C/P cells;
3. remeasure the same systems with one prespecified, annotation-driven, outcome-independent pathway rule;
4. quantify contraction of the molecular recurrence identified set;
5. separately require a local macro pattern and individual branch identity to pass different topology/coding robustness gates;
6. stop causal attribution when the shared event set is empty.

This is the real contribution. It is a bounded application-specific result, not a priority proof or a new general estimator.

## Journal-specific decision rules

### Evolution Letters

The version passes title, abstract, and nominal word limits. It fails the substantive gates:

- recent prior art occupies the broad resolution-dependent and hierarchical-repeatability territory;
- three anthocyanin clusters and two yellow trajectories cannot carry a field-wide law;
- the macro arm is necessary to the manuscript's caution but is not event-matched to the molecular arm;
- four-figure compression hides essential audit gates;
- an honest cover letter cannot explain “substantially moves the field forward” without promoting an application-specific combination into a new framework.

Verdict: **do not submit**.

### Evolution

Three important checks pass:

- the same-system remeasurement is not merely another observation of convergence in another taxon;
- the six-figure architecture is natural;
- a ≤200-word abstract and strong title can preserve the inference ceiling.

The decisive general-contribution gate still fails. Li and Zhang 2026 already provides a much broader nested-definition repeatability analysis, and the macro result remains an independent audit rather than a joint test of molecular accessibility and historical transition. The strongest honest pitch is real but application-specific. A general-evolution editor can reasonably read the paper as two rigorous known-principle demonstrations in *Camellia* rather than a substantive advance in evolutionary predictability.

Verdict: **do not use as first submission**.

### American Journal of Botany

The current version passes because:

- the journal explicitly spans molecular, developmental, ecological, systematic, and evolutionary plant science;
- the narrow novelty is meaningful to the botanical audience;
- the structured 242-word abstract and six figures fit current guidance;
- the public-data audit, negative event gate, and provenance sequence can remain visible;
- no stronger scientific claim is needed to justify relevance.

The January 2026 “Paradigm Shifts in Flower Color” special issue confirms audience relevance, but Paper 1 is not represented as part of that already published issue.

Verdict: **first submission**.
