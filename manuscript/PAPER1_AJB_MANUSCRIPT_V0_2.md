# Repeated flower-colour change does not imply repeated pigment-state packages in *Camellia*

**Running head:** Modular recurrence and flower-colour realization

> Draft v0.2. This manuscript is governed by `data/paper1_authoritative_results_v0_2.csv`, `data/paper1_main_figure_manifest_v0_2.csv`, and `data/paper1_reference_registry_v0_2.csv`. The v0.1 manuscript is retained as provenance and must not be patched back into the molecular framing when it conflicts with the v0.2 result hierarchy.

## ABSTRACT

### Premise of the study

Repeated phenotypic change is often interpreted as evidence that evolution repeatedly uses the same mechanism. That inference can be misleading when different studies observe different subsets of a multivariate pathway state. Flower colour provides a tractable test because visible states can be generated by interacting anthocyanin, flavonol, carotenoid, and proanthocyanidin branches, yet most molecular studies measure only selected parts of that system. We asked whether apparent mechanistic recurrence in *Camellia* survives a standardized, outcome-independent remeasurement of the same public systems, and whether the resulting molecular picture is compatible with the macroevolutionary distribution of wild flower colours.

### Methods

We separated four pigment-state axes—anthocyanin (A), flavonol (F), carotenoid (C), and proanthocyanidin diversion (P)—and two prespecified transition classes: anthocyanin gain and yellow development. Literature-derived mechanisms were represented as partially observed A/F/C/P direction signatures and unresolved axes were enumerated exactly. We then reanalyzed five public RNA-seq systems with one frozen annotation-driven pipeline using the same RefSeq reference, all annotation-matched paralogs, a ≥50% module-completeness rule, and no expected-direction or significance filter. Three independent clusters represented red/pink gain and two represented yellow development. Separately, we normalized public nuclear samples to accepted species, audited wild flower-colour states, tested local phylogenetic colour structure on two independently inferred nuclear topologies, and required transition events to survive alternative wild-colour codings before causal interpretation.

### Key results

Selected literature left complete multivariate recurrence admissible in both molecular transition classes. For anthocyanin gain, literature exact-signature recurrence ranged from 0.333 to 1.0 and pairwise A/F/C/P concordance from 0.25 to 1.0. Candidate-free remeasurement fixed exact-signature recurrence at 0.333 and narrowed pairwise concordance to 0.333–0.5. For yellow development, literature exact-signature recurrence ranged from 0.5 to 1.0 and pairwise concordance from 0.25 to 1.0; standardized remeasurement fixed exact-signature recurrence at 0.5 and pairwise concordance at 0.75. The two yellow trajectories agreed in A, C, and P but differed in F. Thus complete whole-package recurrence was not retained in either class, whereas modular reuse was substantial and transition-class dependent. At the macro scale, nearest-same-colour phylogenetic clustering survived both nuclear-topology and wild-colour sensitivities, but no accepted-species transition branch was robust to strict versus dominant colour coding.

### Conclusions

Repeated flower-colour change in *Camellia* does not map to one invariant pigment-state package. Standardizing the observation regime changes the mechanistic recurrence that is identifiable from the same biological systems, while revealing modular rather than all-or-none repeatability. Robust macroevolutionary pattern likewise need not imply identifiable individual transition events. Mechanistic recurrence, observation regime, and macroevolutionary realization should therefore be treated as distinct quantities.

**Key words:** anthocyanin; *Camellia*; flower colour; identifiability; modular evolution; phylogenetic conservatism; repeatability; RNA-seq

# INTRODUCTION

Repeated evolution is commonly used as evidence for constraint or predictability. When similar phenotypes evolve more than once, the same genes, pathways, or developmental routes may be reused because only a limited set of changes can produce a viable phenotype. Yet “the same mechanism” is not a single scale of description. Repetition can occur at an exact nucleotide, gene, paralog family, biochemical branch, multivariate pathway state, or final phenotype. A recurrent phenotype can therefore coexist with substantial mechanistic heterogeneity, and apparent mechanistic convergence can depend on which level an investigator chooses to measure (Rausher, 2008; Wessinger and Rausher, 2012).

Flower colour makes this problem unusually visible. Anthocyanins are central to many red, pink, purple, and blue flowers, but visible floral appearance is generated by interacting pigment branches, copigments, cellular context, spectra, development, and regulatory architecture. Flavonols can alter copigmentation and compete for precursors, carotenoids contribute yellow pigmentation independently of anthocyanins, and proanthocyanidin-directed flux can divert shared substrates. Consequently, a transition described as “anthocyanin gain” may involve more than an increase in one anthocyanin gene, and two redder flowers need not share one complete biochemical or transcriptional state. The same problem applies to yellow flowers, where carotenoid accumulation may be accompanied by coordinated changes in flavonoid branches (Rausher, 2008; Trunschke et al., 2021; Berardi et al., 2026).

A second problem is observational. Molecular studies are rarely random samples of all components of a pathway. Candidate genes are chosen because prior knowledge makes them plausible, assays differ among studies, and published contrasts often emphasize the pathway expected to explain the focal visible phenotype. This does not make candidate-based work invalid; it means that the literature observation process is different from the biological process being inferred. If anthocyanin-related genes are measured more often than competing pigment branches, recurrence of anthocyanin regulation can appear stronger simply because other axes remain unobserved. A defensible test of recurrence must therefore distinguish biological repeatability from the measurement regime that makes repeatability visible.

This distinction also matters when molecular accessibility is projected onto macroevolution. A molecular route can be feasible without being repeatedly realized or maintained across species. Conversely, visible states can remain phylogenetically structured even if the underlying molecular implementations are diverse. Establishing that a pathway can generate a colour state is therefore different from showing that one pathway package repeatedly generates that state across lineages, and both are different from explaining why the visible state persists through speciation, ecological turnover, and reticulate evolution.

The genus *Camellia* is well suited to separate these quantities. It contains extensive flower-colour diversity and a growing molecular literature spanning cultivars, petal sectors, developmental series, transcriptomes, genomes, and functional studies. It also has unusually rich phylogenomic resources, but deep gene-tree discordance, rapid radiation, reticulate evolution, historical taxonomic duplication, and natural within-species colour variation complicate naive trait-history reconstruction (Wu et al., 2022; Zan et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023; Yan et al., 2024). Fan et al. (2026) further showed broad genomic and structural-variant contributions to colour diversification. The remaining opportunity is therefore not to claim that *Camellia* has a white ancestor or that regulatory variation matters, but to ask how strongly recurrent molecular state is actually identified by the available evidence and how that compares with the realized macroevolutionary pattern.

Here we used a falsification-oriented analysis with an explicit observation layer. We asked four questions. First, how incomplete and nonuniform is the published A/F/C/P mechanism matrix? Second, when the same public biological systems are remeasured with one frozen pathway-wide protocol, does whole-package mechanistic recurrence survive, or is repeatability modular? Third, after accepted-taxonomy normalization and wild-colour auditing, is visible flower colour still phylogenetically structured on independent nuclear topologies? Fourth, can individual historical transition branches be identified robustly enough to support branch-specific ecological or molecular causation? Our results reject a simple equivalence between repeated visible colour and repeated whole pigment-state package. Standardized remeasurement instead reveals transition-class-dependent modular recurrence, while the macro data identify local colour structure but not robust individual transition events.

# MATERIALS AND METHODS

## Study design and inferential hierarchy

We separated molecular feasibility, observation regime, candidate-free remeasurement, identified mechanistic recurrence, macroevolutionary realization, and event-level causal interpretation into sequential gates. Downstream outcomes were not used to tune upstream measurements. In particular, expected A/F/C/P direction and statistical significance were never workflow success conditions for the candidate-free raw-data analyses. Visible hue was not used to impute missing molecular axes. At the macro scale, molecular or ecological labels were not used in nuclear marker selection, species-tree inference, taxonomy normalization, or wild-colour coding.

The current result set is frozen in `data/paper1_authoritative_results_v0_2.csv`; Main and Supplement figure roles are frozen in `data/paper1_main_figure_manifest_v0_2.csv`. Superseded analyses remain in repository history but do not re-enter positive claims.

## Molecular state representation

We represented pigment deployment with four prespecified transcript-state axes: anthocyanin branch (A), flavonol branch (F), carotenoid core (C), and proanthocyanidin diversion (P). The A module used DFR, ANS/LDOX, and UFGT/3GT; the F module used FLS; the C module used preregistered core carotenoid families; and the P module used ANR and LAR. These axes are pathway-wide transcript-state summaries rather than metabolite concentrations, enzyme activities, or claims about a single causal gene.

Visible transitions were canonically oriented before expression inspection. Red/pink systems were placed in an `anthocyanin_gain` class oriented toward more red/pink. Yellow developmental systems were placed in a `yellow_development` class oriented from earlier/paler toward later/more-yellow development. The same dependence clusters were used when comparing selected literature and candidate-free remeasurement.

## Literature observation matrix and partial identification

Published *Camellia* molecular studies were decomposed into biological systems and dependence clusters so that multiple papers or contrasts from the same evolutionary background did not count as independent evolutionary replicates. For each system we recorded whether A, F, C, and P were measured and, where supported, the direction of change after canonical orientation. Unknown axes remained unknown; visible colour was never used as a substitute molecular state.

We first quantified axis ascertainment. Across biological systems, published coverage was A/F/C/P = 8/4/1/3. We evaluated whether anthocyanin coverage exceeded an axis-symmetric observation process while conditioning on the exact number of axes measured in each system. The exact conditional enumeration included all assignments preserving per-system measurement counts. We also repeated the comparison after dependence collapse.

For recurrence, unresolved cluster×axis cells were enumerated over `{up, down, same}`. Two summary statistics were retained: exact-signature recurrence, which asks how many independent clusters share one complete four-axis signature, and pairwise axis concordance, the fraction of A/F/C/P directions matching across cluster pairs. Because unknown axes can produce multiple admissible values, results are reported as exact identified sets rather than single imputed estimates.

## Frozen candidate-free RNA-seq measurement

Public raw RNA-seq systems were admitted only if biological source and target conditions could be frozen independently of expression outcomes. All systems used annotation-informative *Camellia sinensis* RefSeq `GCF_004153795.1`. Transcript-family assignment was derived from reference annotation only, all annotation-matched paralogs were retained, and expression or flower colour was not used to choose paralogs. The reference resolved 129 transcripts across 14 of 19 preregistered families. Module completeness under the frozen reference was A=100%, F=100%, P=50%, and C=87.5%; modules below 50% completeness would have been unresolved rather than outcome-imputed.

Reads were obtained through an audited ENA HTTPS prefix route with integrity checks and verified full-file fallback where streaming failed. An independent public-SRA fallback was audited for transport failures. Within each admitted dataset, transcript TPM values were summed across all annotation-matched paralogs within gene family, transformed as log2(TPM+1), standardized to family z scores across admitted samples, and averaged within each module. Between-dataset expression values were never pooled directly.

For two-group contrasts, signed Hedges' g was calculated as target minus source. For *C. japonica* Joy Kendrick, the frozen contrast was pink versus red petal sectors within the same cultivar (3 vs 3; first 1000000 paired reads/run; Yu et al., 2023). For *C. reticulata* cultivar Manao, the frozen contrast was full-bloom white petal region versus red petal region at the same stage (3 vs 3; 1000000 paired reads/run; Qu et al., 2024); runs were resolved by `GSE236364 GSM -> GEO SRX -> SRR`, not accession order. For *C. sinensis*, all five matched white-versus-pink stages were retained (3 replicates per colour per stage; 30 runs total; Zhou et al., 2020). An axis was called stage-consistent only if at least four of five stages were estimable and at least 80% of estimable stage effects had the same sign. This rule was frozen before the stage effects were inspected.

For yellow development, *C. nitidissima* (Zhou et al., 2017) and *C. perpetua* (Zhu et al., 2024) each contributed S1–S5 × 3 developmental series. The cross-cluster estimator was matched in advance: ordinary least-squares slope across the five prespecified stage means. Exact two-sided permutation P values were obtained from all 5! stage-order permutations and retained as uncertainty metadata, not a direction filter. For *C. perpetua*, the separately preregistered S1→S3 yellow-onset contrast was retained as a within-system biological result but was not substituted for the matched five-stage estimator in the cross-cluster recurrence test.

## Candidate-selected versus candidate-free recurrence

Candidate-free A/F/C/P directions were converted to the same canonical dependence-cluster schema as the literature matrix. Five frozen successful raw-data workflows yielded 20 canonical cluster×axis measurements. We recomputed exact-signature recurrence and pairwise axis concordance using candidate-free directions only, then compared identified-set width with the selected-literature matrix on the identical common cluster set. Direct literature-versus-candidate-free agreement was calculated only for cells independently resolved in both regimes.

## Nuclear phylogenomic backbone and accepted taxonomy

We used public transcriptomic resources associated with genus-scale *Camellia* sampling to construct an independent nuclear sensitivity backbone rather than claiming a new genus phylogeny (Wu et al., 2022). Protein-coding sequences were screened against Angiosperms353 targets; 339 loci met the frozen ≥80% occupancy criterion. Loci were aligned independently and gene trees were inferred under two pipelines: FastTree with an LG+gamma protein model and IQ-TREE with LG+G4 and 1000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates. Gene trees were summarized with ASTRAL/ASTER methods.

Historical sample names were normalized against the pinned World Flora Online Plant List 2026-06 snapshot (World Flora Online Consortium, 2026). Ninety-three historical *Camellia* tips collapsed to 55 accepted species; multi-individual accepted groups were retained through the species-tree mapping rather than represented by arbitrary single tips. On 53 accepted species shared between the two nuclear pipelines, we compared nontrivial splits and Robinson–Foulds distance.

## Wild-colour audit and phylogenetic pattern

Accepted-species visible colour was audited against wild/floristic evidence. Cultivar colour, senescence colour, rare alternatives, and naturally polymorphic species were not forced into one strict state. This produced a strict wild seed of 24 species (A=1, W=19, Y=4) and a dominant-colour sensitivity of 30 species (A=4, W=22,Y=4).

We tested phylogenetic structure with unrooted topology edge counts. The primary statistic was mean distance from each labelled species to its nearest same-colour relative, compared with 100000 count-preserving permutations of A/W/Y labels among the same labelled tips. Global same-state mean pairwise distance was retained as a sensitivity because local clustering need not imply one globally compact colour clade. Analyses were repeated on both accepted-species nuclear topologies and under strict versus dominant-colour coding.

## Event-identifiability gate

Rooted three-state Mk models were used only as a sensitivity for event identity, not as the primary macro pattern test. A branch was eligible for downstream causal analysis only if the same directional endpoint contrast survived alternative branch-length/root treatments within a trait scenario and remained consistent between strict and dominant wild-colour seeds. If no branch survived this cross-scenario gate, branch-specific climate, pollination, or molecular enrichment was stopped rather than rescued with more complex models.

## Ecological context

Climate and pollination analyses were retained as contextual screens and are assigned to Supplement in v0.2. They test whether coarse visible A/W/Y behaves as a sufficient ecological state, not whether a specific transition branch was caused by climate or pollinators. The branch-causal interpretation is forbidden when the event-identifiability gate fails.

# RESULTS

## The published molecular observation regime was incomplete and nonuniform

The published mechanism matrix measured pigment axes unevenly. Across biological systems, A/F/C/P coverage was 8/4/1/3. Conditional on the exact number of axes measured per system, the exact probability of anthocyanin coverage at least as enriched as observed was 0.00836; the probability of any axis imbalance at least as extreme was 0.02395. After dependence collapse, the anthocyanin-enrichment probability weakened to 0.140625. Thus the literature clearly contains a nonuniform observation process at the biological-system level, but the amount attributable to repeated sampling of the same evolutionary backgrounds is non-negligible.

Because the literature matrix left many axes unmeasured, its apparent multivariate recurrence was only partially identified. After canonical orientation and separation of transition classes, the three anthocyanin-gain dependence clusters contained seven unresolved cluster×axis cells, whereas the two yellow-development clusters contained three.

## Standardized remeasurement removed complete anthocyanin whole-package recurrence

The candidate-free anthocyanin analysis included three independent dependence clusters: `CJAPONICA`, `CRETICULATA`, and `CSIN_WHITE_PINK` (Fig. 2A,B).

In Joy Kendrick *C. japonica*, the frozen pink→red 3×3 comparison gave A=-0.0168, F=+0.4287, C=-0.9379, and P=+1.9002 Hedges' g. The A effect is technically negative under the signed registry but effectively near zero; accordingly, we treat it as failure to reproduce the selected-literature A-up representation as a pathway-wide increase rather than evidence for a strong opposite anthocyanin response.

In *C. reticulata* Manao, the frozen full-bloom white-region→red-region 3×3 comparison gave A=+0.1175, F=+0.3233, C=-3.4590, and P=+0.3990. Thus the weak A-up direction agreed with the literature anchor, whereas P conflicted with the literature P-down direction. In *C. sinensis*, the five-stage white→pink series yielded stage-consistent A down (mean g=-2.8595), F down (-0.3679), and C up (+0.8820); P was unresolved under the frozen stage-consistency rule.

On these same three clusters, selected literature left exact-signature recurrence at 0.333–1.0 and pairwise A/F/C/P concordance at 0.25–1.0. Candidate-free remeasurement reduced exact-signature recurrence to **0.333 exactly** and pairwise concordance to **0.333–0.5**. Pairwise identified-set width contracted from 0.75 to 0.1667, a reduction of 0.5833. Among five cells resolved independently in both observation regimes, two agreed and three conflicted. Standardized measurement therefore did not simply narrow uncertainty around a strongly recurrent package; it removed complete three-cluster A/F/C/P recurrence from the admissible set.

## Yellow development retained modular recurrence but not one exact package

The yellow-development comparison used two independently frozen S1–S5 trajectories with the same five-stage slope estimator (Fig. 2A,C).

For *C. nitidissima*, the candidate-free directions were A down (slope -0.2406), F up (+0.1240), C up (+0.1567), and P down (-0.4503). For *C. perpetua*, all 15 runs passed the frozen workflow, with mapping min/mean/max 79.2054/81.4782/84.0166%. Its ordered trajectory was A down (-0.5817), F down (-0.1691), C up (+0.1792), and P down (-0.6156).

The two yellow systems therefore agreed on three of four axes—A down, C up, and P down—and differed only at F. This outcome fell exactly into the pre-specified “axis-specific reuse without exact whole-signature recurrence” branch of the frozen yellow gate.

Selected literature on these two clusters left exact-signature recurrence at 0.5–1.0 and pairwise concordance at 0.25–1.0. Candidate-free measurement point-identified exact-signature recurrence at **0.5** and pairwise concordance at **0.75**. The pairwise identified-set width fell from 0.75 to 0. Direct literature-versus-candidate-free comparison showed four agreements among five independently resolved cells; the only conflict was the *C. perpetua* F axis, literature up versus candidate-free down. Thus yellow development provides a control against the idea that candidate-free measurement mechanically reverses published directions: direct agreement was high, yet completion of previously unmeasured axes still rejected an invariant whole package.

## Candidate-free results distinguish whole-package from modular recurrence

The two transition classes converged on the same negative conclusion at the whole-package level but differed quantitatively in modular reuse. Complete A/F/C/P recurrence remained admissible under selected literature for both classes and was removed after standardized remeasurement. Anthocyanin-gain systems were comparatively heterogeneous, with candidate-free pairwise concordance limited to 0.333–0.5. Yellow-development systems retained 0.75 pairwise concordance because A, C, and P directions were shared.

The result is therefore not absence of recurrence. It is a scale-dependent form of recurrence: complete multivariate pathway packages were not repeated, whereas subsets of pathway directions could recur strongly within a transition class.

## Taxonomic and wild-colour auditing reduced apparent macro trait information

The historical nuclear sampling contained 93 named *Camellia* tips but only 55 accepted *Camellia* species after WFO normalization (Fig. 3A). Twenty accepted groups contained multiple legacy tips. Trait auditing caused a second contraction: 35 provisional single hard colour states became a strict wild-colour seed of 24 species (A=1, W=19, Y=4) and a dominant-colour sensitivity of 30 species (A=4, W=22, Y=4) (Fig. 3B). Eleven provisional hard labels were demoted under strict evidence requirements, often because natural within-species polymorphism had been compressed into one category.

## Local same-colour conservatism survived topology and trait sensitivity

The two accepted-species nuclear topologies were highly but not perfectly concordant. On 53 shared species, 46 of 50 nontrivial splits were shared and normalized RF distance was 0.08 (Fig. 4).

The robust macro pattern was local nearest-same-colour conservatism (Fig. 5). On the FastTree topology, nearest-same-colour distance was lower than the count-preserving null in the strict seed (P=0.00212) and dominant sensitivity (P<0.00001). The result reproduced on the stronger UFBoot topology: strict P=0.00116 and dominant P=0.000080. In contrast, broad same-colour mean pairwise-distance clustering failed topology sensitivity, and anthocyanin-like A clustering was not estimable under the strict wild-colour seed because A was a singleton.

## No accepted-species transition event survived wild-colour uncertainty

No branch passed the strong transition gate under the strict wild-colour seed. One W→A branch passed within the dominant-colour sensitivity, but no branch survived both trait scenarios. The strict×dominant shared robust event count was therefore zero (Fig. 6A).

This negative result defines an inferential boundary rather than evidence that colour transitions never occurred. Current public species-level hard states identify a robust local pattern but not which accepted-species branches can safely be treated as known transition events. We therefore did not perform branch-specific ecological or molecular causal enrichment using those hard-state events.

# DISCUSSION

## Repeated phenotype does not require a repeated whole mechanistic state

The central molecular result is not simply that different genes can produce similar flower colours. It is that the inferred repeatability of a multivariate pigment state changes when the observation regime is standardized on the same biological systems. Selected literature left complete A/F/C/P recurrence admissible for both red/pink gain and yellow development. Candidate-free remeasurement removed that possibility in both classes.

At the same time, the standardized data reject an all-or-none interpretation of recurrence. Yellow development retained shared A-down, C-up, and P-down directions across two independent developmental systems, yielding 0.75 pairwise concordance despite disagreement at F. Anthocyanin-gain systems were less concordant. Mechanistic repeatability in *Camellia* is therefore better described as modular and transition-class dependent than as recurrence of one complete pathway package.

This distinction clarifies how earlier sequence-level evidence should be interpreted. Same-lineage FLS recurrence and DFR paralog substitution remain informative demonstrations that molecular implementations differ in granularity. They no longer need to carry the paper's main recurrence claim. Instead, they provide mechanistic examples consistent with the broader result that phenotype-level repetition can be assembled from different combinations of reused and non-reused modules.

## The observation regime is part of the inferential object

The comparison is not a test of whether candidate-gene papers are “right” or “wrong.” Candidate-selected studies often ask targeted biological questions and can resolve specific causal nodes more precisely than a broad module score. The candidate-free protocol asks a different question: if the same systems are measured with one outcome-independent pathway-wide representation, what multivariate recurrence remains identifiable?

That distinction matters because unmeasured axes are not neutral. In the yellow literature matrix, most directly comparable published directions agreed with candidate-free directions, yet the missing axes still allowed complete whole-package recurrence. Once the missing state dimensions were measured, the F-axis disagreement between the two yellow systems was enough to remove exact whole-signature recurrence. The inferential change therefore arose not from systematic reversal of published effects but from observing dimensions that had previously been absent.

The same logic applies more broadly to evolutionary synthesis. A literature matrix is produced by a research process: candidate selection, assay availability, focal phenotypes, model organisms, and publication history. When that process is nonuniform, the identified evolutionary pattern is a function of both biology and observation. Explicitly modeling or standardizing the observation regime is therefore part of estimating recurrence, not merely a technical correction after the biological analysis.

## Molecular feasibility and macroevolutionary realization are different quantities

The standardized molecular result does not imply that visible flower colour should be evolutionarily labile. It shows that several pigment modules can change and that repeatability is partial rather than package-wide. The accepted-species nuclear analysis addresses a separate question: where realised visible states occur across evolutionary history.

Despite taxonomic collapse and strict wild-colour auditing, a local nearest-same-colour signal persisted on both independently inferred nuclear topologies. Closely related species were more likely than expected to have a nearer same-colour relative, although colours did not form globally compressed clades under the stronger topology. Thus modular molecular flexibility coexists with local historical structure in visible-state realization.

That combination is expected if feasibility, generation, persistence, and observation are separate filters. A molecular state can be readily generated but rarely persist; different molecular states can converge on similar visible outcomes; and inherited developmental or ecological backgrounds can favor persistence within local phylogenetic neighbourhoods. Our data do not identify which filter dominates in any individual lineage, but they reject the shortcut from molecular availability to macroevolutionary event rate.

## Pattern can be identifiable when individual events are not

The macro analysis provides a second identification result. Local visible-colour structure was robust, yet no individual accepted-species transition branch survived alternative treatment of naturally variable colour states. The data therefore support a pattern without identifying the historical events that generated it.

This is not a paradox. Aggregate structure can be estimable even when event assignments are unstable. The same principle appears at the molecular level: selected literature could suggest recurrent pathway mechanisms while leaving many individual A/F/C/P cells unobserved. In both cases, stronger modeling cannot replace absent information. Ancestral-state complexity cannot rescue branch identity when tip states are uncertain, just as a stronger recurrence statistic cannot determine unmeasured molecular axes without new observations.

This identification perspective provides a useful stop rule. We stop branch-specific causal inference when transition identity is not robust, and we stop whole-package recurrence claims when standardized molecular state rejects them. Negative gates therefore narrow the scientific question rather than merely reducing statistical power.

## Scope and limitations

The standardized RNA-seq analysis compares frozen transcript-state modules; it is not a direct assay of pigment concentrations, enzyme activity, cell-specific expression, or causal regulatory variants. A/F/C/P directions therefore define recurrence of this prespecified transcript-state representation. Candidate-selected studies and the standardized protocol answer related but non-identical questions, so disagreement shows that identified recurrence depends on observation scale rather than that either regime uniquely recovers the “true” mechanism.

All five systems were quantified against one annotation-informative *C. sinensis* RefSeq. Using one reference prevents outcome-dependent reference switching and produced high mapping rates in all admitted systems, but it can still miss species-specific paralogs or create differential mappability. Accordingly, our cross-system claims are at the gene-family/module level, not exact ortholog or paralog equivalence across species. Species-native genomes and metabolite, protein, or enzyme assays are important future sensitivities where comparable resources exist.

The raw workflows also use frozen read-prefix depths rather than full-depth differential-expression inference. Prefix depth and contrasts were fixed before outcomes, read integrity and fallback routes were audited, and module directions were not selected by significance. The present claims are therefore restricted to the reproducible fixed-depth observation regime. Full-depth and depth-sensitivity analyses remain useful robustness tests for precision and direction stability rather than hidden evidence that is being outcome-selected here.

## Ecology should enter as filtering and persistence, not as an assumed branch cause

The current ecological screens do not support a universal visible-colour syndrome and are deliberately secondary in v0.2. Coarse A/W/Y state does not uniquely encode ultraviolet reflectance, fluorescence, reward, morphology, flowering phenology, visitor effectiveness, or physiological pigment function. Pollination and abiotic selection remain plausible filters, but branch-specific causal assignment would require transition events that current public hard-state data do not identify (Trunschke et al., 2021; Berardi et al., 2026).

The appropriate next test is therefore population-resolved. Naturally polymorphic *Camellia* lineages can be used to measure calibrated spectra, pigment chemistry, paralog-specific expression, nectar and morphology, effective pollen transfer, fruit and seed set, and flowering-window environment in the same populations. Such data can test whether modular molecular states map to sensory and reproductive function and which states persist under changing ecological conditions.

## Implications for evolutionary repeatability

The results suggest a practical hierarchy for studying repeated evolution. First ask whether a phenotype is feasible. Second ask what the literature or assay system actually observes. Third distinguish recurrence of whole mechanistic states from recurrence of individual modules. Fourth ask which phenotypic states are realized across phylogeny. Finally, require robust event identity before attaching causal histories to branches.

Under this hierarchy, *Camellia* flower-colour evolution is neither fully deterministic nor unconstrained. The same complete molecular package is not repeatedly recovered, but some modules recur; the amount of reuse differs between transition classes; and realized visible states remain locally phylogenetically structured. Repeatability therefore exists at multiple, non-equivalent levels.

# CONCLUSIONS

Repeated flower-colour change in *Camellia* does not imply repetition of one complete A/F/C/P pigment-state package. A frozen pathway-wide remeasurement of five public RNA-seq systems removes complete multivariate recurrence from the admissible set for both anthocyanin gain and yellow development. Yet modular recurrence remains: the two yellow systems share A-down, C-up, and P-down directions while differing at F, and anthocyanin systems show a more heterogeneous combination of reused and non-reused axes.

At the macro scale, accepted-species wild flower colours retain topology-robust local phylogenetic structure, but individual transition branches are not robust to alternative treatment of naturally variable species. Together, these results separate mechanistic feasibility, observation regime, modular recurrence, macroevolutionary realization, and event-level causation. The next step is not a larger categorical colour matrix, but joint population-level measurement of molecular state, sensory phenotype, reproductive function, and environment in naturally variable lineages.
