## Title

**What repeats when evolution reaches similar flower colours? Molecular and historical resolution in *Camellia***

## Abstract

Repeated evolution is often summarized by whether a phenotype recurs, although recurrence may differ among molecular modules, realised phylogenetic patterns, and historical events. We asked what is repeatable in *Camellia* when molecular observation and historical identification are made more stringent. We represented public flower-colour RNA-seq systems on four prespecified transcript-state axes: anthocyanin (A), flavonol (F), carotenoid (C), and proanthocyanidin diversion (P). For three anthocyanin-gain dependence clusters and two yellow-development trajectories, we compared incomplete literature signatures with annotation-driven, outcome-independent remeasurement of the same five systems. Separately, we tested accepted-species wild-colour structure and transition branches across independent nuclear topologies and strict versus dominant colour coding. For anthocyanin gain, exact recurrence contracted from 0.333–1.0 to 0.333, and pairwise concordance from 0.333–1.0 to 0.333–0.5. For yellow development, exact recurrence contracted from 0.5–1.0 to 0.5 and pairwise concordance from 0.25–1.0 to 0.75; A, C, and P directions were shared while F differed. Local nearest-same-colour structure was robust, but no accepted-species transition branch survived both colour codings. Repeatability was therefore hierarchical: standardized observation retained modular reuse without one invariant molecular programme, while robust phylogenetic pattern did not supply robust individual event identity. The molecular contrasts are not independent evolutionary origins, and the molecular and macroevolutionary analyses are complementary rather than event-matched.

## Impact summary

When evolution repeatedly reaches a similar visible phenotype, biologists can ask whether the same genes, pathway modules, spatial pattern, or historical events recur. Those answers need not agree. We used public *Camellia* flower-colour data to make two comparisons under fixed rules. First, we held five biological systems constant and changed how their molecular states were observed. Standardized measurement narrowed the amount of whole-programme recurrence compatible with the literature, although selected pigment-pathway modules still changed in shared directions. Second, we held an accepted species set constant and asked first whether similar wild colours were locally clustered on phylogenies and then whether the individual branches on which colours changed could be identified robustly. The local pattern survived alternative trees and colour coding, but individual events did not. The result is not a new theory of convergence and does not show that molecular accessibility predicts macroevolutionary transitions. It is an empirical demonstration that phenotype recurrence, module reuse, realised phylogenetic structure, and historical event identity are separate inferential targets. Keeping those targets separate prevents incomplete molecular observations or uncertain ancestral histories from being converted into a stronger story than the data support.

2. **Prior-art boundary.** State that hierarchical convergence, alternate biochemical routes, genetic reuse, ascertainment effects, and ancestral-state uncertainty are established. Do not claim a new theory of state identity or many-to-one mapping.
3. **Empirical gap.** Identify the narrower test: the same admitted systems have rarely been compared under incomplete literature observation and one frozen multivariate pathway rule, with unresolved dimensions bounded rather than imputed.
4. **Cross-scale boundary.** Explain that a robust aggregate phylogenetic pattern and robust branch identity are distinct targets. Do not imply that RNA-seq contrasts are reconstructed evolutionary origins.
5. **Predictions and stops.** Predeclare contraction under standardized observation, possible modular rather than whole-programme reuse, topology/coding sensitivity, and a stop on branch ecology if event identity fails.

## Editor-facing novelty statement

The empirical increment is a frozen, same-system comparison of literature-conditioned and standardized multivariate molecular recurrence, paired with a separate design that permits a robust macroevolutionary pattern to remain positive while individual event identity fails. No priority is claimed for hierarchical convergence, ascertainment effects, partial identification, many-to-one mapping, or ancestral-state uncertainty.

Dear Editors,

We submit “What repeats when evolution reaches similar flower colours? Molecular and historical resolution in *Camellia*” for consideration as a Letter.

The manuscript asks a broad evolutionary question with two bounded empirical tests. In the molecular test, the biological systems are held fixed while incomplete literature descriptions and one annotation-driven, outcome-independent A/F/C/P observation rule are compared directly. The standardized rule narrows whole-programme recurrence while retaining transition-class-dependent module reuse. In the independent macroevolutionary test, local nearest-same-colour structure is robust across topology and coding sensitivities, but no accepted-species transition branch survives the strict-versus-dominant event gate.

The study shows, in one frozen public-data design, that repeated phenotype, repeated molecular programme, realised phylogenetic pattern, and identified historical event cannot be used as interchangeable evidence.
