# *Camellia perpetua* site–time bridge audit v0.1

## Why this audit was needed

The first micro–ecology overlap audit correctly found that the candidate-free molecular contrast and the ecology contrast are not the same samples or the same experimental event. A deeper provenance check, however, shows that *C. perpetua* is not merely a taxon-name overlap: the two public studies are unusually close in space and time.

The question is therefore:

> **How much of a matched public molecular–ecology system already exists for *C. perpetua*, and what exact observation is still missing?**

## Molecular dataset

Zhu et al. 2024 (`10.1007/s44281-024-00052-5`) sampled *C. perpetua* at the golden-camellia germplasm resource nursery of the Guangxi Institute of Botany:

- coordinates: **25°4′14″N, 110°17′58″E**;
- three **15-year-old** plants;
- clear morning in **June 2022**, 09:00–10:00;
- June explicitly described as peak bloom;
- five developmental stages S1–S5;
- 15 composite samples (3 plants × 5 stages);
- RNA-seq plus widely targeted metabolomics.

The candidate-free `CPERPETUA` A/F/C/P signature is derived from this developmental dataset. It should therefore be interpreted as a **summer/June developmental trajectory**, not as a season-invariant species molecular state.

## Ecology dataset

Jiang et al. 2025 (`10.1016/j.flora.2025.152727`) compared summer and winter nectar, visitors and reproductive success in two populations. The Guilin Botanical Garden (GBG) site is:

- coordinates: **25°4′12″N, 110°18′1″E**;
- observations in the **2021–2023** study period;
- adult introduced *C. perpetua*, approximately **15–20 years old**;
- explicit summer vs winter nectar, bird/bee visitation, fruit set and seed set comparison.

The second ecology population, PaiRu Village in the Chongzuo reserve, is spatially separate and has no candidate-free molecular sample.

## CHUN spatial/temporal calculation

Using the published coordinates, the molecular nursery and the GBG ecology coordinate are separated by approximately:

> **104 m**.

June 2022 falls inside the ecology study's 2021–2023 observation period and corresponds to the summer/peak-bloom side of the seasonal ecology contrast.

Thus *C. perpetua* passes:

- same taxon;
- near-same local site;
- overlapping calendar period;
- overlapping summer flowering season;
- similar plant age as contextual support.

It does **not** pass:

- demonstrated same individual trees;
- same flower samples;
- matched winter vs summer petal molecular/spectral measurement;
- event-matched molecular-state → pollinator → reproductive-fitness causation.

## Revised interpretation

The correct status is neither `independent unrelated studies` nor `matched causal experiment`.

It is:

> **a quasi-matched same-site/time public system with a missing winter floral molecular state.**

This makes *C. perpetua* the strongest immediate public-data bridge in `chun`.

The current candidate-free result, `A down / F down / C up / P down` across S1–S5, is a June developmental result. It must not be used as though the same A/F/C/P trajectory had been measured in winter. The ecology study shows that the pollination/reward regime changes strongly between seasons, but current public molecular data cannot tell whether the mature-flower latent pigment/spectral state also changes.

## New directly testable hypothesis

### H_CP_SEASONAL_LATENT_STATE

If flowering-window ecological filtering in *C. perpetua* acts partly through the floral biochemical/sensory phenotype, then mature flowers at the GBG site should show a reproducible winter–summer shift in one or more of:

- A/F/C/P expression modules;
- flavonoid/carotenoid metabolite composition;
- UV/visible reflectance or fluorescence;
- correlated nectar/reward traits.

The strongest test is not another S1–S5 developmental series. It is **matched mature flowers from winter and summer in the same GBG population**.

## Minimum experiment/data needed to close the chain

At the GBG population:

1. collect mature flowers in winter and summer;
2. quantify the same A/F/C/P modules used by `chun`, or a validated equivalent targeted expression panel;
3. quantify pigment chemistry and UV/visible reflectance;
4. measure nectar traits and effective bird/bee visitation in the same flowering windows;
5. retain fruit/seed outcome at the same plant/population scale.

This would test:

`season -> latent floral state + reward -> pollinator effectiveness -> reproductive success`

without requiring an unstable ancestral transition branch.

## Claim ceiling

The public studies are spatially and temporally close but do not document shared individual plants or shared samples. The current evidence therefore supports a **quasi-matched provenance bridge**, not a seasonal molecular effect or a causal colour-selection result.
