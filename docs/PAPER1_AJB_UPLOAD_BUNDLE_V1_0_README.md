## Biological headline

The paper does not rely on the generic claim that similar flower colours can evolve through different mechanisms, nor on a simple replay/non-replay dichotomy. Its strongest defensible result is hierarchical, transition-class-dependent repeatability across distinct inferential levels.

At the molecular level, the same public *Camellia* systems are first represented by the heterogeneous, partially observed A/F/C/P states available in the literature and then remeasured using annotation-driven, outcome-independent quantification within the four prespecified pigment modules (operational shorthand: `candidate-free`). These RNA-seq contrasts quantify colour-state-generating transcript changes within admitted systems. They are **not** treated as direct observations of independent macroevolutionary branch events.

For anthocyanin gain, literature-compatible exact recurrence spans 0.333–1.0 but standardized remeasurement fixes it at 0.333; pairwise concordance contracts from 0.333–1.0 to 0.333–0.5. Yellow development retains stronger modular recurrence, with shared A/C/P directions but a differing F axis.

At the macroevolutionary level, topology/coding-robust local colour structure is retained while no individual accepted-species transition branch survives strict-versus-dominant coding. The molecular and macro arms are therefore cross-scale complements, not event-for-event matches: colour-state generation, molecular module reuse, realised phylogenetic pattern, and historical event identity are separate evolutionary quantities.

## Claim ceiling

Do not claim priority for:

- same phenotype arising from different mechanisms;
- pathway-level or modular flower-colour convergence;
- candidate/discovery-method effects on apparent repeatability;
- partial identification as a general statistical idea;
- ancestral-state uncertainty or coding sensitivity;
- the first micro-to-macro *Camellia* flower-colour study.

Do not describe same-cultivar petal-sector or developmental RNA-seq contrasts as independent evolutionary origins. Do not infer branch-specific pollinator, climate, or other ecological causes while transition-event identity is not robust. White remains a visible evolutionary context, not a molecular zero or a definitive root-state claim.
