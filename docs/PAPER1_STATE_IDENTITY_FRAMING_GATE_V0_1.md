# Paper 1 state-identity framing gate v0.1

Checked: 2026-08-28 against frozen science v0.2.2, framing v0.3.4, the Figure 1 contract, and the 2026-08-27 novelty audit.

**Decision: wording-only pass; retain the current v0.3.4 manuscript and lock American Journal of Botany.**

## Question tested

> **When lineages repeatedly occupy similar visible flower-colour states, what remains equivalent as molecular observation and historical identification become stricter?**

This question links two real contractions in the frozen evidence:

1. the literature-permitted molecular recurrence set contracts when the same admitted public systems are remeasured with one annotation-driven, outcome-independent A/F/C/P rule; and
2. a topology/coding-robust local colour pattern remains detectable while the strict-by-dominant shared set of accepted-species transition events is empty.

The link is interpretive rather than a new joint estimator. The molecular comparison changes the observation rule within five public systems. The macroevolutionary comparison changes the robustness target from an unrooted local pattern to rooted branch-event identity across accepted-species trees and alternative colour codings. These layers do not form one latent state-resolution series: their units, evidence, and estimands differ.

The current accepted-species macro dataset cannot repair that mismatch. Of 53 nuclear-tree tips, five overlap direct taxon-level mechanistic evidence, three have immediately standardized raw-data coverage, and **zero have complete defensible A/F/C/P states**. Visible hue must not fill the missing molecular axes. Therefore Paper 1 cannot quantify how one joint molecular-visible state changes across the phylogeny, and it cannot present state identity itself as a newly established theory.

## Submission-only alternative artifact

The following is a deliberately stronger alternative used to test the gate. It is not the active manuscript.

### Alternative title

**When is the same flower colour the same evolutionary state? Resolution-dependent equivalence in *Camellia***

### Alternative running head

**Resolution-dependent flower-colour identity**

### Alternative structured abstract

#### Premise of the study

Repeatedly observed flower colours may be equivalent as visible phenotypes while differing in molecular implementation or historical origin. We asked what remains equivalent when molecular observation and macroevolutionary identification become stricter in *Camellia*.

#### Methods

We represented admitted public RNA-seq systems on four prespecified transcript-state axes: anthocyanin, flavonol, carotenoid, and proanthocyanidin diversion (A/F/C/P). We bounded recurrence under incomplete literature observation, remeasured five systems using annotation-driven, outcome-independent pathway quantification, and separately tested accepted-species wild-colour structure and branch events across nuclear topologies and alternative colour codings.

#### Key results

For three anthocyanin-gain dependence clusters, exact whole-signature recurrence contracted from 0.333–1.0 under literature observation to 0.333 after standardized remeasurement; pairwise concordance contracted from 0.333–1.0 to 0.333–0.5, with direct agreement in two of six comparable cells. Two yellow-development trajectories retained stronger modular recurrence: A, C, and P directions agreed, whereas F differed. Wild colours retained topology/coding-robust local phylogenetic structure, but the strict-by-dominant shared robust branch-event count was zero.

#### Conclusions

Visible similarity does not define one invariant molecular state or guarantee an identifiable historical event. What remains equivalent depends on transition class and inferential level: molecular modules may recur, realised colours may remain locally structured, and individual origins may still be unresolved. The molecular and macroevolutionary analyses are complementary resolution tests, not event-for-event matches.

### Alternative Figure 1 framing

**Panel A — Four non-interchangeable quantities.** Visible colour labels a phenotype; A/F/C/P measurements describe within-system transcript-state change; accepted-species topology/coding tests describe realised phylogenetic pattern; rooted branch reconstruction proposes historical event identity. Arrows mark changes in inference, not a demonstrated causal chain.

**Panel B — Equivalence contracts under stricter observation.** On the molecular side, incomplete literature states permit wider recurrence bounds than standardized observation of the same systems. On the macro side, local same-colour structure survives topology/coding sensitivity, whereas individual accepted-species events do not survive the stricter shared-event gate. The two contractions share a logic of resolution but not a common unit or estimator.

Suggested legend sentence:

> Similar visible colours can remain equivalent at one inferential level and separate at another; Figure 1 distinguishes observation of molecular generation, realised phylogenetic pattern, and historical event identity without treating within-system RNA-seq contrasts as macroevolutionary origins.

### Alternative Introduction opening

Evolutionary repetition depends on what is required to count two outcomes as the same. Similar visible flower colours may share a pigment module, differ elsewhere in the molecular programme, occupy comparable positions in a phylogeny, or derive from historical events that cannot be identified robustly from present data. These are distinct equivalence claims. Treating them as one converts a tractable question about repeatability into an unsupported assertion that visible similarity, molecular state, and origin are interchangeable.

### Alternative Introduction closing

We therefore ask what remains equivalent as resolution increases at two inferential boundaries. First, we compare incomplete literature descriptions with standardized, outcome-independent A/F/C/P remeasurement of the same admitted public systems. Second, we compare topology/coding-robust local colour structure with the stricter identity of individual accepted-species transition branches. This is a matched inferential audit across scales, not an event-for-event alignment of RNA-seq contrasts and reconstructed macroevolutionary origins.

### Alternative Discussion opening

The answer is hierarchical rather than binary. Standardized observation narrows the molecular signatures that can be called recurrent, but does not erase modular reuse; yellow development retains more shared direction than anthocyanin gain. At the macroevolutionary level, realised visible colours remain locally structured while individual transition-event identity fails the strict-by-dominant robustness gate. Similar colour therefore remains equivalent in some modules and patterns without becoming one invariant molecular state or one securely identified historical event.

### Alternative Discussion closing

Resolution-dependent equivalence is thus the synthesis, not a new latent-state estimate. Paper 1 shows where equivalence survives under the observations currently available and where it stops. Testing whether molecular accessibility predicts macroevolutionary transition probability will require taxon-resolved A/F/C/P states and robust event histories that the present public dataset does not contain.

## Adopted wording and frozen boundary

Adopt this sentence in submission strategy and the v1.0 cover letter:

> **When similar visible flower colours are compared, what remains equivalent as molecular observation and historical identification become stricter?**

Retain the active title **“Hierarchical molecular repeatability coexists with local flower-colour conservatism in *Camellia*.”** Retain all v0.3.4 Introduction, Methods, Results, figures, estimates, and Discussion claims. Do not present the alternative abstract or Figure 1 wording as a new analysis.

## Journal verdict

The alternative fails the predeclared condition for reconsidering *Evolution*: it does not yield a genuinely new quantified state-resolution result from admissible evidence. It passes only as a concise submission-facing question. Paper 1 is therefore locked to the **American Journal of Botany v1.0 route**, and Issue #85 becomes the next execution objective.
