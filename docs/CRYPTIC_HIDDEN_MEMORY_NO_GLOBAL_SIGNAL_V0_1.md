# Cryptic hidden memory in globally no-signal radiations — v0.1

## Frozen question

Can fine flower-colour history remain phylogenetically organized inside a coarse state even when the ordinary unconditional profile classifies the radiation as having no detectable phylogenetic flower-colour signal?

The focal subgroup and primary exact sign test were frozen before this subgroup calculation.

## Result

Among the 21 radiations with genuine fine-to-coarse opportunity, **10** had the frozen terminal class:

`PROFILE_NO_PHYLOGENETIC_SIGNAL`

Within those 10:

- positive hidden-memory effect: **8/10**
- negative: **2/10**
- median centered effect: **+0.02754 AUC**
- mean centered effect: **+0.02477 AUC**
- exact one-sided sign test: **P = 0.05469**

The frozen primary criterion required a positive median **and** sign P <= 0.05.

Therefore:

**PRIMARY_EXACT_SIGN_TEST_NOT_SUPPORTED**

The one-sided Wilcoxon test is P = **0.00977**, but it was predeclared secondary and cannot rescue the primary result.

## What is still interesting

The descriptive pattern is strong:

- 8/10 globally no-signal clades point positive;
- the no-signal subgroup median hidden effect (**+0.02754**) is not smaller than the descriptive median among the 11 globally signalled clades (**+0.01727**);
- only *Pouteria* and *Rhus* have negative hidden effects in the no-signal subgroup.

No between-group winner test was opened.

So an unconditional “no phylogenetic signal” label does **not** mean that every nested component of evolutionary history is absent. Positive hidden organization can remain after conditioning on the coarse phenotype.

But because the frozen exact subgroup test missed alpha = 0.05 by a small margin, the paper should not state that cryptic memory is inferentially established specifically in the no-signal subgroup.

## Evolutionary interpretation

This sharpens the distinction between two questions:

1. **Global signal:** does phenotype identity, considered across all pairs and states, show detectable phylogenetic organization?
2. **Hidden hierarchy:** after broad phenotype membership is held fixed, do finer states retain additional lineage organization?

Those questions can yield different answers in the same radiation.

That is consistent with the hierarchical-memory architecture, but the no-global-signal subgroup evidence remains descriptive under the frozen rule.
