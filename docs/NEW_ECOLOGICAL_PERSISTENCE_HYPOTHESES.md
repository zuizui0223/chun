# New ecological hypotheses generated after the direct-climate falsification

## Current empirical starting point

These hypotheses are generated from **chun results**, not copied from literature residual questions.

1. Anthocyanin-like visible A is strongly concentrated in historical backgrounds, even after fixing the number of A taxa.
2. A universal direct `cold -> A` explanation is not supported: the apparent BIO6 signal disappears after historical blocking, and colour divergence does not align with greater within-section climate divergence.
3. White-containing mechanistic systems do not require demonstrated structural pathway loss, and reversible white/coloured switches occur in independent systems.
4. Human-visible hue is a lossy ecological label: the same coarse visible state can contain different pollinator-function strategies.

The new ecological problem is therefore:

> **What ecological process filters readily reversible pigment-network states so that only some states persist in particular lineages?**

## H6 — Pollinator-reliability filtering

### Hypothesis

Selection acts less on annual mean climate than on the **reliability of reproductive service during the flowering window**. Climate and weather alter which pollinator guild is available and effective; floral sensory states are retained when they stabilize reproduction under that temporally variable service.

This differs from the rejected direct-cold model:

`annual cold -> anthocyanin`

and instead predicts:

`flowering-window climate/weather -> pollinator reliability -> floral sensory phenotype -> persistence`.

### Predictions

1. Colour/sensory transitions correlate more strongly with flowering-season variability (temperature/precipitation seasonality, cloudy/rainy periods, pollinator activity windows) than with annual BIO1/BIO6 means.
2. Shifts toward bird or mixed pollination precede or coincide with floral sensory changes where insect service becomes seasonally unreliable.
3. Species with strongly seasonal pollinator weighting should show either sensory-state plasticity or stronger divergence in UV/visible/reward traits than expected from visible hue alone.
4. After pollinator-reliability variables are added, residual direct climate -> visible-colour effects should shrink.

### Falsification

Reject this as a general explanation if flowering-window reliability has no greater explanatory value than annual climate after phylogeny, phenology, and geography are controlled.

## H7 — Ecological preadaptation versus genetic lineage permissivity

### Hypothesis

The strong lineage concentration of A may not be caused only by pigment-network genetics. Certain lineages may first evolve an ecological package — flowering season, flower size/shape, nectar reward, longevity, or accessibility to birds/large insects — that makes a pigment-state shift selectively profitable.

Conceptually:

`floral ecological architecture -> pollinator opportunity -> pigment-state payoff`

rather than necessarily:

`pigment-network permissivity -> colour transition`.

### Predictions

1. Flowering phenology, floral architecture, nectar/reward, or pollinator regime should change before or synchronously with recurrent A gains.
2. Once these ecological preadaptation traits are controlled, the unexplained clade/lineage concentration of A should weaken if ecological predisposition is the main cause.
3. If the lineage effect remains strong after those controls, the alternative **genetic permissivity** explanation gains support.

### Why this matters

H7 is a required competing explanation for H4. The present section-concentration result alone cannot distinguish a permissive genome from a lineage that simply inherited the right ecological syndrome.

## H8 — Latent sensory-state filtering

### Hypothesis

Natural selection does not primarily see the categories A/W/Y. It acts on a multidimensional reproductive signal composed of pigment chemistry, UV reflectance/absorption, fluorescence, morphology, nectar/reward, and phenology.

Visible colour is therefore a projection of a higher-dimensional **latent sensory phenotype**.

### Predictions

1. Pollinator function is predicted better by latent sensory traits than by A/W/Y hue.
2. The same visible state can be maintained under different pollinator regimes by modifying UV, fluorescence, morphology, or reward instead of changing human-visible hue.
3. Some pollinator-regime transitions will occur without visible-colour transition; they should instead coincide with changes on these hidden sensory axes.
4. Macro colour-transition counts will underestimate the number of ecologically important floral-signal transitions.

### Existing diagnostic

The current seven-taxon pollination ledger already contains multiple visible states with heterogeneous pollinator-function classes. This is a plausibility result, not yet a genus-wide effect estimate.

## H9 — Ecological hysteresis of retained pigment pathways

### Hypothesis

Because pigment machinery is often retained rather than deleted, floral states may behave as **history-dependent ecological states**. A lineage can enter W without losing the molecular capacity for A/Y, and later return when the ecological payoff changes. The probability and timing of reversal therefore depend on both present ecology and previous state.

### Predictions

1. W->A/Y and A/Y->W reversals should occur without requiring repeated structural-gene loss and regain.
2. Transition rates should be context dependent rather than homogeneous across the tree.
3. Ecological changes may precede pigment changes by a lag, producing hysteresis; identical contemporary environments need not imply identical floral states if histories differ.
4. Reversal-rich clades should preferentially retain multiple paralogs/regulatory routes, connecting the micro paralog-substitution result to macro ecological persistence.

### Falsification

This hypothesis weakens if apparent reacquisitions require repeated de-novo restoration of lost pathway structure or if transition rates are adequately history independent.

## H10 — Flowering-window selection rather than annual-climate selection

### Hypothesis

The relevant abiotic environment is the climate experienced **while flowers and pollinators interact**, not the species-wide annual climatic centroid.

This is narrower than H6: H10 concerns the time window of selection, while H6 concerns the biological mediator (pollinator reliability).

### Predictions

1. Monthly/flowering-season temperature and precipitation extremes, BIO4/BIO15-like seasonality, and short-term weather explain persistence better than annual BIO1/BIO6.
2. Species with similar annual climates but different flowering seasons can experience different reproductive selection and therefore different floral signals.
3. The apparent failure of annual direct-climate models can coexist with strong flowering-season ecological selection.

## Discriminating model set after the rooted nuclear tree

The decisive analysis should compare, on the same branches:

- **M0 historical sorting:** lineage/geography only;
- **M1 direct abiotic:** climate -> pigment;
- **M2 reliability mediated:** flowering-window climate -> pollinator reliability -> sensory state;
- **M3 ecological preadaptation:** phenology/morphology/reward -> pollinator -> pigment;
- **M4 genetic permissivity:** molecular accessibility/retained paralog structure interacts with ecology;
- **M5 hysteresis:** M2/M3/M4 plus previous pigment state and transition lag.

The central question has therefore sharpened from “why are some flowers red?” to:

> **Why do some molecularly accessible floral states cross the ecological persistence threshold in particular lineages, while equally accessible alternatives disappear or remain latent?**
