Please consider our Research Article, **“Hierarchical molecular repeatability coexists with local flower-colour conservatism in *Camellia*,”** for publication in the *American Journal of Botany*.

### What question is addressed?

When similar visible flower colours are compared, what remains equivalent as molecular observation and historical identification become stricter? We address this question at two explicitly separate inferential levels. At the molecular level, we represent admitted public RNA-seq systems on four prespecified transcript-state axes—anthocyanin, flavonol, carotenoid, and proanthocyanidin diversion (A/F/C/P)—first bounding recurrence in the incompletely observed literature and then remeasuring five auditable systems with one annotation-driven, outcome-independent protocol. At the macroevolutionary level, we test accepted-species wild-colour structure across independent nuclear topologies and alternative wild-colour codings, then impose a stricter robustness gate before identifying historical branches.

These analyses are matched tests of inference across scales, not an event-for-event match. Developmental and petal-sector RNA-seq contrasts describe colour-state-generating transcript changes within admitted systems; they are not treated as independent macroevolutionary origins.

### What is the major contribution?

The result is hierarchical rather than all-or-none. For three anthocyanin-gain dependence clusters, standardized remeasurement fixes exact whole-signature recurrence at one third and limits pairwise A/F/C/P concordance to 0.333–0.5; direct literature/standardized agreement remains two of six comparable cells. Two yellow-development trajectories retain stronger modular repeatability: A, C, and P directions agree while F differs.

The macroevolutionary analysis establishes a complementary boundary. Wild flower colours retain topology/coding-robust local phylogenetic structure, but no accepted-species transition branch is shared as robust between strict and dominant colour codings. The paper therefore distinguishes molecular module reuse, realised phylogenetic pattern, and historical event identity instead of assuming that visible similarity defines one invariant molecular programme or one securely identifiable origin.

### Why is this suitable for AJB?

The manuscript connects molecular, developmental, and macroevolutionary evidence around a general botanical question while keeping each evidential level explicit. *Camellia* provides repeated visible states, auditable public molecular contrasts, and independent phylogenomic resources within one genus. The resulting framework should interest readers working on flower-colour evolution, convergent evolution, evolutionary predictability, molecular evolution, and comparative phylogenetics.

The manuscript does **not** claim that same-phenotype/different-mechanism outcomes, pathway convergence, measurement dependence, or ancestral-state uncertainty are themselves new concepts. Its contribution is the same-system empirical audit of how much multivariate molecular replay survives standardized observation, coupled to a separate topology/coding robustness test that distinguishes macroevolutionary pattern from event identity.
