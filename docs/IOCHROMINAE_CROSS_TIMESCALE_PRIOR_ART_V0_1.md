# Iochrominae cross-timescale prior-art boundary — v0.1

## Why this matters

The post-Paper-1 programme asks how present flower-colour variation relates to repeated evolutionary transitions through time. Iochrominae is not merely a convenient coloured-ancestor control; it already contains direct prior art on **within-population colour morphs versus fixed species-level colour transitions**. Any cross-clade novelty claim must therefore be written around, not through, this literature.

## Prior art that is already established

### 1. Macroevolutionary transition architecture
Smith & Goldberg 2015 (`10.3732/ajb.1500163`; Dryad `10.5061/dryad.0732g`) estimated gain/loss rates for anthocyanin pigmentation across four clades including Iochrominae. Flower colour had already been treated explicitly as an evolutionary-time transition process before the current programme.

### 2. Four independent fixed losses converge at pathway-expression level
Larter et al. 2018 (`10.1093/molbev/msy117`) profiled anthocyanidins across 28 Iochrominae species and expression of seven anthocyanin-pathway genes. Four independent losses of ancestral delphinidin pigmentation were associated with strong coordinated reduction of F3'5'H, DFR and ANS expression. This is explicit pathway-level regulatory convergence across independent macroevolutionary colour losses.

### 3. Present white morphs are not developmental replicas of fixed white species
Larter et al. 2019 (`10.1002/dvdy.82`; Dryad `10.5061/dryad.p5dq84v`) directly compared white morphs within normally pigmented species with independently white-flowered species. Both lacked colourful anthocyanins, but white morphs retained expression profiles more similar to pigmented species, whereas fixed white species showed stronger downstream pathway reduction.

The paper explicitly frames the possibility that many mechanisms can generate segregating white morphs while a subset is preferentially fixed over evolutionary time.

### 4. A concrete within-population mutation can differ from fixed macro routes
Coburn et al. 2015 (`10.3732/ajb.1400395`) identified an 11-amino-acid deletion in DFR causing a naturally occurring white morph of *Iochroma calycinum*, with no corresponding broad pathway-expression downregulation. This supplies a mechanistic example of the within-population side of the contrast.

## Claims that are therefore NOT novel

Do not claim novelty for:

- placing flower colour on an evolutionary time axis;
- asking whether within-population morphs predict fixed species differences;
- the general preferential-fixation/filtering hypothesis that many mutations generate a phenotype but only some mechanisms fix through time;
- pathway-level convergence among repeated fixed pigmentation losses;
- the statement that present white morphs need not use the same developmental mechanism as fixed white species.

## Retained programme-level opportunity

The cross-clade atlas can still make a distinct contribution if it moves beyond the single-clade Iochrominae design in four ways.

### A. Common observation intervention across clades
Re-express the same admitted public systems under one outcome-independent, functional-module ontology, instead of comparing literature-defined candidate states across unrelated studies.

### B. Multiple ancestral baselines
Compare white-like, coloured and organ-mixed ancestral states rather than conditioning the study on one direction of colour loss. Camellia, Epimedium, Iochrominae, Linoideae and Hydrangea provide different baseline/transition architectures.

### C. Generation/accessibility versus long-term realization
Quantify, under a common rule, whether molecularly accessible state diversity exceeds the diversity of repeatedly realized phylogenetic states. This is broader than asking whether one white morph becomes one white species.

### D. Organ- and chemistry-aware state ontology
Keep display organ and pigment chemistry explicit. A white inner sepal, yellow ancestral petal, anthocyanin-free corolla and carotenoid/aurone yellow state are not silently collapsed into one scalar colour variable.

## Role of Iochrominae in the atlas

Iochrominae is promoted to a **mandatory benchmark / hostile prior-art control**:

1. Can the atlas reproduce the known fixed-loss regulatory convergence when using the common functional-module representation?
2. Can it reproduce the known non-convergence between white morphs and fixed white species without outcome-tuning?
3. Does the common observation rule change the apparent amount of whole-state versus module-level repeatability?
4. Do conclusions from white-loss Iochrominae generalize to colour gains, reversals and mixed ancestral-organ states in other clades?

A method that cannot recover these well-established qualitative Iochrominae results should not be trusted for pooled cross-clade inference.

## Current programme novelty sentence

> The programme does not introduce the idea that flower-colour polymorphisms and fixed species differences can use different mechanisms. It tests whether the relationship between molecular accessibility, repeated module use and long-term realization follows a common architecture across independently evolving floral radiations when those radiations are remeasured under the same organ-aware, outcome-independent state definition.
